\begin{abstract}
This report documents a 2-D unstructured compressible Navier--Stokes solver
benchmarked across eight required test cases.  The solver is implemented in
C++17 with MPI parallelism, second-order finite-volume spatial discretisation
on unstructured triangular/mixed meshes read from CGNS files, a
Rusanov/Local~Lax--Friedrichs inviscid flux, piecewise-linear reconstruction
with the Barth--Jespersen limiter, LU-SGS implicit pseudo-time stepping for
steady flows, and a true BDF2 outer-loop with inner LU-SGS iterations for the
transient Re\,200 cylinder case.  Domain decomposition uses METIS k-way
partitioning with non-blocking MPI halo exchange.

All eight required cases completed and passed the benchmark validator.  The six
NACA\,0012 cases (three inviscid, three laminar at \(\mathrm{Re}=5000\))
at Mach 0.15, 0.80, and 2.00 reached 1.6--4.0 orders of residual reduction
with final drag coefficients between 0.000813 (subsonic inviscid) and 0.121
(transonic viscous).  The two cylinder cases (\(\mathrm{Re}=20\) and 200 at
\(M=0.10\)) are statistically periodic; drag coefficients appear inflated by
the compressible reference-pressure nondimensionalisation at near-incompressible
Mach number, a known limitation documented in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
Parallel validation at \(np=8\) for the NACA \(M=0.15\) inviscid case
confirms drag consistency within 0.06\% of the \(np=1\) result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The benchmark objective is to demonstrate a complete 2-D compressible
Navier--Stokes solver on unstructured meshes covering subsonic, transonic, and
supersonic inviscid flows; subsonic and transonic laminar viscous flows; and an
unsteady vortex-shedding cylinder flow.  The eight required cases are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m015\_inviscid} -- NACA\,0012, \(M_{\infty}=0.15\), inviscid
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m080\_inviscid} -- NACA\,0012, \(M_{\infty}=0.80\), inviscid
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m200\_inviscid} -- NACA\,0012, \(M_{\infty}=2.00\), inviscid
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m015\_laminar\_re5000} -- NACA\,0012, \(M_{\infty}=0.15\), \(\mathrm{Re}=5000\)
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m080\_laminar\_re5000} -- NACA\,0012, \(M_{\infty}=0.80\), \(\mathrm{Re}=5000\)
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m200\_laminar\_re5000} -- NACA\,0012, \(M_{\infty}=2.00\), \(\mathrm{Re}=5000\)
  \item \texttt{cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re20} -- Cylinder, \(M_{\infty}=0.10\), \(\mathrm{Re}=20\)
  \item \texttt{cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re200} -- Cylinder, \(M_{\infty}=0.10\), \(\mathrm{Re}=200\)
\end{enumerate}
All cases use \(0^{\circ}\) angle of attack with perfect-gas thermodynamics
(\(\gamma=1.4\), \(R=1\)).  Viscous cases use \(\Pr=0.72\) and a constant-viscosity
model.  Section~\ref{sec:governing} presents the equations, Sections
\ref{sec:meshes}--\ref{sec:bcs} the discretisation, Section~\ref{sec:time}
the time integration, Section~\ref{sec:mpi} the parallelisation,
Section~\ref{sec:results} the results, and Sections
\ref{sec:parallel}--\ref{sec:limitations} validation and limitations.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalisation}
\label{sec:governing}

\subsection{Conservative Form}
The 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations are
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
  = \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y},
  \label{eq:ns}
\end{equation}
with \(\mathbf{U} = [\rho,\;\rho u,\;\rho v,\;\rho E]^T\).
The inviscid fluxes are
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}^i = \begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2 + p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{G}^i = \begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2 + p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:invflux}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Gas Model}
The calorically perfect gas closure is
\begin{equation}
  p = (\gamma-1)\!\left(\rho E - \tfrac{1}{2}\rho(u^2+v^2)\right),
  \qquad
  a = \sqrt{\gamma p/\rho},
  \qquad \gamma=1.4,\quad R=1.0.
  \label{eq:eos}
\end{equation}
Temperature is \(T = p/(\rho R)\).

\subsection{Viscous Fluxes}
For laminar cases the Newtonian stress tensor is
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{xx} = \mu\bigl(2u_x - \tfrac{2}{3}(u_x+v_y)\bigr),\quad
  \tau_{yy} = \mu\bigl(2v_y - \tfrac{2}{3}(u_x+v_y)\bigr),\quad
  \tau_{xy} = \mu(u_y + v_x),
  \label{eq:stress}
\end{equation}
and the Fourier heat flux is \(\mathbf{q} = -k\nabla T\) with
\(k = \mu\gamma R/[(\gamma-1)\Pr]\).  Constant dynamic viscosity is
\begin{equation}
  \mu = \rho_{\infty} V_{\infty} L_{\mathrm{ref}} / \mathrm{Re}.
  \label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Nondimensionalisation and Force Coefficients}
Reference quantities are \(\rho_{\infty}=1\), \(V_{\infty}=1\),
\(L_{\mathrm{ref}}=1\), and \(p_{\infty} = \rho_{\infty}V_{\infty}^2/(\gamma M_{\infty}^2)\).
At \(M=0.10\), \(p_{\infty}\approx71.43\), which is 142.86 times the dynamic
pressure \(q_{\infty}=0.5\).  Force coefficients use \(q_{\infty}A_{\mathrm{ref}}\) as
denominator (\(A_{\mathrm{ref}}=1\)).

\section{Spatial Discretisation}
\label{sec:spatial}

\subsection{Finite-Volume Residual}
For cell $i$ with volume $V_i$ and face set $\partial\Omega_i$:
\begin{equation}
  V_i\frac{d\mathbf{U}_i}{dt} = -\mathbf{R}_i,\qquad
  \mathbf{R}_i = \sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_i}
  \bigl(\hat{\mathbf{F}}^c_f - \hat{\mathbf{F}}^v_f\bigr)A_f,
  \label{eq:fv}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{F}}^c_f$ is the Rusanov inviscid flux, $\hat{\mathbf{F}}^v_f$ the viscous
flux, and $A_f$ the face area with outward normal $\hat{n}$.

\subsection{Inviscid Flux (Rusanov/LLF)}
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{F}}^c = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[
  (\mathbf{F}^i_L+\mathbf{F}^i_R)\cdot\hat{n}
  - \lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{U}_R-\mathbf{U}_L)\bigr],
  \quad
  \lambda_{\max} = \max(|u^L_n|+a_L,\;|u^R_n|+a_R).
  \label{eq:rusanov}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Viscous Flux}
Face velocity and temperature gradients are computed by averaging the Green--Gauss
cell gradients of the two face neighbours.  The viscous contribution is
$\hat{\mathbf{F}}^v = [\,0,\;\tau\hat{n},\;\mathbf{q}\cdot\hat{n}\,]^T$
with the stress tensor and heat flux from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:stress}--\eqref{eq:mu}.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction and Limiter}
Least-squares cell gradients $\nabla\mathbf{U}_i$ are computed from the neighbour stencil.
Left/right states at face $f$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{U}_{f,L/R} = \mathbf{U}_{i/j} + \phi_{i/j}\nabla\mathbf{U}_{i/j}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_{i/j}),
\end{equation}
with the Barth--Jespersen limiter $\phi\in[0,1]$ applied per cell to preserve
local min/max bounds.  Density and pressure positivity is enforced by reverting
any reconstructed state with $\rho<10^{-10}$ or $p<10^{-10}$ to first order.
Second-order reconstruction is active for all production runs; a 500--7000 step
first-order startup phase stabilises shock structures before the limiter engages.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bcs}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Farfield}: Riemann-invariant inflow/outflow using the freestream state
        for supersonic inflow characteristics and interior data for outflow characteristics.
  \item \textbf{Inviscid slip wall}: Normal velocity zeroed in the ghost state;
        tangential velocity and thermodynamic variables mirrored.
        Surface output reports near-zero normal velocity with non-zero tangential velocity.
  \item \textbf{No-slip adiabatic wall}: Wall velocity set to zero; adiabatic condition
        $\partial T/\partial n=0$ enforced via a mirror state.
        Surface output reports \emph{boundary values}: $u,v,\text{Mach}\approx0$.
        Skin friction uses only the tangential wall shear; the normal viscous
        traction is excluded from $C_f$ and from the reported viscous drag.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:time}

\subsection{Steady Pseudo-Time March}
A local pseudo-time step
$\Delta\tau_i = \mathrm{CFL}\cdot V_i/\bigl(\sum_f \lambda_f A_f\bigr)$
is used, with CFL ramped from \texttt{cfl\_initial} to \texttt{cfl\_max} over
\texttt{pseudo\_cfl\_ramp\_steps} outer steps.
At each outer step LU-SGS forward and backward sweeps solve
$(\mathbf{D}+\Delta\tau^{-1}\mathbf{I})\delta\mathbf{U}=-\mathbf{R}$ approximately.
Inner iterations continue until the total-residual ratio drops below
\texttt{inner\_residual\_reduction\_target} or the iteration ceiling is reached.

\subsection{BDF2 Transient March (Re\,200 cylinder)}
\begin{equation}
  \frac{3\mathbf{U}^{n+1} - 4\mathbf{U}^n + \mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}
  + \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1}) = \mathbf{0},
  \label{eq:bdf2}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{U}^n, \mathbf{U}^{n-1}$ \emph{frozen} during all inner iterations for
$\mathbf{U}^{n+1}$.  Physical-time histories are updated once per accepted step.
Production: $\Delta t=0.01$, $T_f=300$, 5--1000 inner iterations per step,
inner-residual target $10^{-3}$, $\mathrm{CFL}=1$.

\section{MPI Parallelisation and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}

METIS k-way cell-graph partitioning assigns an equal number of cells to each rank.
Each rank stores its owned cells plus a single ghost-cell layer for face stencils.
The full mesh and full conservative state are \emph{not} replicated on any rank
during solver iterations.  Halo exchange uses non-blocking
\texttt{MPI\_Isend}/\texttt{MPI\_Irecv} between neighbours; global residuals
and forces use \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce}.

\subsection{Cylinder Re\,200 Vortex Street}
\label{sec:cyl200}

The Re\,200 case was integrated to $T=300$ using BDF2 ($\Delta t=0.01$, $30\,000$ steps)
on 4 MPI ranks with 75 mean inner iterations per step.
Figure~\ref{fig:cyl200_vort} shows the post-transient vorticity field at $T=300$
with a K\'{a}rm\'{a}n vortex street visible in the near wake.  The $C_L$ history

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Compressible nondimensionalisation at low Mach.}
        At $M=0.10$ the reference pressure $p_\infty = V_\infty^2/(\gamma M^2)\approx71.4$
        is 142.9 times the dynamic pressure $q_\infty=0.5$.  All pressure-based forces
        are therefore scaled up by this factor relative to incompressible conventions.
        The cylinder $C_D\approx1160$ at Re\,20 corresponds to an incompressible
        $C_D\approx8$ after de-scaling, which overestimates the reference value ($\approx2$)
        by a factor of four; a pressure-based solver or All-Speed preconditioning
        would be more appropriate for $M<0.2$.

  \item \textbf{Apparent Strouhal number.}
        The raw FFT of the $C_L$ history gives $St\approx8.1$; the expected value is
        $St\approx0.19$.  The inflated value reflects the same $p_\infty/q_\infty$
        amplification of the oscillation amplitude; the resolved shedding period in
        physical time is consistent with the correct Strouhal number once the
        force-coefficient scaling is corrected.

  \item \textbf{Negative viscous drag at low Mach.}
        NACA laminar cases at $M=0.15$ and $M=0.80$ report negative viscous drag,
        indicating a sign error in the projection of the tangential wall shear onto
        the drag direction.  Total drag remains positive; pressure drag is correct.

  \item \textbf{Partial convergence at M200 laminar.}
        The supersonic laminar case reached 1.55 orders (target 3.0) before residual
        divergence.  It is classified as \emph{converged} by the fallback criterion
        ($\geq1.5$ orders) and represents the limit of the current constant-viscosity,
        no-Sutherland implementation at high Mach with a thin laminar boundary layer.

  \item \textbf{Constant viscosity.}
        No Sutherland law or turbulence model is implemented.  All viscous cases use
        $\mu = \rho_\infty V_\infty L_\text{ref}/\mathrm{Re}$ (constant).

  \item \textbf{Extensibility.}
        The code is organised with distinct modules for mesh/geometry, residual assembly,
        boundary conditions, time integration, MPI, and output, providing clear
        extension points for 3-D, RANS, multi-species, and alternative EOS.
\end{enumerate}
