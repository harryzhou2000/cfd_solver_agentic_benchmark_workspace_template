\begin{abstract}
This report documents the \texttt{cfd2d} solver, a C++17/MPI finite-volume
solver for the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations on unstructured grids.
The solver reads CGNS meshes, partitions them with METIS, and runs all eight
required benchmark cases (three inviscid NACA0012, three laminar NACA0012, one
steady cylinder Re=20, one transient cylinder Re=200). The solver uses
second-order Green--Gauss reconstruction with a Barth--Jespersen limiter,
Rusanov inviscid flux, Newtonian viscous flux, and LU-SGS implicit time
integration with BDF2 for the transient case. All cases are run with MPI domain
decomposition and neighbor-scoped halo exchange. The CFL is capped below the
supplied values for LU-SGS stability; this conservative deviation is documented.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The benchmark requires building a 2-D unstructured finite-volume CFD solver for
the compressible Navier--Stokes equations. The solver must run eight cases:
three inviscid NACA0012 airfoils at Mach 0.15, 0.8, and 2.0; three laminar
NACA0012 airfoils at Re=5000 for the same Mach numbers; a steady cylinder at
Re=20; and a transient cylinder at Re=200 with vortex shedding. The solver must
use MPI domain decomposition with METIS partitioning, second-order spatial
accuracy with a limiter, an implicit time integration method, and produce
machine-readable outputs satisfying the output contract.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}

The conservative state is
\[
\mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T,
\]
and the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form are
\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y}.
\]
The inviscid fluxes are
\[
\mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The perfect-gas closure is
\[
p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2),\qquad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}.
\]
For viscous cases, the Newtonian stress tensor is
\[
\tau_{xx}=2\mu u_x-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{yy}=2\mu v_y-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{xy}=\mu(u_y+v_x),
\]
with heat flux $\mathbf{q}=-k\nabla T$, $k=\mu c_p/\mathrm{Pr}$. The viscosity is
constant, set from the Reynolds number: $\mu = \rho_\infty U_\infty L_{\mathrm{ref}}/\mathrm{Re}$.

The cases use nondimensional variables with $\rho_\infty=1$, $U_\infty=1$,
$L_{\mathrm{ref}}=1$. The pressure is set from the Mach number:
$p_\infty = \rho_\infty a_\infty^2/\gamma = 1/(M_\infty^2 \gamma)$.

Force coefficients use the freestream dynamic pressure
$q = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty U_\infty^2$ and reference area $A_{\mathrm{ref}}=1$.

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags, and Case Inputs}

The solver reads CGNS meshes using the CGNS library. Multi-zone meshes (the
cylinder mesh has two zones) are merged by matching coincident vertices using a
coordinate-keyed map with tolerance $10^{-9}$. Boundary faces are extracted from
BAR\_2 element sections and matched to the case-file boundary condition mapping.

The NACA0012 mesh (\texttt{NACA0012\_H2.cgns}) has 15,682 vertices, 20,816
cells (10,064 quads + 7,752 tris), with families \texttt{bc-2} (farfield) and
\texttt{bc-4} (wall). The mesh includes interior cells inside the airfoil;
these are excluded by flood-fill from the farfield boundary.

The cylinder mesh (\texttt{CylinderB1.cgns}) has two zones with 10,338 vertices
and 10,185 cells, with families \texttt{WALL} (no-slip wall) and \texttt{FAR}
(farfield).

\section{Spatial Discretization}

The solver uses a cell-centered finite-volume method on the unstructured mesh.
The residual for cell $i$ is
\[
R_i = \frac{1}{V_i}\sum_{\mathrm{faces}} \left(\mathbf{F}^i_n - \mathbf{F}^v_n\right) |\mathbf{n}| \, l_f,
\]
where $\mathbf{F}^i_n$ and $\mathbf{F}^v_n$ are the inviscid and viscous normal
fluxes, $l_f$ is the face length, and $V_i$ is the cell area.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}

Green--Gauss cell gradients are computed from face-midpoint averages:
\[
\nabla \phi_i \approx \frac{1}{V_i}\sum_{\mathrm{faces}} \phi_f \, \mathbf{n} \, l_f.
\]
Piecewise-linear reconstruction gives left/right face states:
\[
\phi_L = \phi_i + \nabla\phi_i \cdot (\mathbf{x}_f - \mathbf{x}_i).
\]
Reconstruction is active for all production runs.

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}

A Barth--Jespersen limiter is applied to density and pressure. The limiter
coefficient for each cell is
\[
\phi_i = \min\left(1, \frac{\phi_{\max}-\phi_i}{\Delta\phi_f^+}, \frac{\phi_{\min}-\phi_i}{\Delta\phi_f^-}\right),
\]
where $\phi_{\max}$ and $\phi_{\min}$ are the max/min of the cell and its
neighbors. If the reconstructed state violates positivity (density or pressure
below 10\% of the cell value), the reconstruction falls back to first order.

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}

The inviscid flux uses the Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann
solver:
\[
\mathbf{F}_{1/2} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{F}_L + \mathbf{F}_R) - \frac{1}{2}s_{\max}(\mathbf{U}_R - \mathbf{U}_L),
\]
where $s_{\max} = \max(|u_n|+a)$ over left and right states.

Viscous fluxes use the Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier heat flux with
averaged gradients at faces. The temperature gradient is derived from pressure
and density gradients.

\section{Boundary Conditions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Farfield}: Characteristic-based. Inflow uses freestream state;
outflow uses zero-gradient (cell state).
\item \textbf{Slip wall (inviscid)}: Reflects normal velocity:
$u_g = u_c - 2(u_c \cdot \mathbf{n})\mathbf{n}$. Density and pressure are
copied.
\item \textbf{No-slip adiabatic wall}: Mirrors velocity: $u_g = -u_c$. Density
and pressure are copied (adiabatic: zero temperature gradient).
\end{itemize}

Ghost cells are created for all boundary faces. Surface output reports boundary
values: no-slip wall rows have near-zero velocity; inviscid slip walls have
near-zero normal velocity.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}

\subsection{Steady Cases}

Steady cases use pseudo-time continuation with local time stepping:
\[
\frac{d\mathbf{U}}{d\tau} + \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}) = 0.
\]
The LU-SGS implicit solver linearizes this as
\[
\left(\frac{1}{\Delta\tau} + \mathbf{A}\right)\Delta\mathbf{U} = -\mathbf{R},
\]
where $\mathbf{A}$ is a simplified flux Jacobian using the spectral radius
$(|u_n|+a)$ plus the viscous spectral radius. The LU-SGS performs forward and
backward sweeps with under-relaxation factor 0.7.

The CFL is ramped from 1.0 to a capped maximum: CFL=5 for inviscid, CFL=2 for
viscous cases. This is a conservative deviation from the supplied CFL values
(up to 100), necessitated by the simplified Jacobian in the LU-SGS. Three
inner iterations with three LU-SGS sweeps are used per pseudo-time step.

\subsection{Cylinder Re=200 Transient}

The Re=200 case uses BDF2 physical-time integration:
\[
\frac{3\mathbf{U}^{n+1} - 4\mathbf{U}^n + \mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t} + \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1}) = 0.
\]
The BDF2 coefficient $3/(2\Delta t)$ is included in the implicit diagonal.
$U^n$ and $U^{n-1}$ are frozen during all inner iterations for $U^{n+1}$.
The history is updated only after the inner solve converges.

The production settings are $\Delta t = 0.01$, $t_f = 300.0$ (30,000 physical
steps), with 5--50 inner iterations and inner residual target $10^{-3}$. The
pseudo-time CFL is fixed at 1.0.

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}

The cell adjacency graph is built from shared faces. METIS PartGraphKway
partitions the graph into $N$ subdomains. Each rank owns a subset of cells
plus ghost cells required by its stencil. Ghost cells are identified from
faces crossing partition boundaries.

Halo exchange uses non-blocking \texttt{MPI\_Isend/Irecv} with neighbor ranks
only. The communication maps are built during preprocessing: each rank knows
which owned cells to send and which ghost cells to receive from each neighbor.
No full-state replication occurs during iterations.

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The LU-SGS uses a simplified spectral-radius Jacobian. This limits the
stable CFL to approximately 5 (inviscid) and 2 (viscous), below the supplied
values. A full flux Jacobian or matrix-free Krylov method would allow higher
CFL and faster convergence.
\item The drag values for viscous cases are higher than reference values,
indicating incomplete convergence. Longer runs or a stronger implicit solver
would improve accuracy.
\item The Re=200 inner iterations may not always reach the $10^{-3}$ target
within the iteration budget, affecting the converged-step fraction.
\item The mesh reader relies on coordinate matching for multi-zone merging;
topology-based 1-to-1 matching would be more robust.
\end{enumerate}
