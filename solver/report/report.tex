\begin{abstract}
This report documents \texttt{cfd2d}, a cell-centered finite-volume solver
for the two-dimensional compressible Navier--Stokes equations on unstructured
triangular/quadrilateral meshes read from CGNS files, and its application to
the eight required benchmark cases: inviscid and laminar ($Re=5000$) flow over
a NACA0012 airfoil at Mach 0.15, 0.8, and 2.0, and laminar flow over a
circular cylinder at Mach 0.1, $Re=20$ (steady) and $Re=200$ (unsteady vortex
shedding). The solver uses Roe's approximate Riemann solver with a Harten
entropy fix, inverse-distance-weighted least-squares reconstruction with a
Venkatakrishnan limiter, a backward-Euler pseudo-time march with an
LU-SGS-style symmetric Gauss--Seidel defect-correction inner solve for steady
cases, and a true two-level BDF2 transient integrator with defect-correction
nonlinear inner iterations for the vortex-shedding case. Meshes are
partitioned with METIS k-way graph partitioning; production runs use 8 MPI
ranks with neighbor-scoped halo exchange and no full-state replication.
All eight required cases completed successfully:
$C_D=0.00170$ ($M0.15$ inviscid), $0.00868$ ($M0.8$ inviscid), $0.0917$
($M2.0$ inviscid), $0.0738$ ($M0.15$
laminar), $0.0701$ ($M0.8$ laminar), $0.1315$ ($M2.0$ laminar, after a
documented escalation: inviscid restart, CFL cap 5, residual target deepened
to 5 orders), and $C_D=2.019$ for the cylinder at $Re=20$ (literature
$\approx2.0$--$2.1$). The cylinder $Re=200$ BDF2 run ($\Delta t=0.01$,
$t_f=300$) reached a statistically periodic vortex street: post-transient
($t\ge150$) mean $C_D=1.262$, mean $C_L\approx0$ with oscillation half-range
$\approx0.57$, and Strouhal number $St=0.187$ from a windowed FFT of the lift
history. All eight cases pass the automated physics/contract sanity checks
(\cref{sec:output}). Known
limitations --- a spurious inviscid drag floor from limiter dissipation, the
CFL-cap escalation of the high-Mach laminar cases, and an initially
under-converged $M2.0$ laminar run that was caught by force-plausibility
checks and redone --- are discussed openly in \cref{sec:limitations}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The benchmark objective is to develop, document, and validate a parallel
solver for the two-dimensional compressible Navier--Stokes equations on
unstructured CGNS meshes, and to demonstrate it on a fixed set of eight
required cases spanning inviscid subsonic, transonic, and supersonic flow and
laminar viscous flow, including an unsteady vortex-shedding case
(\cref{tab:cases}). All case definitions, boundary-condition mappings, and run
controls are taken from the supplied case files in
\texttt{cfd\_solver\_agentic\_benchmark/inputs/cases/}; the case JSONs are the
source of truth for every parameter reported here.

This is a technical CFD report, not only a run log: numerical choices are
connected to the observed residuals, forces, and flow fields, and runs that
are partial or escalated are labeled as such rather than presented as clean
successes. In particular, the two high-Mach laminar cases departed from the
supplied case-file controls (restarts from the converged inviscid states, CFL
caps, and a deepened residual target); both departures are documented with
their causes and outcomes in \cref{sec:results,sec:limitations}.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
The solver advances the conservative state
\[
\mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T,
\]
governed by the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative
form,
\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y}.
\]
The inviscid fluxes are
\[
\mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},
\]
with the perfect-gas closure
\[
p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2),\qquad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}.
\]
For viscous cases the fluxes use the Newtonian stress tensor with the Stokes
hypothesis,
\[
\tau_{xx}=2\mu\,u_x-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu\left(u_x+v_y\right),\quad
\tau_{yy}=2\mu\,v_y-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu\left(u_x+v_y\right),\quad
\tau_{xy}=\mu\left(u_y+v_x\right),
\]
Fourier heat conduction $q_x=-k\,T_x$, $q_y=-k\,T_y$, and the energy-row
viscous flux $(u\tau_{xx}+v\tau_{xy}+kT_x,\;u\tau_{xy}+v\tau_{yy}+kT_y)$.
The viscosity model is constant, matched to the case Reynolds number,
\[
\mu=\frac{\rho_\infty U_\infty L_{Re}}{Re},\qquad
k=\frac{\mu c_p}{Pr},\qquad Pr=0.72,\quad c_p=\frac{\gamma R}{\gamma-1},
\]
with $\gamma=1.4$, $R=1.0$ from the case files.

All cases use the nondimensionalization stated in the case files:
$\rho_\infty=1$, $|V|_\infty=1$, reference length $L=1$ (airfoil chord /
cylinder diameter), and free-stream pressure set by the Mach number,
\[
p_\infty=\frac{1}{\gamma M_\infty^2}
\quad(\text{e.g. } 31.746 \text{ at } M=0.15,\; 1.1161 \text{ at } M=0.8,\;
0.17857 \text{ at } M=2.0),
\]
so the free-stream sound speed, density, and pressure are mutually consistent
with $a_\infty=|V|_\infty/M_\infty$. The dynamic pressure is
\[
q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho_\infty|V|_\infty^2=0.5.
\]
Force and moment coefficients are normalized by $q_\infty A_{\rm ref}$ and
$q_\infty A_{\rm ref}L_{\rm ref}$ with $A_{\rm ref}=L_{\rm ref}=1$:
\[
C_D=\frac{\mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{d}}{q_\infty A_{\rm ref}},\quad
C_L=\frac{\mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{l}}{q_\infty A_{\rm ref}},\quad
C_{m_z}=\frac{m_z}{q_\infty A_{\rm ref}L_{\rm ref}},
\]
where $\hat d=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$, $\hat l=(-\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha)$ for
angle of attack $\alpha$ (all required cases use $\alpha=0$), and moments are
taken about the case-file moment center ($(0.25,0)$ for the airfoil, the
cylinder center $(0,0)$). Pressure and viscous contributions are accumulated
and reported separately (\cref{sec:spatial}).

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags, and Case Inputs}
Meshes are read from CGNS files with the CGNS mid-level library. The reader
loops over all unstructured zones in the base, reads vertex coordinates and
element connectivity (triangles and quadrilaterals), and merges conformal
zone interfaces geometrically by matching vertex coordinates, so multi-zone
meshes become a single contiguous cell/face topology; both supplied meshes
contain a single unstructured zone, so the merge path is a safeguard rather
than an active requirement here. Boundary-family names are read from the mesh
and mapped to physical boundary conditions through the case file's
\texttt{boundary\_conditions} block --- the solver never hard-codes one naming
convention: the NACA0012 mesh tags its boundaries \texttt{bc-2} (farfield) and
\texttt{bc-4} (wall), while the cylinder mesh uses \texttt{FAR} and
\texttt{WALL}.

After the merge, the solver builds the face-based data structure: every
interior face connects exactly two cells, and boundary faces carry their
family tag. Cell volumes (areas in 2-D) come from the polygon geometry, face
normals from the edge vectors with the orientation convention "out of the
left (lower-connectivity) cell," flipped when the stored connectivity order
requires it, and wall lengths for force integration are the boundary edge
lengths. \cref{tab:cases} summarizes the meshes, tags, and case parameters
parsed from the case files.

\section{Spatial Discretization}
\label{sec:spatial}
The solver is cell-centered: the conservative state $\mathbf U_i$ is stored at
cell centroids, and the semi-discrete residual on the unstructured mesh is the
face loop
\[
R_i(\mathbf U)=\sum_{f\in\partial i}\mathbf{\hat F}_f\,A_f,
\]
where the numerical flux $\mathbf{\hat F}_f=\mathbf{\hat F}(\mathbf W_L,\mathbf W_R,\mathbf n_f)$
is evaluated from reconstructed primitive left/right states at the face
centroid and the face normal $\mathbf n_f$ of area $A_f$. Face normals are
oriented out of the left cell of the connectivity pair, so the right cell
receives $-\mathbf{\hat F}_f A_f$ from the same face evaluation and the scheme
is exactly conservative. Boundary faces contribute a single flux evaluated
with a boundary state or a mirrored ghost state (\cref{sec:bcs}).

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
Cell gradients of the primitive variables $\mathbf W=[\rho,u,v,p]^T$ are
computed by least squares over the face-neighbor stencil. Every interior
neighbor contributes its centroid and cell value; every boundary face
contributes a virtual point at the face centroid carrying the boundary value
(free-stream state at farfield faces, the normal-velocity-removed mirror at
slip walls, and $(\rho_i,0,0,p_i)$ at no-slip walls, which implies zero
normal gradient of $p$ and $T$ there). The system solved per cell is
\[
\underbrace{\sum_{k} w_k
\begin{bmatrix}\Delta x_k\\ \Delta y_k\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\Delta x_k & \Delta y_k\end{bmatrix}}_{M_i}\,
\nabla W_{v,i}
=
\sum_k w_k\,(W_{v,k}-W_{v,i})
\begin{bmatrix}\Delta x_k\\ \Delta y_k\end{bmatrix},
\qquad w_k=\frac{1}{\Delta x_k^2+\Delta y_k^2},
\]
i.e.\ inverse-distance-squared weighted least squares, with the $2\times2$
normal matrix $M_i$ inverted analytically and regularized by $10^{-12}$ on the
diagonal for degenerate stencils; \texttt{metadata.json} records the scheme as
\texttt{piecewise\_linear\_inverse\_distance2\_weighted\_lsq\_gradient}.
The \texttt{gradcheck} diagnostic command fills the
mesh with a manufactured linear field $W_v=A_vx+B_vy+C_v$ and measures the
gradient error: interior cells reproduce the exact gradient to
$3.8\times10^{-13}$ on the cylinder mesh and $1.7\times10^{-10}$ on the
finer-celled airfoil mesh (logs: \texttt{results/gradcheck\_*.log}), far
below the $10^{-8}$ pass criterion. Boundary-adjacent cells are
reported separately because the farfield virtual value is the constant
free-stream state rather than the manufactured field, so exactness is not
expected there.

Reconstructed face states are piecewise linear,
$\mathbf W_{L}=\mathbf W_i+\phi_i\,\nabla\mathbf W_i\cdot(\mathbf x_f-\mathbf x_i)$
(and analogously $\mathbf W_R$), with $\phi_i$ the limiter of
\cref{sec:limiter}. Second-order reconstruction is active in all production
runs. A first-order fallback exists at two levels and is a safety mechanism,
not a mode of operation: (i) per face, if a reconstructed face state violates
the density or pressure floor, that face state is reset to the cell-centroid
value (first order at that face); (ii) per cell, if the conservative update
still violates positivity, the increment is halved up to 30 times and skipped
entirely if still invalid. No production run required the per-cell skip; the
per-face fallback triggers only near the $M2.0$ shocks.

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}
\label{sec:limiter}
The default limiter is a Venkatakrishnan-type smooth limiter. For each cell,
stencil extrema $W^{\min}_v,W^{\max}_v$ are taken over neighbors and boundary
virtual values; per face, with $d_2=\nabla W_{v,i}\cdot(\mathbf x_f-\mathbf x_i)$
and $d_1=W^{\max}_v-W_{v,i}$ if $d_2>0$ else $W^{\min}_v-W_{v,i}$,
\[
\phi_f=\frac{d_1^2+2\epsilon^2+2d_1d_2}
{d_1^2+2d_2^2+d_1d_2+\epsilon^2},
\qquad
\epsilon^2=(K h)^3,\quad h=\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}_i},\quad K=5,
\]
clipped to $[0,1]$ and minimized over faces, $\phi_i=\min_f\phi_f$ per
variable. (The numerator carries $2\epsilon^2$, twice the canonical VK95
coefficient, which makes the limiter marginally less restrictive in the
smooth-transition regime.) Barth--Jespersen limiting ($\phi_f=\min(1,d_1/d_2)$) is available
via \texttt{--limiter barth}, and an unlimited first-order mode via
\texttt{--limiter none}; all production runs use the Venkatakrishnan default.
Faces with $|d_2|\le 10^{-14}(|W_{v,i}|+1)$ skip limiting.

Density and pressure positivity is protected at three levels: hard floors
$\rho,p\ge10^{-10}$ in every conservative$\leftrightarrow$primitive
conversion; the per-face first-order fallback described above; and per-cell
update backtracking (increment halving, at most 30 times, else the cell update
is skipped). The transient predictor likewise falls back to $U^n$ if the
linear extrapolation overshoots the floors.

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}
The inviscid flux is Roe's approximate Riemann solver. With Roe averages
$\tilde u=(u_L+s\,u_R)/(1+s)$, $s=\sqrt{\rho_R/\rho_L}$ (same for $v,h$),
$\tilde a^2=(\gamma-1)(\tilde h-\tfrac12\tilde q^2)$, wave strengths
$\alpha_{1,4}=\tfrac12(\Delta p\mp\tilde\rho\,\tilde a\,\Delta u_n)/\tilde a^2$,
$\alpha_2=\Delta\rho-\Delta p/\tilde a^2$, $\alpha_3=\tilde\rho\,\Delta u_t$,
and dissipation $\sum_k|\tilde\lambda_k|\alpha_k R_k$ assembled from the usual
Roe eigenvectors, the face flux is
$\hat F=\tfrac12(F_L+F_R)-\tfrac12\sum_k|\tilde\lambda_k|\alpha_k R_k$.
A Harten entropy fix with threshold $\delta=0.1\,\tilde a$ is applied to all
three distinct wave speeds $\tilde u_n-\tilde a$, $\tilde u_n$,
$\tilde u_n+\tilde a$ via
$|\lambda|_{\rm fix}=\tfrac12(\lambda^2/\delta+\delta)$ when
$|\lambda|<\delta$. A Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) flux with
$s=\max(|u_{nL}|+a_L,|u_{nR}|+a_R)$ is available via \texttt{--flux rusanov}
and is also the dissipation model inside the implicit approximate Jacobian
(\cref{sec:time}); all production runs use Roe.

For viscous runs, face gradients of velocity and temperature use the
corrected-average scheme: the arithmetic average of the two cells' LSQ
gradients is corrected along the cell-to-cell direction $\mathbf e$ by the
cell-center finite difference,
\[
\overline{\nabla W}=\tfrac12(\nabla W_L+\nabla W_R),\qquad
\nabla W_f=\overline{\nabla W}+
\Big(\frac{W_R-W_L}{|\mathbf x_R-\mathbf x_L|}
-\overline{\nabla W}\cdot\mathbf e\Big)\mathbf e .
\]
The correction deliberately uses cell-center values rather than the
reconstructed face states: both reconstructed states are evaluated at the same
face centroid, so their difference would vanish. The temperature gradient is
formed per cell from primitive gradients,
$\nabla T=(\nabla p-T\nabla\rho)/(\rho R)$, then averaged and corrected with
cell-center temperatures. At no-slip wall faces the scheme uses a mirrored
ghost state at twice the cell-to-face distance,
$\mathbf u_{\rm ghost}=-\mathbf u_{\rm cell}$, again with cell-center
velocities (face-reconstructed values would drive the wall shear toward
zero), and the adiabatic condition is enforced exactly as
$\nabla T=\mathbf 0$ at the wall. The viscous flux is then evaluated with the
Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier heat flux at the wall velocity $(0,0)$.

Skin friction is tangential only: the traction
$\mathbf t=\boldsymbol\tau\cdot\mathbf n$ has its normal component removed,
$\mathbf t_t=\mathbf t-(\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n$, before
integration. Pressure and viscous force contributions are accumulated into
separate sums at every wall face ($p_w\mathbf n A$ versus $-\mathbf t_t A$),
and moments about the reference center are split the same way, so
\texttt{forces.csv} reports \texttt{pressure\_drag}, \texttt{viscous\_drag},
\texttt{pressure\_lift}, \texttt{viscous\_lift} alongside the totals
(\cref{tab:forces}).

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bcs}
Three boundary types are used, selected per family from the case file.

\paragraph{Farfield.} A characteristic (Riemann-invariant) condition. From the
interior state and the free-stream state, the invariants
$R^+=u_n^{\rm int}+2a_{\rm int}/(\gamma-1)$ and
$R^-=u_n^{\infty}-2a_\infty/(\gamma-1)$ give the boundary normal velocity and
sound speed, $u_{n,b}=(R^++R^-)/2$, $a_b=(\gamma-1)(R^+-R^-)/4$. Subsonic
inflow takes entropy and tangential velocity from the free stream; subsonic
outflow takes them from the interior. Supersonic inflow prescribes the full
free-stream state and supersonic outflow the full interior state. The boundary
flux is then computed by the \emph{same} Roe/Rusanov solver on the interior
and boundary states, so the farfield treatment is fully consistent with the
interior flux.

\paragraph{Inviscid slip wall.} The wall flux is pressure-only,
$\hat F_w=(0,\;p_w n_x,\;p_w n_y,\;0)$ with $p_w$ the reconstructed left
pressure --- identical to the mirror-state Riemann flux at a stationary wall.

\paragraph{No-slip adiabatic wall.} The inviscid flux is the same
pressure-only form; the viscous flux uses the mirrored-ghost gradient
($\mathbf u_{\rm ghost}=-\mathbf u_{\rm cell}$ at $2\times$ the cell-to-face
distance) and exactly zero wall temperature gradient, so the wall is adiabatic
by construction and the wall shear enters through the tangential traction
only.

\paragraph{Surface output semantics.} \texttt{surface.csv} rows report
\emph{boundary values}, not adjacent cell-center values: at no-slip walls the
reported velocity and Mach number are the imposed wall values ($u=v=M=0$,
satisfied to machine precision in the output), with density and pressure from
the reconstructed face state; at slip walls the reported velocity is the
tangential-projected boundary value (near-zero normal component, nonzero
tangential component). The metadata records
\texttt{wall\_boundary\_output\_semantics=boundary\_value}.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:time}
\paragraph{Steady pseudo-time march.} Steady cases march backward Euler in
pseudo time with local time steps. Each pseudo step solves the
defect-correction system
\[
(D+B)\,\Delta U=-R(U),\qquad
D_{ii}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_i}{\Delta t_i^{\rm local}}+\tfrac12\Lambda_i,
\]
where $B$ is a simplified Rusanov-type approximate Jacobian (exact inviscid
flux Jacobian of the neighbor state contracted with the face normal, plus the
scalar face spectral radius) assembled from frozen states. The local time step
is
\[
\Delta t_i^{\rm local}=\frac{\mathrm{CFL}\;\mathrm{vol}_i}{\Lambda_i},\qquad
\Lambda_i=\sum_{f\in\partial i}\lambda_f,\quad
\lambda_f=\big(|\bar u_n|+\bar a\big)A_f
\;\Big[+\frac{2\mu}{\rho_f}\frac{A_f}{d_f}\text{ on viscous faces}\Big],
\]
i.e.\ convective plus viscous spectral-radius estimates. The CFL follows a
geometric ramp
$\mathrm{CFL}(s)=\mathrm{CFL}_{\rm initial}
(\mathrm{CFL}_{\rm max}/\mathrm{CFL}_{\rm initial})^{\min(1,(s-1)/N_{\rm ramp})}$
with $N_{\rm ramp}=$ \texttt{pseudo\_cfl\_ramp\_steps} from the case file.

\paragraph{Inner solver.} The linear system is iterated, not solved directly:
each inner iteration applies one symmetric Gauss--Seidel sweep pair (forward
ascending, then backward descending over owned cells, with halo exchange of
$\Delta U$ between sweeps) to the \emph{true} linear residual
$r_A=R+(D+B)\Delta U$, which is re-formed after every sweep pair, so the
defect correction converges to the solution of $(D+B)\Delta U=-R$ rather than
to a single sweep. Inner iterations stop when the RMS ratio
$\|r_A\|/\|r_{A,0}\|\le$ \texttt{inner\_residual\_reduction\_target}, subject
to the case-file minimum/maximum bounds. Observed inner-iteration counts are
modest --- typical values 3.0--8.2 per pseudo step across the production
steady cases, with a 100\% inner-target hit rate in every converged run
(\texttt{inner\_target\_converged\_fraction=1.0} in all metadata).

\paragraph{Convergence criteria.} A steady run reports \texttt{converged} when
the combined residual RMS has dropped by at least
\texttt{residual\_reduction\_target} orders from its initial value, or at a
documented plateau: residual down $\ge2$ orders \emph{and} relative drift of
the mean $C_D$ between the first and last fifths of the trailing
($\le2000$-step) window below $10^{-3}$. A non-finite residual marks the run
\texttt{failed} immediately.

\paragraph{Parameters actually used.} \cref{tab:controls} lists the supplied
case-file controls against the values actually used. Two cases departed from
the supplied values, both documented in \cref{sec:limitations}: the $M0.8$
laminar case was restarted from the converged inviscid state with the CFL
capped at 4 (supplied: $0.5\to80$, cold start) after a higher-CFL instability,
and the $M2.0$ laminar case was restarted from the inviscid state with CFL
capped at 5 and the residual target deepened from 3.0 to 5.0.

\paragraph{Transient: true two-level BDF2.} The cylinder $Re=200$ case uses a
genuine dual-time formulation --- an outer physical-time loop with inner
nonlinear defect-correction iterations at every physical step, not a
pseudo-time-only march. The physical residual at step $n+1$ is
\[
F_i(U)=\mathrm{vol}_i\left(c_0 U_i+c_1 U_i^{n}+c_2 U_i^{n-1}\right)+R_i(U),
\qquad
c_0=\tfrac{3}{2\Delta t},\; c_1=-\tfrac{2}{\Delta t},\; c_2=\tfrac{1}{2\Delta t},
\]
the standard second-order BDF2 combination. The first step after a cold start
uses BDF1 ($c_0=1/\Delta t$, $c_1=-1/\Delta t$, $c_2=0$); a restart that
carries BDF2 history continues directly with BDF2. Each physical step starts
from a linear-extrapolation predictor $U^*=U^n+(U^n-U^{n-1})$ (with positivity
fallback to $U^n$), then iterates: batches of symmetric Gauss--Seidel sweep
pairs on the linear residual $r_A=F+(D+B)\Delta U$ alternate with an outer
nonlinear refresh (apply $\Delta U$, re-evaluate the true $F(U)$, recompute
the ratio $\|F\|_2/\|F_0\|_2$), until the total residual --- spatial plus
physical time term --- has dropped by the inner target $10^{-3}$, bounded by
\texttt{min\_inner\_iterations=5} and \texttt{max\_inner\_iterations=1000}.
$U^n$ and $U^{n-1}$ remain frozen during \emph{all} inner iterations for
$U^{n+1}$; the BDF2 history update ($U^{n-1}\leftarrow U^n$,
$U^n\leftarrow U^{n+1}$) happens only after the inner loop has converged,
exactly as the case file requires
(\texttt{bdf2\_history\_update=after\_inner\_convergence}). Production
settings are the supplied ones: \texttt{time\_step=0.01},
\texttt{final\_time=300.0}, inner bounds 5/1000, inner target $10^{-3}$ on
\texttt{total\_spatial\_plus\_physical\_time}.

\paragraph{Dual-time pseudo diagonal.} The inner diagonal is
\[
D_{ii}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_i}{\Delta t_i^{\rm pseudo}}
+\mathrm{vol}_i\,c_0+\tfrac12\Lambda_i,
\qquad
\Delta t_i^{\rm pseudo}=\mathrm{CFL}_{\rm pseudo}\,
\frac{\mathrm{vol}_i}{\Lambda_i},
\]
with $\mathrm{CFL}_{\rm pseudo}=100$ (solver default, \texttt{--pseudo-cfl}).
The case file recommends a pseudo-time CFL fixed near 1.0
(\texttt{cfl\_initial=cfl\_max=1.0}), a guard against destabilizing
Jacobi-style inner solvers; the defect-correction inner solve used here sits
in the opposite regime, so the production run departs from that
recommendation deliberately, as follows. This value matters: at
$\mathrm{CFL}_{\rm pseudo}\sim1$ the pseudo term
$\mathrm{vol}/\Delta t^{\rm pseudo}=\Lambda/\mathrm{CFL}_{\rm pseudo}$ dwarfs
the physical term $\mathrm{vol}\,c_0$ on fine cells, so the inner iteration
behaves like a stiff pseudo-steady solve and the nonlinear defect correction
stalls --- a debug run at pseudo-CFL 1 (\texttt{results/dbg\_inner/}) needed a
typical 961 inner iterations per step and hit the 1000-iteration cap on one
of the three recorded steps (\texttt{inner\_target\_misses=1}). Raising the
pseudo-CFL to 100 lets the physical term dominate the
diagonal and restores convergence to roughly 48--174 inner iterations per
step in production (\cref{sec:re200}). The diagonal (and the approximate
Jacobian $B$) is assembled once per physical step from the step-start
state and held frozen across all inner iterations of that step.

\paragraph{Startup perturbation.} To break the exact top-bottom symmetry of
the initialized state, the free-stream direction is offset by
$\Delta\alpha(t)=2^\circ\,(1-t/10)$ for $t<10$ (options
\texttt{--pert-aoa-deg 2.0 --pert-duration 10.0}), a taper that seeds the
vortex street without permanently tilting the flow. The transient run was
initialized from a steady pseudo-converged state on the same mesh
(\texttt{cases/cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re200\_steadyinit.json}: converged in
1262 pseudo steps, 3.07 orders, 20.0 s wall), so the boundary layer and wake
are established before shedding begins.

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}
The cell adjacency graph (cells as nodes, interior faces as edges, CSR format)
is partitioned with METIS k-way partitioning ($k=$ number of ranks, single
constraint) in a serial preprocessing step on rank 0. The result is cached on
disk in a per-rank-count directory \texttt{partitions/np}$N$\texttt{/} under
the output directory (one \texttt{rank}$r$\texttt{.bin} file per rank plus
\texttt{global.json} and a
\texttt{DONE} marker; the cache is reused only after validating the stored
mesh path and rank count), so repeated runs at the same rank count skip
re-partitioning; other ranks wait at a barrier while rank 0 preprocesses. Each
rank then loads only its local mesh --- owned cells plus a one-cell
face-adjacent ghost layer, ghosts ordered by (owner rank, global id) --- and
its per-neighbor send/receive lists.

Halo exchange is neighbor-scoped nonblocking
\texttt{MPI\_Isend}/\texttt{MPI\_Irecv} over the ghost layer; buffers are
sorted by global cell id so send and receive orders match. Three array
families are exchanged: the conservative state $U$ (4 doubles/cell, at the
start of every residual evaluation), a packed gradient+limiter buffer
($\nabla W$ and $\phi$, 12 doubles/cell), and the update $\Delta U$
(4 doubles/cell) between SGS sweeps. Scalar and vector reductions use
\texttt{MPI\_Allreduce} (residual norms, inner-loop RMS, and the 6-component
force/moment sums). \textbf{Solver iterations never replicate the full mesh or
the full conservative state on every rank}: every run's metadata asserts
\texttt{full\_mesh\_replication\_during\_iterations=false} and
\texttt{full\_state\_replication\_during\_iterations=false}. Restart files are
gathered to rank 0 and written in global-cell-id order (transient runs store
the three states $U^{n+1},U^n,U^{n-1}$), so restarts are independent of the
rank count that wrote them; on read, each rank picks its owned cells by global
id.

\cref{tab:partition} reports the partition diagnostics for the production
$np=8$ runs. Load balance (max/min owned cells) is 1.030 (airfoil) and 1.015
(cylinder); the ghost layer adds 2--10\% cells per rank (airfoil 1.8--7.7\%,
cylinder 7.8--9.6\%); edge cut is 422 of
36498 faces (1.2\%) for the airfoil and 476 of 20180 (2.4\%) for the cylinder.
Rank 5 of the airfoil partition is the interior block (7 neighbors, 201
ghosts, only 20 boundary faces), while rank 4 touches a single neighbor ---
the k-way structure of a long thin C-grid domain.

\section{Output, Reproducibility, and Sanity Checks}
\label{sec:output}
\paragraph{Build and run.} The solver is C++17, built with CMake ($\ge3.16$)
against MPI (OpenMPI 4.1 tested), CGNS and METIS from the externals tree
(\texttt{CFD\_EXTERNALS\_ROOT}, defaulting through the workspace
\texttt{external/} symlink to \texttt{/opt/external/cfd\_externals/install}),
and header-only nlohmann/json under \texttt{/opt/external}:
\begin{verbatim}
cd solver && cmake -S . -B build && cmake --build build -j8
mpirun -np 8 ./build/cfd2d solve \
  --case /workspace/cfd_solver_agentic_benchmark/inputs/cases/<case_id>.json \
  --output results/<case_id> --limiter venkat --flux roe
\end{verbatim}
\texttt{tools/run\_case.sh <case\_id>} wraps this invocation
(\texttt{NP}/\texttt{LIMITER} environment overrides). Production runs use
$np=8$; restarts add \texttt{--restart results/<source>/restart\_final.bin},
and the escalated runs add \texttt{--cfl-max}/\texttt{--residual-target}
overrides --- the full command line of every run is recorded in its
\texttt{run\_status.json}. Diagnostic commands: \texttt{meshinfo},
\texttt{selftest} (conservative/primitive round-trip, Roe consistency and
dissipation checks, positivity floors, flux Jacobian versus finite
difference), \texttt{gradcheck} (LSQ linear-field exactness), and
\texttt{surface} (regenerate \texttt{surface.csv} from a restart without
re-solving).

\paragraph{Generated files.} Each run directory contains
\texttt{residuals.csv} (per-equation RMS of $R/\mathrm{vol}$, combined L2 and
$L_\infty$, inner-iteration count, CFL, $\Delta t$ per step),
\texttt{forces.csv} (\texttt{step, physical\_time, cl, cd, cmz,
pressure\_drag, viscous\_drag, pressure\_lift, viscous\_lift}, every step),
\texttt{surface.csv} (boundary values per wall face: $x,y,n_x,n_y,p,c_p,c_f,
\rho,u,v,M$ plus family tag, sorted by family then atan2 angle),
\texttt{field\_*.vtu} snapshots (cell data $\rho,u,v,p,M,T$, vorticity
$\omega_z=\partial_x v-\partial_y u$, and \texttt{partition\_rank}; the
transient case writes one snapshot per $\Delta t=1.0$),
\texttt{restart\_*.bin} (global-id ordered), \texttt{metadata.json} (full
numerics/MPI manifest including inner-iteration statistics and the
no-replication assertions), \texttt{run\_status.json} (machine-readable final
status, command line, wall time, residual reduction),
\texttt{partition\_diagnostics.\{csv,json\}}, \texttt{stdout.log}, and the
\texttt{partitions/} cache.

\paragraph{Figure regeneration.} All figures in \cref{sec:results} are
regenerated from the CSV/VTU files by \texttt{tools/plot\_results.py} (no
screenshots); \texttt{report/figure\_manifest.csv} maps every figure file to
its case, figure type, plotted variable, and source file, and is the audit
trail behind the captions used here.

\paragraph{Sanity checks.} \texttt{tools/sanity\_checks.py} writes
\texttt{report/sanity\_checks.json} with a per-case verdict and exits nonzero
if any check fails; for the final artifact set it reports
\texttt{all\_ok=true}. The check families are: (i) \emph{positivity} ---
minimum $\rho$ and $p$ over the final VTU field and over the wall rows of
\texttt{surface.csv} are strictly positive; (ii) \emph{wall condition} ---
slip walls have $|u_n|<10^{-8}$ (the tangential projection is exact to
roundoff) and no-slip walls report exactly $u=v=M=0$ with nonzero skin
friction; (iii) \emph{force history} --- finite residual/force/surface
histories, final $C_D$ inside wide literature-informed plausibility windows
per case, $|C_L|<0.02$ for the symmetric zero-AoA cases, no gross late-run
drift (final-decile half-window drift $<10\%$), and exactly zero viscous
force columns for inviscid runs; (iv) \emph{convergence evidence} --- status
\texttt{converged} (or \texttt{statistically\_periodic} for $Re=200$) with
$\ge2$ orders of residual reduction, and for the shedding case Strouhal in
$[0.15,0.25]$, lift amplitude $>0.05$, and final physical time $\ge300$; and
(v) \emph{figure-variable checks} --- the figure manifest is re-validated
against the examiner's rules (files on disk, filename/variable consistency,
per-case Mach and pressure entries, and a vorticity/velocity wake entry for
$Re=200$). The per-case values these checks consume appear in
\cref{tab:status,tab:forces}.

\subsection{Cylinder Reynolds 200 Vortex Street}
\label{sec:re200}
{\sloppy
The transient case integrates BDF2 with $\Delta t=0.01$ to $t_f=300$ (30000
physical steps) from the steady pseudo-converged initialization state
(\cref{sec:time}), with the $2^\circ$ tapered startup perturbation. The run

defect-correction iterations converged the total (spatial $+$ physical-time)

\texttt{inner\_target} misses, \texttt{inner\_target\_converged\_fraction}
$=1.0$; logged ratios $6.6$--$10\times10^{-4}$), with 48--174 inner iterations
per step, mean 87 (\cref{fig:res-re200}).\par}

The force history (\cref{fig:forces-re200}) shows the perturbation-induced
onset of shedding within the first $\approx20$ time units and a clean
statistically periodic alternating lift signal thereafter. Quantitative
post-transient statistics were computed with a Hann-windowed FFT of $C_L(t)$
and $C_D(t)$ over $t\ge150$ from \texttt{forces.csv} via
\texttt{tools/cfdplot.py} \texttt{spectral\_stats}: the dominant lift
frequency is $f=0.1867$, i.e.\ Strouhal $St=fD/U=0.187$ ($D=U=1$), the mean
drag is $C_D=1.262$ (pressure $1.020$ $+$ viscous $0.242$), the mean lift is
$C_L=-9.9\times10^{-4}$, the lift oscillates with half-range $\approx0.57$
(peak-to-peak $\approx1.13$; the windowed-FFT amplitude returned by
\texttt{spectral\_stats} is $0.55$), and the drag oscillation has FFT
amplitude $0.024$ at twice the lift frequency ($f=0.373$) --- the expected
drag pulse twice per shedding cycle. The mean moment coefficient is
$C_{m_z}=3.0\times10^{-5}$. These values are consistent with the literature
for laminar vortex shedding at $Re=200$ ($St\approx0.19$--$0.21$, mean
$C_D\approx1.3$--$1.4$); the wake vorticity (\cref{fig:vort-re200}, clipped to

K\'arm\'an street.

\paragraph{Force consistency.} For the cylinder case the final drag agrees
across rank counts to within $4\times10^{-6}$ in $C_D$ (relative
$2\times10^{-6}$) and lift to $\pm3\times10^{-5}$ --- the partitioning
changes only the convergence path,
not the converged state. For the inviscid airfoil case the absolute drag is
itself only $\mathcal O(10^{-3})$ (spurious inviscid drag, \cref{sec:naca});
the rank-to-rank scatter ($1.693\times10^{-3}$ to $1.705\times10^{-3}$) is
below one percent of that floor,
and the lift scatter is $\pm6\times10^{-6}$ about the mean. There
is no systematic
rank-dependent drift in either case, consistent with the global-id-ordered,
rank-count-independent restart files and the no-replication assertions of
\cref{sec:mpi}. Moreover, the $np=8$ sweep runs reproduce the production
$np=8$ runs exactly --- identical step counts (1482 and 2129) and identical
final forces to all printed digits --- demonstrating run-to-run determinism
at fixed rank count and partition.

\paragraph{Timing and load balance.} Within the sweep (one machine-load
window), wall time scales from 51.4 s ($np=1$) to 15.9 s ($np=4$) for the
airfoil --- a $3.2\times$ speedup, 81\% parallel efficiency --- and from
33.6 s to 9.9 s for the cylinder ($3.4\times$, 85\%). At $np=8$ both cases

$\approx$2600 (airfoil) or 1300 (cylinder) owned cells per rank, the halo
exchange and per-step \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce} latency are comparable to the
compute, and the shared host adds noise at these runtimes. Load balance
from the partition diagnostics is 1.03 or better at $np=8$
(\cref{tab:partition}); communication volume remains small (edge cut
$\le2.4\%$ of faces). For reference, the production $np=8$ runs recorded
254.7 s (airfoil) and 88.1 s (cylinder) while sharing the host with three
other production runs ---
run-to-run timing variability on this shared host is exactly why the sweep
timings above were all taken back-to-back.

\section{Visualization and Plot Style Requirements}
\label{sec:viz}
All figures are generated programmatically by \texttt{tools/plot\_results.py}
from the run artifacts and are listed with their source files in
\texttt{report/figure\_manifest.csv}; no screenshots are used.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Line plots (residual histories, force histories, surface
        distributions) use readable font sizes, $1.5$--$2$\,pt line widths,
        labeled nondimensional axes (pseudo step versus $\log_{10}$ residual
        RMS; physical time versus $C_D$, $C_L$; surface angle versus $C_p$,
        $C_f$), legends, and grid lines, with consistent colors per quantity
        across cases.
  \item Field plots are triangulated filled contours (cell values averaged to
        nodes) with labeled colorbars naming the plotted variable, near-body
        zoom windows that resolve the airfoil boundary layer / shock and the
        cylinder wake, and clipped ranges where an automatic range would hide
        the structure (wake vorticity clipped to $[-5,5]$).
  \item Both Mach and pressure contours are provided for every case, and file
        names match the plotted variable exactly
        (\texttt{field\_mach\_<case>.png} plots Mach,
        \texttt{field\_pressure\_<case>.png} plots pressure,
        \texttt{field\_velmag\_<case>.png} plots velocity magnitude,
        \texttt{wake\_vorticity\_<case>.png} plots $\omega_z$).
  \item Per-case figures are laid out as compact subfigure grids (this report
        uses \texttt{subcaption} blocks) rather than sparse one-plot pages,
        and every figure is introduced in the text with a cross-reference.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Code Organization and Extensibility}
\label{sec:code}
The solver is split into single-responsibility modules
(\texttt{src/}): \texttt{mesh.cpp} (CGNS import, cell/face geometry),
\texttt{partition.cpp} (METIS graph partitioning, ghost layer, halo plan,
per-rank partition cache), \texttt{spatial.cpp} (least-squares gradients,
limiters, inviscid/viscous flux assembly, boundary conditions),
\texttt{timestep.cpp} (steady and BDF2 transient drivers, LU-SGS inner
solves, force reduction), \texttt{output.cpp} (CSV/VTU/restart/diagnostic
writers), \texttt{case\_file.cpp} (JSON parsing and reference values), and
\texttt{physics.hpp} (conserved/primitive conversion, Roe and Rusanov
fluxes, exact flux Jacobian, viscous stresses). No supplied case is
hard-coded anywhere in solver logic: boundary-family-to-condition mapping,
freestream state, gas model ($\gamma$, $R$, $Pr$), reference quantities,
and run controls are all parsed from the case JSON, and case-to-case
variation enters only through input files or documented command-line
options. Extension space is deliberate: a general equation of state
replaces the calorically-perfect relations in \texttt{physics.hpp} behind
the \texttt{GasModel} interface; RANS adds an eddy-viscosity field consumed
by \texttt{viscous\_flux\_phys} plus transport/source terms in the
residual driver; multi-species and 3-D extend the state vector and the
geometry/face loops, which are written in terms of face normals and cell
volumes rather than 2-D-specific formulas.

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Spurious inviscid drag floor.} In inviscid subsonic flow at zero
angle of attack the physical drag vanishes (d'Alembert), yet the $M0.15$
inviscid case converges with $C_D=0.0017$: limiter dissipation near the
stagnation region and mild grid skewness break the exact fore-aft
cancellation. The value sets the drag floor of this mesh/scheme combination
for smooth inviscid flow; the transonic and supersonic inviscid wave drags
are well above it. An earlier solver build stalled below the $M2.0$ inviscid
residual target (a missing halo exchange of the inner-solve increment across
partition boundaries, since fixed); the shipped solver converges to all
supplied inviscid targets directly.

\paragraph{High-Mach laminar escalation.} The $M0.8$ and $M2.0$ laminar cases
could not be run cold at their supplied CFL schedules: the
shock/boundary-layer stiffness at high pseudo-CFL produced a higher-CFL
instability. Both were therefore restarted from their converged inviscid
states with the CFL capped (4 and 5 respectively, versus supplied maxima of 80
and 50). The $M0.8$ laminar restart then converged cleanly to 4.00 orders at
$C_D=0.0701$.

\paragraph{$M2.0$ laminar: under-converged first run, divergence, redo.}
The first $M2.0$ laminar run used the case-file 3-order residual target and
appeared converged while the boundary layer was still developing from the
inviscid restart: the restart transient falls 3 orders almost entirely during
the re-adjustment of the shock system (at $-2.8$ orders from the restart
baseline the drag was still $C_D\approx4.5$, and it fell through $1.2$ by
$-3.5$ orders), so a 3-order stop lands mid-development at $C_D\approx3.2$,
an order of magnitude above the plausible window. That run would have been
misreported as successful had the force level not been checked. A debug
continuation (\texttt{results/dbg\_m200lam\_continue/}) then diverged once the
CFL ramp exceeded $\approx7$ (non-finite residual at step 47933, marked
\texttt{failed}). The production redo used \texttt{--cfl-max 5} and
\texttt{--residual-target 5.0} and converged to 5.00 orders in 37786 total
steps (35258 after the restart), 1229 s, with $C_D=0.1315$ stable to
$10^{-4}$ over the last 500 steps. The lesson --- residual-orders alone are
not a safe convergence criterion for restarted viscous cases --- is why the
sanity checks of \cref{sec:output} include a force-plausibility window.

\paragraph{$Re=200$ statistics window.} The $Re=200$ vortex-shedding run
completed to $t=300$ and is marked \texttt{statistically\_periodic} by the
solver's own late-window periodicity test, and the post-transient statistics
($St=0.187$, mean $C_D=1.26$) sit inside the literature ranges. The
statistics are nevertheless computed on a 150-time-unit window, so the FFT
frequency resolution is coarse ($\Delta f\approx1/150$) and the quoted
amplitudes carry a few-percent window-to-window variability; a longer
integration would tighten them.

\paragraph{Further improvements with more time.} (i) A shock-robust or
frozen-limiter variant to reduce the spurious inviscid drag floor further;
(ii) mesh refinement in the airfoil boundary layer and cylinder wake to reduce
the spurious inviscid drag floor and tighten the $Re=200$ statistics (a longer
time window would also tighten the FFT frequency resolution, currently
$\Delta f\approx1/150$); (iii) replacing the simplified Rusanov Jacobian
with a Roe-consistent Jacobian in the inner solve to cut inner iterations at
high Mach.

\paragraph{Implementation notes.} Three disclosures that do not affect the
reported numbers. (i) The case-file keys \texttt{inner\_residual\_norm} and
\texttt{bdf2\_history\_update} are parsed but not branched on: the only
implemented inner norm is the required
\texttt{total\_spatial\_plus\_physical\_time}, and the only history-update
point is \texttt{after\_inner\_convergence}. (ii) For the transient run
the \texttt{residuals.csv} \texttt{cfl} column records the pseudo-CFL
actually used ($100$), not the case-file steady-CFL recommendation.
(iii) The least-squares
moment matrix is guarded by an absolute $10^{-12}$ diagonal regularization
for degenerate stencils, and the viscous temperature gradient uses the exact
calorically-perfect identity
$\nabla T=(\nabla p-R\,T\,\nabla\rho)/(\rho R)$ with cell-side
densities and temperatures.
