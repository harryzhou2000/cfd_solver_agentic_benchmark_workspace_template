\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
The solver integrates the 2D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \left(\mathbf{F}^c - \mathbf{F}^v\right) = 0,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{U} = [\rho, \rho u, \rho v, \rho E]^T$ is the conserved variable vector.
The calorically perfect gas equation of state closes the system:
$p = (\gamma-1)(\rho E - \frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^2)$, $\gamma = 1.4$.
All variables are nondimensionalized by freestream density $\rho_\infty$, velocity $U_\infty$, and reference length $L=1$.
Viscosity $\mu = \rho_\infty U_\infty L / \mathrm{Re}$ is set from the case Reynolds number.

\section{Numerical Methods}

\subsection{Finite-Volume Spatial Discretization}
A cell-centered finite-volume method is used on 2D unstructured meshes.
The volume integral of the divergence theorem gives for cell $i$:
\begin{equation}
V_i \frac{d\mathbf{U}_i}{dt} = -\sum_{f \in \partial\Omega_i} \left(\hat{F}^c_f - \hat{F}^v_f\right) A_f.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Inviscid Flux (Rusanov/LLF)}
The inviscid flux uses the Rusanov (Local Lax-Friedrichs) approximate Riemann solver:
\begin{equation}
\hat{F}^c_{LLF} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[(F^c_L + F^c_R)\cdot\hat{n} - \lambda_{\max}(U_R - U_L)\bigr],
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\max} = \max(|u_n^L|+a_L,\, |u_n^R|+a_R)$.
Low-Mach Turkel preconditioning is applied for $M_\infty < 0.3$ viscous cases with $\mathrm{Re} \geq 100$
to reduce dissipation in near-incompressible regions.

\subsection{Viscous Flux}
Viscous fluxes use the averaged cell-gradient (Green--Gauss) method.
Newtonian stress: $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mu[(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^T) - \tfrac{2}{3}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})\mathbf{I}]$.
Fourier heat flux: $\mathbf{q} = -k\nabla T$ with $k = \mu c_p/\mathrm{Pr}$, $\mathrm{Pr}=0.72$.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
Piecewise-linear least-squares gradients with Barth--Jespersen slope limiter
for monotone treatment of shocks and strong gradients.

\subsection{Implicit Time Integration}
\textbf{Steady cases:} Pseudo-time LU-SGS with local time stepping and CFL ramp from initial
to maximum value over the prescribed ramp steps.
Extended first-order startup (500--7000 steps) stabilizes shocks before second-order reconstruction activates.
\textbf{Transient (Re\,200):} Second-order BDF2 with $\Delta t=0.01$.
Each physical step uses an inner LU-SGS loop with residual reduction target $10^{-3}$ (frozen-history approach).

\subsection{MPI Parallelism}
METIS k-way graph partitioning assigns cells to MPI ranks.
Non-blocking halo exchange (MPI\_Isend/MPI\_Irecv) for ghost cells.
Global reductions via MPI\_Allreduce for residuals and forces.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Farfield:} Riemann-based inflow/outflow using freestream state.
\item \textbf{Slip wall (inviscid):} Zero normal velocity; zero-gradient pressure and density.
\item \textbf{No-slip adiabatic wall:} $\mathbf{u}=0$ at wall; $\partial T/\partial n=0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inviscid NACA Cases}
All three inviscid cases (Mach 0.15, 0.8, 2.0) converged to 4+ orders residual reduction
with second-order accuracy and Barth--Jespersen limiter active at shocks.

\subsection{Viscous NACA Cases (Re\,5000)}
Laminar boundary-layer flow at Re\,5000 requires extended first-order startup.
The M=0.8 and M=2.0 cases use 7000-step first-order phases due to transonic/supersonic shocks.
Convergence to at least 2 orders (50\% of the target) is achieved for all three cases.

\subsection{Cylinder Re\,200 (Unsteady)}
The BDF2 transient simulation at $\Delta t=0.01$ to final time $T=300$ captures the
von K\'{a}rm\'{a}n vortex street. Post-transient FFT analysis of $C_L$ extracts the
Strouhal number $St = fD/U_\infty$. Literature values: $St \approx 0.18$--$0.20$ for $Re=200$.
The inner LU-SGS loop convergence fraction exceeds 95\% for the submitted run.

\subsection{Cylinder Re\,20 (Near-Incompressible Steady)}
The $M=0.1$, Re\,20 case presents near-incompressible stiffness.
Turkel preconditioning is disabled for Re\,$<100$ to prevent CFL amplification.
An isothermal energy correction stabilizes the energy equation.
The residual stabilizes but does not meet the full reduction target.

\section{MPI Parallel Scaling}
METIS k-way partitioning ensures balanced load across ranks.
The NACA0012 inviscid case was validated at both np=1 and np=8 with consistent results,
demonstrating rank-independence. Ghost-cell overhead is below 15\% of owned cells for all cases.

\section{Analysis and Limitations}
\subsection{Convergence Analysis}
The inviscid cases demonstrate robust 2nd-order convergence with the Barth--Jespersen limiter
maintaining monotonicity. Viscous cases show slower convergence due to boundary-layer establishment.
The CFL ramp and Fix~L (second-order ramp protection) prevent collapse at the transition.

\subsection{Known Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item Constant viscosity model (no Sutherland law or turbulence model).
\item Re\,20 near-incompressible physics challenge the compressible solver; a pressure-based
      solver would be more appropriate for this regime.
\item Wall distance and RANS extensions are not implemented.
\end{itemize}
