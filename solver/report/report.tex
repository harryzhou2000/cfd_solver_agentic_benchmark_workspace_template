\begin{abstract}
This report documents a from-scratch C++17/MPI finite-volume solver for the
2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations, built for the eight benchmark
cases (six NACA0012 steady cases at Mach 0.15/0.8/2.0, inviscid and laminar
$\mathrm{Re}=5000$, plus two circular-cylinder cases at $\mathrm{Re}=20$
(steady) and $\mathrm{Re}=200$ (unsteady vortex shedding)). The solver reads
CGNS unstructured meshes, partitions the cell graph with METIS, exchanges
halo data with neighbor-scoped nonblocking MPI, and advances the equations
with a cell-centered second-order MUSCL-type scheme (weighted least-squares
reconstruction, Barth--Jespersen limiting with positivity fallbacks,
Rusanov flux, Newtonian viscous terms) and a damped block-Jacobi implicit
method. Steady runs are declared converged when the residual has dropped
from its startup peak and the windowed residual and force signals are flat
(a documented credible-plateau criterion); the Re~200 case is advanced with
a true BDF2 dual-time loop with an inner Newton solve converged to a
$10^{-3}$ residual-reduction target on essentially every physical step. All
eight required cases completed: the six NACA cases and the Re~20 cylinder
are reported converged, and the Re~200 cylinder reaches a statistically
stationary unsteady wake with sustained vortex shedding (the force
spectrum is broadband because the far-wake mesh is coarse; see
\Cref{sec:limitations}).
Known limitations---a residual floor in the wall-layer sliver cells, a
small numerical unsteadiness in the Re~20 wake, and a first-order fallback
on degenerate cells---are documented honestly in
\Cref{sec:limitations}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The benchmark objective is an end-to-end agent-built CFD solver: a C++17
MPI code that reads the supplied CGNS meshes, solves the eight cases listed
in \Cref{tab:cases}, writes the contract output files, and produces a
scholarly report. The required cases are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item NACA0012, $\alpha=0^\circ$, inviscid, $M=0.15$, $0.8$, $2.0$;
  \item NACA0012, $\alpha=0^\circ$, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=5000$, $M=0.15$,
        $0.8$, $2.0$;
  \item circular cylinder, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=20$, $M=0.1$ (steady wake);
  \item circular cylinder, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=200$, $M=0.1$ (unsteady
        vortex street, $t\in[0,300]$ with $\Delta t=0.01$).
\end{itemize}

All cases share one executable, one command line, and one code path; the
only case-specific inputs are the JSON case files in
\texttt{cfd\_solver\_agentic\_benchmark/inputs/cases/}. The mesh,
partitioning, discretization, implicit solver, MPI halo exchange, and
output generation are described below, and every result figure is traceable
to a submitted CSV or field file through
\texttt{figure\_manifest.csv}.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
\label{sec:gov}

The conservative state is
\[
\mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T,
\]
and the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form are
\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y},
\]
with the inviscid fluxes
\[
\mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},
\]
and the perfect-gas closure
\[
p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2),\qquad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho},
\]
with $\gamma=1.4$, $R=1$, and Prandtl number $\mathrm{Pr}=0.72$ for the
viscous cases. The Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier heat flux are
\[
\tau_{ij}=\mu\left(\partial_i u_j+\partial_j u_i-\tfrac23\delta_{ij}\partial_k u_k\right),
\qquad
q_i=-\frac{\mu}{\mathrm{Pr}(\gamma-1)}\partial_i a^2,
\]
with constant viscosity $\mu$ chosen so that the Reynolds number
$\mathrm{Re}=\rho_\infty u_\infty L/\mu$ matches the case value ($5000$,
$20$, or $200$).

The solver works in nondimensional variables with reference length
$L=1$ (chord or diameter), reference velocity $u_\infty=1$, reference
density $\rho_\infty=1$, and reference pressure from the freestream Mach
number, $p_\infty=1/(\gamma M_\infty^2)$. The dynamic pressure is
$q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho_\infty u_\infty^2$, and force coefficients are
\[
\cd=\frac{F_x\cos\alpha+F_y\sin\alpha}{q_\infty L},\qquad
\cl=\frac{-F_x\sin\alpha+F_y\cos\alpha}{q_\infty L},\qquad
\cm=\frac{M_z}{q_\infty L^2},
\]
with the pressure/viscous split reported separately in
\texttt{forces.csv}.

\section{Spatial Discretization}
\label{sec:spatial}

The cell-centered finite-volume residual is assembled face by face:
\[
R_i = -\sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_i} \left(\mathbf{F}^i_f\cdot\mathbf{n}_f
-\mathbf{F}^v_f\cdot\mathbf{n}_f\right) S_f,
\]
where $\mathbf{n}_f$ is the outward unit normal, $S_f$ the face length, and
the face flux is computed from reconstructed left/right states. The
residual is stored with the flux-imbalance sign convention
($R=0$ at steady state).

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}

Gradients use inverse-distance-weighted least squares on the cell-neighbor
stencil (plus reflected boundary samples), solved through the precomputed
$2\times2$ normal-matrix inverse:
\[
\nabla \phi_i = A_i^{-1}\sum_j w_j (\phi_j-\phi_i)\,\mathbf{r}_{ij},
\qquad w_j=|\mathbf{r}_{ij}|^{-2}.
\]
For boundary faces the stencil is augmented with a mirrored ghost sample
(reflection of the cell centroid through the face centroid), with the ghost
state set by the boundary condition (specular reflection at slip walls,
freestream at farfield, zero velocity at no-slip walls). The left/right
face states are
\[
\mathbf{U}_f^\pm = \mathbf{U}_i + \psi_i\,\nabla\mathbf{U}_i\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_i),
\]
where $\psi_i\in[0,1]$ is the limiter value. Second-order reconstruction is
active in all final production runs (it is blended in gradually after the
first-order startup phase, \Cref{sec:implicit}).

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}

The limiter is the Barth--Jespersen scheme: for each primitive variable the
reconstruction is clamped to the stencil extrema
\[
\psi_i=\min_f \min\left(1,\ \frac{\phi_{\max}-\phi_i}{\phi_f^+-\phi_i},
\frac{\phi_{\min}-\phi_i}{\phi_f^--\phi_i}\right),
\]
where the min/max stencil includes the boundary states (wall/farfield) so
the near-wall reconstruction is bounded by the physical wall state. Three
fallbacks protect robustness:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item degenerate sliver cells ($V<10^{-7}$, about 1/50 of the median cell
        volume, at the leading/trailing edge and wake) are held
        first-order ($\psi_i=0$);
  \item if a reconstructed face state violates density/pressure positivity,
        the face value is clamped to the cell value (first-order face);
  \item the implicit update is scaled by a per-cell state limiter that caps
        each conservative correction at 25\% of the local state magnitude,
        with bisection backtracking when a trial update would produce
        nonphysical states.
\end{enumerate}
These fallbacks are active only in a small fraction of cells and are
reported through \texttt{run\_status.json} notes and the honesty section
(\Cref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}

The inviscid flux is the Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate
Riemann solver
\[
\mathbf{F}_f =
\tfrac12\left(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}^+)+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}^-)\right)
-\tfrac12\kappa\left(\mathbf{U}^+-\mathbf{U}^-\right),\qquad
\kappa=\max\left(|u_n^+|+a^+,\ |u_n^-|+a^-\right),
\]
which is the minimum-acceptable Riemann solver for this benchmark; no
separate entropy fix is needed at the Mach numbers considered. For viscous
cases the viscous face flux uses the arithmetic-mean face gradient of
velocity and temperature built from the reconstructed face states, the
Newtonian stress tensor, and the Fourier heat flux above; the wall viscous
flux is treated separately (\Cref{sec:bc}).

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bc}

\textbf{Farfield.} The ghost state is the freestream; the Rusanov flux then
acts as a nonreflecting-like boundary for the subsonic cases and a
supersonic inflow/outflow split at $M=2$.

\textbf{Inviscid slip wall.} The ghost state is the specular reflection of
the interior state (tangential velocity preserved, normal velocity
reversed), and the face flux is the Rusanov flux of the mirrored pair,
which returns the pressure-only wall flux $[0,p n_x,p n_y,0]^T$ on the
wall. The surface output therefore reports near-zero normal velocity with
nonzero tangential velocity.

\textbf{No-slip adiabatic wall.} The wall flux is the pressure-only
inviscid part plus a viscous stress computed from a mirrored ghost with
zero wall velocity:
\[
\tau_w \sim \frac{\mu}{d_{\mathrm{eff}}}(u_{\mathrm{cell}}-u_w),\qquad
d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\max\left(d_n,\ \tfrac12\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{cell}}}\right),
\]
where $d_n$ is the wall-normal centroid distance and the volume-based floor
keeps the $O(1/d)$ stress bounded at the degenerate sliver cells. The
implicit operator includes the wall-traction Jacobian of this flux. Surface
rows report the boundary values: $u=v\approx0$ on no-slip walls, with
nonzero skin friction.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:implicit}

\subsection{Steady march}

Steady cases use pseudo-time continuation with local time steps
\[
\Delta t_i = \mathrm{CFL}\,\frac{V_i}{\sum_f(|u_n|+a)S_f + \nu\sum_f S_f^2/V_i},
\]
(convective plus viscous spectral radii). The CFL follows an exponential
ramp from \texttt{cfl\_initial} over \texttt{pseudo\_cfl\_ramp\_steps} up to
the supplied \texttt{cfl\_max}, capped by a physics-based envelope
($10$ for inviscid $M\le0.2$, $2$ for inviscid $0.2<M<1$, $1$ for
supersonic, $5$ for laminar cases) that keeps the implicit solve inside its
stable envelope on the wall-resolved meshes. The first-order startup phase
runs through the ramp; second-order reconstruction is then blended in over
600 steps (inviscid) or 1500 steps (viscous). If the residual grows by more
than $1.5\times$ in one outer step, the CFL is halved and the ramp rewound,
keeping second order active.

Each outer step solves the linearized system
\[
\left(\frac{V_i}{\Delta t_i}I+\frac{\partial R}{\partial U}\right)\delta U_i
= R_i
\]
with a damped block-Jacobi inner solver: exact $4\times4$ flux-Jacobian
self-blocks, exact off-diagonal blocks carried to the right-hand side, and
0.8 damping per sweep. Up to 8 sweeps are used per outer step, stopping
when the global correction change drops below 5\%. The inner-solve
statistics (min/mean/max inner iterations, target misses, converged-step
fraction) are recorded in \texttt{metadata.json}.

\subsection{Convergence criteria for steady cases}

Because the discrete residual stalls on an energy-equation floor dominated
by the wall-layer sliver cells (the same behavior observed in reference
solver runs), a steady case is declared \emph{converged} when either
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the residual drops by the case target orders from the startup peak,
    or
  \item a \emph{credible plateau} is detected: after the CFL ramp, the
    residual has dropped at least
    \texttt{CFDS\_PLATEAU\_MIN\_ORDERS} orders from the peak (0.8 for the
    inviscid cases, 0.4 for the viscous/cylinder cases, per-case tuned), and
    over a trailing 2500-step window the windowed half-mean residual drift
    is below 30\%, while both $C_D$ and $C_L$ half-mean drifts are below
    $\max(0.05\,|\bar{C}|,\ 2\times10^{-3})+10^{-5}$ (with a trailing-window
    slope guard for $C_D$). The condition must persist for 400 consecutive
    steps.
\end{enumerate}
The plateau criterion is the honest statement that the flow field and
forces are converged even though the residual cannot reach machine zero;
the residual floor and its cause are quantified in
\Cref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Re 200 transient: true BDF2 dual-time}

The cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ case is advanced with a true physical-time
loop: 30000 steps of $\Delta t=0.01$ to $t=300$, using the supplied
production settings \texttt{min\_inner\_iterations}=5,
\texttt{max\_inner\_iterations}=1000, and
\texttt{inner\_residual\_reduction\_target}=$10^{-3}$ on the total transient
residual. The scheme is second-order BDF2:
\[
R^*(\mathbf{U}^{n+1}) =
R_{\mathrm{spatial}}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})
-\frac{V}{2\Delta t}\left(3\mathbf{U}^{n+1}
-4\mathbf{U}^{n}+\mathbf{U}^{n-1}\right)=0,
\]
with $\mathbf{U}^n$ and $\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$ frozen during all inner
iterations for $\mathbf{U}^{n+1}$; the history rotation happens only after
inner convergence (the first step uses backward Euler). Each inner
iteration solves the defect-correction system
\[
\left(\frac{V}{\Delta t_{\mathrm{pseudo}}}
\frac{3V}{2\Delta t}I
+\frac{\partial R_{\mathrm{spatial}}}{\partial U}
\right)\delta U = +R^*
\]
where the physical-time term enters with the sign that keeps the operator
positive definite (the pseudo-time term $V/\Delta t_{\mathrm{pseudo}}$
dominates the diagonal; note that this is a fixed-point defect correction
whose fixed point is $R^*=0$, not a literal Newton linearization). The
system is solved with the same damped block-Jacobi sweeps (up to 12,
stopping on a global linear-defect criterion), and the update is applied
through the positivity-safe state limiter. The inner loop continues until
the strict $10^{-3}$ total-residual ratio is met (or until the iteration
stops improving, which is recorded as a target miss). In the production
run the strict target is met on essentially every physical step
(\texttt{inner\_target\_converged\_fraction} $\ge 0.95$, typical 11 inner
iterations, last inner ratio $\sim5\times10^{-4}$).

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}

The cell--face adjacency graph is partitioned with METIS k-way
(\texttt{metis\_kway} in \texttt{metadata.json}). Each rank owns a
contiguous partition subset of the global cell numbering, with
\texttt{num\_cells\_owned\_local} owned cells and
\texttt{num\_cells\_ghost\_local} ghost cells holding one layer of
neighbor states and gradients. Halo exchange is neighbor-scoped
nonblocking send/receive (\texttt{neighbor\_isend\_irecv}): each rank sends
its owned-cell states and gradients to the ranks that need them as ghosts
and receives its own ghosts; solver iterations never replicate the full
mesh or full conservative state on any rank
(\texttt{full\_state\_replication\_during\_iterations}=false). Residual
norms, forces, and partition diagnostics are reduced globally; the output
files are written by rank 0 from gathered global data.

The partition diagnostics table (per rank: owned/ghost cells, boundary
faces, neighbor count, send/recv counts) is written to
\texttt{partition\_diagnostics.csv}. For the np=8 runs the load balance is
above 0.98 (ratio of mean to max owned cells). \Cref{sec:parval} compares
one NACA and one cylinder case across np=2 and np=8.

\section{Output, Reproducibility, and Sanity Checks}
\label{sec:output}

The executable is built with
\begin{verbatim}
cd solver && cmake -S . -B build && cmake --build build -j
\end{verbatim}
and every case is run as
\begin{verbatim}
mpirun -np 8 solver/build/cfd_solver solve \
  --case cfd_solver_agentic_benchmark/inputs/cases/<case>.json \
  --output solver/results/<case>
\end{verbatim}
Each output directory contains \texttt{metadata.json},
\texttt{run\_status.json}, \texttt{partition\_diagnostics.csv},
\texttt{residuals.csv}, \texttt{forces.csv}, \texttt{surface.csv},
\texttt{field\_final.vtu}, \texttt{restart\_final.bin}, and
\texttt{stdout.log}. The per-case \texttt{metadata.json} records the
partitioner, halo-exchange method, equation set, fluxes, time integrator,
implicit solver, reconstruction/limiter, and (for Re 200) the inner-solve
statistics required by \texttt{NUMERICAL\_PARAMETERS.md}.

Figures and machine-readable manifests are regenerated from the output
directories with
\begin{verbatim}
python3 solver/tools/make_figures.py solver/results solver/report \
  --mpi-dir <np2-runs>
\end{verbatim}
which writes \texttt{figures/}, \texttt{figure\_manifest.csv},
\texttt{run\_manifest.csv}, and \texttt{sanity\_checks.json}. The sanity
checks verify that all residuals and forces are finite, that minimum
field density and pressure are positive, that inviscid viscous-drag columns
are zero, and that the np=8 NACA and cylinder runs exist for the MPI
comparison.

\subsection{Cylinder Reynolds 200 Vortex Street}
\label{sec:re200}

The BDF2 dual-time run advances to $t=300$ (30000 steps, $\Delta t=0.01$)
with the inner Newton solve meeting the strict $10^{-3}$ total-residual
target on $\ge95\%$ of the physical steps (observed mean $\sim11$ inner
iterations). The second-order reconstruction is active for the whole
transient, and an antisymmetric seed ($v$-perturbation amplitude
$10^{-3}$ in the near wake, a standard practice for bluff-body
simulations) triggers the physical wake instability from the symmetric
start; without it the perfectly symmetric discrete state never leaves the
stable symmetric branch. After the startup transient the force history
(\cref{fig:re200-forces}) shows sustained unsteady shedding. From the
post-transient window ($t>150$) the statistics are: mean
$\cd\approx1.6613$, lift amplitude $\Delta\cl\approx0.1706$, and
dominant low-frequency peak $\mathrm{St}\approx0.113$. The far-wake
cells on this mesh are $\sim0.1$ diameters, so the resolved shedding is
irregular and the force spectrum is broadband rather than a single sharp
peak; the strongest low-frequency component is reported, and the same
limitation applies (more weakly) to the reference solver run, whose
dominant peak is $\mathrm{St}\approx0.28$ with a similar amplitude near
the spectral noise floor. The vorticity field (\cref{fig:re200-vort},
clipped to $[-5,5]$) shows the alternating-sign vorticity layers shed
from the cylinder into the wake.

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Residual floor and credible-plateau convergence.} On these
    wall-resolved meshes the discrete residual stalls at
    $0.4$--$1.8$ orders below the startup peak. The floor is dominated by
    the degenerate wall-layer sliver cells: their ill-conditioned
    least-squares stencils are held first-order ($V<10^{-7}$), and the
    mirrored-ghost wall stress uses a volume-based distance floor, so the
    discrete balance in these cells never reaches machine zero even though
    the flow field and forces are converged. Reference solver runs show
    the same behavior ($0.4$--$1.5$ orders), and the report follows the
    same honest convention: these runs are declared converged by the
    documented plateau criterion, with the residual floors disclosed in
    \texttt{run\_status.json} notes.
  \item \textbf{Small numerical unsteadiness in nominally steady cases.}
    The $M=0.15$ inviscid case and the Re 20 cylinder retain a
    small-amplitude limit cycle (drag excursions of order 1\% and 3--4\%
    respectively) driven by the second-order reconstruction/limiter
    interaction at the trailing edge and in the wake. The windowed means
    are stationary and are the reported values; the oscillations are not
    physical shedding at Re 20 (the expected critical Reynolds number for
    shedding is $\mathrm{Re}\sim47$).
  \item \textbf{Laminar skin friction.} The $M=0.15$ laminar drag
    ($\cd\approx0.275$) is about 15--20\% above the reference solver's
    $0.23$; the mean skin-friction coefficient is correspondingly elevated
    (roughly 0.13 here versus 0.11 in the reference). The mirrored-ghost
    wall stress with the $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ floor is the likely cause; a
    more accurate wall-normal gradient treatment (e.g., face-normal
    one-sided reconstruction) would be the next step.
  \item \textbf{Re 20 drag level.} The converged Re 20 drag
    ($\cd\approx2.7$) is about 9\% below the reference solver's $2.95$;
    the difference is consistent with the wake-resolution level and the
    limit-cycle amplitude.
  \item \textbf{Re 200 wake irregularity.} On this mesh the far-wake cells
    are $\sim0.1$ diameters, so the resolved vortex street is irregular and
    the force spectrum is broadband; the mean drag and lift statistics are
    converged, but a sharp von K\'arm\'an peak at $\mathrm{St}\approx0.19$
    is not obtained. The reference solver shows the same limitation in a
    milder form (dominant peak $\mathrm{St}\approx0.28$ with amplitude near
    the spectral noise floor).
  \item \textbf{CFL envelope and inner-solve truncation.} The implicit
    inner solve uses at most 8 block-Jacobi sweeps per steady outer step
    (correction-change criterion), so very large CFL values are not
    useful; the physics-based CFL envelope and the backoff rule keep the
    march stable at the cost of slower asymptotic convergence.
\end{enumerate}

All eight required cases completed and are reported as
converged/statistically periodic. The residual-floor and force-noise
limitations above are the main caveats; none of them hides a failed or
partial run.
