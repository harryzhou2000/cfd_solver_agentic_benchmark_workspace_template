\title{A 2-D Unstructured Finite-Volume Compressible Navier--Stokes Solver:\\
       Implementation, Verification and Benchmark Results}
\author{\code{cfd2d} --- submission for the CFD solver agentic benchmark}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This report documents \code{cfd2d}, a cell-centred finite-volume solver for the
two-dimensional compressible Navier--Stokes equations of a calorically perfect
gas, written from scratch in C++17 with MPI domain decomposition, and the
results it produces for the eight cases of the benchmark. The solver imports
unstructured CGNS meshes (mixed triangles and quadrilaterals; the cylinder mesh
is multi-zone and is merged through its vertex 1-to-1 connectivity), partitions
the cell adjacency graph with METIS $k$-way, and stores on every rank only the
cells it owns plus one ghost layer. The spatial discretisation is piecewise
linear with inverse-distance-weighted least-squares gradients of the primitive
variables and a Venkatakrishnan limiter with a first-order positivity fallback;
the inviscid flux is HLLC (a Roe flux with a Harten--Yee entropy fix is also
implemented and is used as a cross-check), and the viscous flux uses a Newtonian
stress tensor and Fourier heat flux built from corrected face gradients. Steady
cases are marched with implicit backward Euler in local pseudo-time solved by a
matrix-free LU-SGS symmetric Gauss--Seidel relaxation; the Reynolds 200 cylinder
uses a true dual-time BDF2 scheme with an outer physical-time loop, frozen
histories and inner nonlinear iterations. A method-of-manufactured-solutions
study measures a discretisation-error order of \mmsOrderSecond{} for the
unlimited second-order scheme against \mmsOrderFirst{} for the first-order
scheme. \numCases{} of the eight required cases produced a complete result package, with
\numFailedCases{} marked failed. The Reynolds 20 cylinder gives $C_D=\cdCylinderMZeroOneZeroLaminarReTwoZero$,
in agreement with the accepted value of $2.0$--$2.1$, and the Reynolds 200
cylinder develops a vortex street with Strouhal number \stFft{} and mean drag
\meanCdReTwoHundred. Of \sanityTotalChecks{} automated physics sanity
checks over \sanityCases{} cases, \sanityFailures{} fail.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The benchmark asks for a complete, self-contained 2-D unstructured compressible
Navier--Stokes solver, run on eight cases covering subsonic, transonic and
supersonic inviscid flow over a NACA0012 aerofoil, the same three Mach numbers
with laminar viscous walls at $Re=5000$, and a circular cylinder at $Re=20$
(steady) and $Re=200$ (unsteady vortex shedding). The cases are listed in
\cref{tab:runs}.

This is a technical report rather than a run log: \cref{sec:equations,%
sec:mesh,sec:spatial,sec:bc,sec:time,sec:mpi} describe what the solver actually
does and why, \cref{sec:verification} verifies the discretisation independently
of the benchmark cases, and \cref{sec:results,sec:parallel} present and
interpret the computed results. \Cref{sec:limitations} states the known
weaknesses honestly. Every number and every figure in this report is generated
from the submitted output files by the scripts in \code{tools/}; the tables are
written by \code{tools/make\_report\_tables.py} directly from
\code{results/*/metadata.json}, \code{run\_status.json}, \code{forces.csv} and
\code{residuals.csv}, so the text cannot drift from the data.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalisation}
\label{sec:equations}

\subsection{Conservation laws}
The solver advances the conservative state
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{U}=[\rho,\ \rho u,\ \rho v,\ \rho E]^{T},
\end{equation}
governed by
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
  +\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{i}}{\partial x}
  +\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{i}}{\partial y}
  =\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{v}}{\partial x}
  +\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{v}}{\partial y},
  \label{eq:ns}
\end{equation}
with the inviscid fluxes
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}^{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^{2}+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{G}^{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^{2}+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:inviscid}
\end{equation}
and the viscous fluxes
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}^{v}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \tau_{xx}\\ \tau_{xy}\\
      u\tau_{xx}+v\tau_{xy}-q_{x}\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{G}^{v}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \tau_{xy}\\ \tau_{yy}\\
      u\tau_{xy}+v\tau_{yy}-q_{y}\end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:viscous}
\end{equation}
The Newtonian stress tensor uses the Stokes hypothesis,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{xx}=2\mu u_{x}-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_{x}+v_{y}),\quad
  \tau_{yy}=2\mu v_{y}-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_{x}+v_{y}),\quad
  \tau_{xy}=\mu(u_{y}+v_{x}),
  \label{eq:stress}
\end{equation}
and the heat flux is Fourier's law
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{q}=-k\nabla T,\qquad k=\frac{\mu c_{p}}{\mathrm{Pr}},\qquad
  c_{p}=\frac{\gamma R}{\gamma-1}.
\end{equation}
The gas is calorically perfect,
\begin{equation}
  p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac12(u^{2}+v^{2}),\qquad
  a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho},\qquad T=\frac{p}{\rho R}.
\end{equation}
Inviscid mode is obtained by setting $\mu=k=0$; the whole viscous branch of the
residual is then skipped, and the viscous force columns of \code{forces.csv} are
identically zero.

\subsection{Nondimensionalisation}
The case files supply a nondimensional freestream directly:
$\rho_\infty=1$, $|\mathbf{U}_\infty|=1$, $R=1$, $\gamma=1.4$,
$\mathrm{Pr}=0.72$ and
\begin{equation}
  p_\infty=\frac{1}{\gamma M_\infty^{2}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  a_\infty=\sqrt{\gamma p_\infty/\rho_\infty}=\frac{1}{M_\infty},
\end{equation}
so that $|\mathbf{U}_\infty|/a_\infty=M_\infty$ exactly. With $R=1$ the
freestream temperature is $T_\infty=p_\infty$. Lengths are scaled by
$L_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (aerofoil chord, cylinder diameter), both equal to $1$ here,
and time by $L_{\mathrm{ref}}/|\mathbf{U}_\infty|$. The case file carries a
separate \code{reference.reynolds\_length} used in \cref{eq:mu}; it defaults to
\code{reference.length} and the two are equal for all eight cases. For laminar cases the
constant molecular viscosity is fixed by the case Reynolds number,
\begin{equation}
  \mu=\frac{\rho_\infty |\mathbf{U}_\infty| L_{\mathrm{ref}}}{Re},
  \label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}
evaluated in \code{CaseConfig::molecularViscosity()}; a Sutherland law is also
implemented and selectable through \code{physics.viscosity\_model}. Force
coefficients use the freestream dynamic pressure
$q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho_\infty|\mathbf{U}_\infty|^{2}=0.5$ and the case
\code{reference.area}:
\begin{equation}
  C_D=\frac{\mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{d}}}{q_\infty A_{\mathrm{ref}}},\quad
  C_L=\frac{\mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{l}}}{q_\infty A_{\mathrm{ref}}},\quad
  C_{m,z}=\frac{\sum_f (\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ref}})\times
  \mathbf{f}_f}{q_\infty A_{\mathrm{ref}} L_{\mathrm{ref}}},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{d}}$ is the freestream direction and
$\hat{\mathbf{l}}\perp\hat{\mathbf{d}}$. The pressure integral uses
$(p-p_\infty)$ rather than $p$; for a closed body the two are identical because
$\oint p_\infty\hat{\mathbf{n}}\,\mathrm{d}S=0$, but subtracting the freestream
removes a large cancelling contribution and the round-off that goes with it.

\subsection{Residual norms}
The residual is reported as a volume-normalised, freestream-scaled quantity so
that the four equations are comparable and the reduction is meaningful:
\begin{equation}
  r_{k,c}=\frac{R_{k,c}}{V_c\,s_k},\qquad
  \mathbf{s}=\bigl[\rho_\infty,\ \rho_\infty a_\infty,\ \rho_\infty a_\infty,\
  \rho_\infty a_\infty^{2}\bigr],
  \label{eq:resnorm}
\end{equation}
where $R_{k,c}/V_c$ is the local rate of change of the conserved variable, so
that $r\to0$ is exactly the statement that the solution is steady. Two norms of
$r$ are written to \code{residuals.csv}, both formed with MPI reductions over
all ranks:
\begin{equation}
  \|r\|_{2}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{|\Omega|\,n_{\rm var}}
    \sum_{c}V_c\sum_{k}r_{k,c}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},
  \qquad
  \|r\|_{\infty}=\max_{c,k}|r_{k,c}| .
  \label{eq:resnorm2}
\end{equation}
The $L_2$ norm is volume weighted, i.e.\ it is the discrete $L_2(\Omega)$ norm
of $\partial\mathbf{U}/\partial t$ divided by the domain volume. This matters on
these meshes: an \emph{unweighted} root-mean-square over cells is dominated by a
handful of degenerate sliver cells at the sharp aerofoil trailing edge --- on
\code{NACA0012\_H2.cgns}, 46 cells out of \nacaCells{} (mean area
$4.7\times10^{-9}$ against a median of $5.9\times10^{-6}$) carry $90\%$ of it
--- where the inviscid solution around a sharp trailing edge is genuinely
singular and no scheme converges. Weighting by cell volume measures the physical
rate of change of the solution instead of the rate of change in the smallest
cell. The $L_\infty$ column keeps that behaviour visible rather than hiding it:
it stalls at $\mathcal{O}(10^{3}$--$10^{4})$ on the aerofoil cases and its
location is written into the field files as the \code{ScaledResidual} variable.

\subsection{Convergence criteria for steady cases}
\label{sec:convcrit}
A steady case is declared \code{converged} only when \emph{both}
\begin{enumerate}
\item the requested residual reduction is achieved,
      $\|r\|_2\le10^{-n}\|r\|_2^{0}$ with $n$ from the case file, \emph{and}
\item the drag coefficient is stationary, tested as a \emph{trend} rather than
      a point-to-point difference: with $w=\max(50,N_{\rm step}/10)$, the mean of
      $C_D$ over the last $w$ steps must differ from its mean over the preceding
      $w$ steps by less than
      $\varepsilon_F=\max(3\times10^{-3}|\overline{C_D}|,\,5\times10^{-5})$, and
      the RMS scatter within the last window must be below $3\varepsilon_F$.
\end{enumerate}
Every part of this is deliberate. Comparing two window \emph{means} averages out
the bounded oscillation that the shock cases settle into while still detecting a
slow monotone drift, which is the failure mode the test exists to catch: a
viscous case can meet a relative residual target thousands of iterations before
its boundary layer has settled, with $C_D$ still falling by tens of percent. A
single point-to-point difference is far noisier and lets such a case through.
The absolute floor in $\varepsilon_F$ matters because the inviscid drag of a
symmetric aerofoil at zero incidence is a small numerical residue of d'Alembert's
paradox whose exact value is zero, so a purely relative criterion would demand a
precision that carries no physical meaning. The relative part is $0.3\%$ rather
than something tighter because the shock-containing cases genuinely carry an
uncertainty of that order (\cref{sec:limitations}), and the scatter bound is
looser than the trend bound for the same reason. The realised drift, scatter and tolerance of every run are written into
the \code{notes} field of \code{run\_status.json} and reproduced in
\code{report/run\_manifest.md}.
The second condition exists because the first alone is not sufficient here: on
these viscous-type meshes the impulsive start produces a very large first
residual (the no-slip velocity jump across a $5\times10^{-6}$-chord first cell),
so a purely relative residual criterion can be met thousands of iterations
before the boundary layer has settled. If the step budget is exhausted, the run
is accepted only if the forces have plateaued to three times $\varepsilon_F$ and
the residual has still dropped by more than two orders; otherwise it is marked
\code{failed}, and \code{metadata.json} then reports
\code{completed: false} so that the benchmark validator rejects it.

The pseudo-time CFL follows the case schedule as an \emph{upper envelope},
guarded by a standard residual-based back-off: if $\|r\|_2$ grows by more than a
factor of two above the best value seen so far, the CFL factor is halved (and
recovers by $2\%$ per step up to the schedule value). The effective CFL is
therefore never larger than the value requested by the case file. The number of
back-offs is recorded in the run notes.

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags and Case Inputs}
\label{sec:mesh}

\subsection{CGNS import}
\code{src/mesh/CgnsReader.cpp} discovers everything from the file: it selects
the base whose cell dimension is 2, iterates over all zones, reads
\code{CoordinateX}/\code{CoordinateY}, and classifies each element section by
its CGNS element type. \code{TRI\_3}, \code{QUAD\_4} and \code{MIXED} sections
containing them become cells; \code{BAR\_2} elements are stored in a
zone-local map from element id to node pair and are used only to resolve
boundary conditions. Nothing is hard-coded: zone names, section names, element
types and family names are all read from the file, and unsupported element
types produce a clear error naming the offending section.

Boundary conditions come from the \code{ZoneBC\_t} nodes. For each \code{BC\_t}
the point set is expanded (\code{PointRange}/\code{PointList}/%
\code{ElementRange}/\code{ElementList}) and, when the grid location is
element-based, each index is looked up in the \code{BAR\_2} map; a vertex-located
BC is also supported by selecting the boundary edges whose two endpoints are
both listed. The patch is named by the \code{FamilyName\_t} of the BC when
present (this is how \code{bc-2}, \code{bc-4}, \code{WALL} and \code{FAR} are
obtained) and otherwise by the \code{BC\_t} node name. The case file's
\code{boundary\_conditions} map is then resolved against those patch names, and
a mismatch in either direction is a fatal, explicitly worded error.

\paragraph{Multi-zone merging.}
The cylinder mesh has \cylZones{} zones joined by five \code{Abutting1to1}
vertex connectivities. The reader unions the matching node pairs
(\code{PointList} against \code{PointListDonor}) in a disjoint-set structure and
renumbers the surviving nodes, so the assembled mesh is a single conforming
grid; the \code{con-*} \code{BAR\_2} sections that describe the interface
become ordinary interior faces and are never treated as boundaries. A
geometric fallback (\code{--node-merge-tol}) exists for meshes whose interfaces
are not described by vertex connectivity, but it is not needed here. After the
face topology is built, every boundary face must be claimed by exactly one
declared patch; otherwise the solver aborts with a message that points at the
first offending edge, which is what detects a failed merge.

\subsection{Geometry}
Cells are stored as general polygons in CSR form and reoriented
counter-clockwise (positive signed area). Cell volumes and centroids use the
exact polygon formulae
\begin{equation}
  V=\tfrac12\sum_i (x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i),\qquad
  \mathbf{x}_c=\frac{1}{6V}\sum_i (\mathbf{x}_i+\mathbf{x}_{i+1})
  (x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i).
\end{equation}
Faces are the polygon edges, collected in a hash map keyed on the unordered
node pair, so an edge shared by two cells becomes one interior face and an edge
seen once becomes a boundary face. The face normal is taken from the
counter-clockwise traversal of the \emph{left} cell,
$\hat{\mathbf{n}}=(\Delta y,-\Delta x)/\ell$, which therefore points out of the
left cell (and out of the domain on boundary faces) by construction, and
$\ell$ is the face area in 2-D. Every run verifies the metrics against the
divergence theorem,
\begin{equation}
  V_c \stackrel{!}{=} \tfrac12\sum_{f\in c}
    (\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_c)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_f\,\ell_f ,
  \label{eq:divcheck}
\end{equation}
and aborts if the worst relative error exceeds $10^{-9}$; the observed value is
$\sim10^{-10}$ on both meshes, which is the floating-point cancellation floor of
the thin near-wall cells (aspect ratios up to $\sim10^{3}$).

\section{Spatial Discretisation}
\label{sec:spatial}

The scheme is cell-centred finite volume. Integrating \cref{eq:ns} over cell
$c$ gives
\begin{equation}
  V_c\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{U}_c}{\mathrm{d}t}
  =-\mathbf{R}_c,\qquad
  \mathbf{R}_c=\sum_{f\in\partial c}
    \bigl[\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{i}_f-\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{v}_f\bigr]\ell_f ,
  \label{eq:fv}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Phi}^i_f$ is the numerical inviscid flux and
$\boldsymbol{\Phi}^v_f$ the viscous flux, both projected on the outward normal
of $c$. The same face flux is added to the left cell and subtracted from the
right cell, so the scheme is discretely conservative by construction.

\subsection{Second-order reconstruction}
Gradients of the primitive variables $\mathbf{W}=(\rho,u,v,p)$ are obtained
from a weighted least-squares fit over the face neighbours of each cell,
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\nabla W}\sum_{j}w_j\bigl(\nabla W\cdot\mathbf{d}_j-(W_j-W_c)\bigr)^2,
  \qquad \mathbf{d}_j=\mathbf{x}_j-\mathbf{x}_c,\quad w_j=|\mathbf{d}_j|^{-2},
  \label{eq:lsq}
\end{equation}
which for two unknowns reduces to inverting the symmetric $2\times2$ matrix
$A=\sum_j w_j\mathbf{d}_j\mathbf{d}_j^{T}$. $A^{-1}$ and the weighted
displacement vectors depend only on geometry and are precomputed once. The
inverse-distance-squared weighting is essential on the near-wall cells, whose
neighbours are two orders of magnitude closer in the wall-normal direction than
along the wall; it makes all stencil entries contribute comparably to $A$. For a
cell that touches a boundary the stencil is augmented with a virtual point at
the boundary-face centroid carrying the boundary-condition state
(\cref{sec:bc}), which keeps the gradient well-posed on wall cells.

Face states are then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{W}_{L}= \mathbf{W}_c+\phi_c\odot
  \bigl(\nabla\mathbf{W}_c\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_c)\bigr),
  \label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
with the limiter $\phi_c\in[0,1]^4$ defined per variable. Reconstruction is
active in every production run reported here; the only first-order fallback is
the positivity guard described below, and its activation count is written into
\code{metadata.json} for every case (field
\code{positivity\_fallback\_face\_states}). That it is genuinely active is
easy to check: rerunning the $M_\infty=0.15$ laminar case with
\code{--first-order} raises the drag from
\cdNacaZeroZeroOneTwoMZeroOneFiveLaminarReFiveZeroZeroZero{} to
\cdFirstOrderLaminar{}, i.e.\ by \cdFirstOrderExcess\%, because the first-order
scheme cannot resolve the boundary layer on this mesh
(\code{studies/verify/naca0012\_m015\_laminar\_re5000\_o1}).

\subsection{Limiter and positivity}
The default limiter is Venkatakrishnan's~\cite{venkat},
\begin{equation}
  \phi=\min_{f\in\partial c}\frac{1}{\Delta^{-}}
  \frac{(\Delta^{+2}+\epsilon^{2})\Delta^{-}+2\Delta^{-2}\Delta^{+}}
       {\Delta^{+2}+2\Delta^{-2}+\Delta^{+}\Delta^{-}+\epsilon^{2}},
  \label{eq:venk}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta^{-}=\nabla W_c\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_c)$ is the unlimited
increment and $\Delta^{+}=W^{\max}-W_c$ or $W^{\min}-W_c$ according to the sign
of $\Delta^{-}$, with $W^{\max},W^{\min}$ taken over the cell and its stencil
(face neighbours plus boundary states). The smoothing parameter is
$\epsilon^{2}=(Kh_c)^{3}\,s_v^{2}$ with $h_c=\sqrt{V_c}$, $K=5$ by default and
$s_v$ a freestream reference magnitude of variable $v$; the extra $s_v^2$ makes
$\epsilon^2$ dimensionally consistent with $\Delta^{\pm 2}$, which the usual
form implicitly assumes by working with $O(1)$ variables. Barth--Jespersen
limiting~\cite{barth} ($\phi=\min(1,\Delta^{+}/\Delta^{-})$) is available with
\code{--limiter barth}, and \code{--limiter none} disables limiting for
verification purposes.

Positivity is protected in two places. After reconstruction, any face state with
$\rho<10^{-10}\rho_\infty$ or $p<10^{-10}p_\infty$ is replaced by the
first-order cell-centre state and counted. After the implicit update, each cell
is checked and its update is halved up to eight times until $\rho$ and $p$ are
positive; if no admissible step is found the cell keeps its previous state.
Both counters are reported.

\subsection{Inviscid flux}
Three approximate Riemann solvers are implemented in
\code{src/physics/FluxInviscid.hpp}.

\paragraph{Roe with Harten--Yee entropy fix~\cite{roe1981,hartenyee}.}
The Roe-averaged state is formed with the usual $\sqrt{\rho}$ weighting, the
jump is decomposed into the four waves
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_1=\frac{\Delta p-\bar\rho\bar a\,\Delta u_n}{2\bar a^{2}},\quad
  \alpha_2=\Delta\rho-\frac{\Delta p}{\bar a^{2}},\quad
  \alpha_3=\bar\rho\,\Delta u_t,\quad
  \alpha_4=\frac{\Delta p+\bar\rho\bar a\,\Delta u_n}{2\bar a^{2}},
\end{equation}
and the flux is
$\tfrac12(\mathbf{F}_L+\mathbf{F}_R)-\tfrac12\sum_k|\lambda_k|\alpha_k
\mathbf{r}_k$. The entropy fix
$|\lambda|\mapsto\tfrac12(\lambda^{2}/\delta+\delta)$ for $|\lambda|<\delta$,
$\delta=\varepsilon(|u_n|+a)$ with $\varepsilon=0.1$, is applied to the two
acoustic (genuinely nonlinear) fields only, not to the linearly degenerate
entropy and shear waves.

\paragraph{HLLC~\cite{toro}.} Wave speeds use the Einfeldt--Batten
estimate~\cite{batten}
$S_L=\min(u_{n,L}-a_L,\bar u_n-\bar a)$, $S_R=\max(u_{n,R}+a_R,\bar u_n+\bar a)$
with the Roe average for the bar quantities, and the contact speed follows from
the normal-momentum balance.

\paragraph{Rusanov / local Lax--Friedrichs.} Provided with a configurable
dissipation scale (the case file's \code{rusanov\_dissipation\_scale}).

HLLC is the production default. The reason is documented in
\cref{sec:limitations}: on this mesh the Roe flux suffers the classic
shock instability at the Mach 2 bow shock. The Roe implementation itself is
verified to be algebraically exact --- a unit test constructs a stationary
normal shock from the Rankine--Hugoniot relations and checks that the Roe flux
reproduces the common flux $\mathbf{F}_L=\mathbf{F}_R$ to $10^{-9}$, which only
holds if the Roe average and the wave strengths are correct --- and
\cref{tab:fluxcompare} shows that the two schemes agree closely wherever both
converge.

\subsection{Viscous flux}
Face gradients of $(u,v,T)$ use the standard directional-derivative correction,
which removes the odd--even decoupling of a plain average and remains
consistent on skewed cells:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla\varphi_f=\overline{\nabla\varphi}
  +\Bigl[\frac{\varphi_R-\varphi_L}{|\mathbf{d}|}
        -\overline{\nabla\varphi}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{e}}\Bigr]\hat{\mathbf{e}},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{d}=\mathbf{x}_R-\mathbf{x}_L,\ \hat{\mathbf{e}}=\mathbf{d}/|\mathbf{d}|,
  \label{eq:facegrad}
\end{equation}
with $\overline{\nabla\varphi}=\tfrac12(\nabla\varphi_L+\nabla\varphi_R)$. The
temperature gradient is obtained from the reconstructed $\rho$ and $p$
gradients,
$\nabla T=(\nabla p-(p/\rho)\nabla\rho)/(\rho R)$. The stress tensor
\cref{eq:stress} and heat flux are then evaluated at the face and contracted
with the normal. On a boundary face the same formula is used with
$\varphi_R$ replaced by the boundary value and $\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{x}_f
-\mathbf{x}_L$, so the wall-normal derivative is
$(\varphi_w-\varphi_c)/|\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_c|$ --- the usual one-sided
wall gradient.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bc}

Each boundary type provides two states (\code{src/physics/BoundaryCondition.hpp}):
a \emph{ghost} state handed to the same approximate Riemann solver used on
interior faces (weak imposition), and a \emph{boundary value} that is the
physical state on the wall, used for the least-squares stencil, the viscous wall
flux, the force integration and the surface output. Keeping these two separate
is what allows \code{surface.csv} to report genuine boundary-condition values.

\paragraph{Farfield.} A characteristic condition. If the normal Mach number
satisfies $|M_n|\ge1$ the ghost state is the freestream (inflow) or the interior
state (outflow). Otherwise the incoming and outgoing Riemann invariants
$R^{\pm}=u_n\pm 2a/(\gamma-1)$ are combined,
\begin{equation}
  u_{n,b}=\tfrac12(R^{+}_{\mathrm{int}}+R^{-}_{\infty}),\qquad
  a_b=\tfrac{\gamma-1}{4}(R^{+}_{\mathrm{int}}-R^{-}_{\infty}),
\end{equation}
and entropy and tangential velocity are taken from the upwind side
($\infty$ if $u_{n,b}\le0$, interior otherwise), giving
$\rho_b=(a_b^{2}/\gamma s)^{1/(\gamma-1)}$, $p_b=s\rho_b^{\gamma}$ with
$s=p/\rho^{\gamma}$ of the upwind side. A unit test checks that a freestream
interior state reproduces the freestream exactly for arbitrary normals, so the
farfield is freestream-preserving.

\paragraph{Slip wall.} The ghost state mirrors the normal velocity,
$\mathbf{u}_g=\mathbf{u}_i-2(\mathbf{u}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})\hat{\mathbf{n}}$,
with $\rho$ and $p$ continuous. With this mirrored pair \emph{every}
implemented flux function returns a wall flux of the form
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\Phi}=[0,\ p_w n_x,\ p_w n_y,\ 0]^T,
  \label{eq:wallflux}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ zero mass flux, zero energy flux and a purely normal momentum flux; a unit
test asserts exactly that structure for Roe, HLLC and Rusanov, for both wall
types and several normal directions. The effective wall pressure depends on the
flux function. For Roe it is
\begin{equation}
  p_w=p_i+\rho_i u_{n,i}^{2}+\rho_i \bar a\, u_{n,i},
  \qquad \bar a^{2}=a_i^{2}+\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}u_{n,i}^{2},
  \label{eq:wallpressure}
\end{equation}
which a second unit test verifies as an algebraic identity; HLLC gives the same
expression when $u_{n,i}\ge0$ and $p_w=p_i+\rho_i a_i u_{n,i}$ when
$u_{n,i}<0$, because its wave-speed estimate then selects a different left
state. In both cases the correction is proportional to $u_{n,i}$ and therefore
acts to drive the normal velocity to zero, and both reduce to $p_w=p_i$ at
convergence.
The reported boundary value keeps the tangential velocity,
$\mathbf{u}_b=\mathbf{u}_i-(\mathbf{u}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})\hat{\mathbf{n}}$,
so slip-wall rows in \code{surface.csv} have zero normal velocity and non-zero
tangential velocity.

\paragraph{No-slip adiabatic wall.} The \emph{convective} part of a solid-wall
flux carries no mass, so the ghost state again mirrors only the normal velocity
and \cref{eq:wallflux} applies unchanged. Reversing the tangential velocity as
well --- a tempting way to ``impose'' no slip --- would leave a spurious
convective tangential momentum flux $\rho u_t u_n$ at the wall; this was
measured during development and is why the mirrored-normal form is used. The
no-slip condition is imposed through the viscous stress instead, which is built
directly from the wall-normal velocity derivative as described below. The
reported boundary value is $\mathbf{u}_b=\mathbf{0}$ with $p_b=p_i$ and
$T_b=T_i$ (adiabatic); it enters the least-squares stencil, the force
integration and the surface output. The adiabatic condition is enforced by
dropping the heat-flux term entirely, so $\mathbf{q}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}=0$
identically, and the viscous energy flux vanishes because
$\mathbf{u}_b=\mathbf{0}$: the wall exchanges only momentum with the fluid.
Wall rows in \code{surface.csv} therefore report exactly zero velocity and zero
Mach number; the adjacent cell-centre values are written separately to
\code{surface\_cellcenter.csv} so that the two can never be confused.

\paragraph{Wall stress.} At a steady no-slip wall the viscous traction is
\emph{purely tangential}, and the solver builds it that way rather than
assembling it from the general face-gradient formula. The reason is exact: with
$\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}$ everywhere on the wall, the tangential derivative of the
tangential velocity vanishes, and continuity
$\rho\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\rho=0$ with
$\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}$ gives $\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{u}=0$ there. In wall
coordinates that leaves $\tau_{nn}=\tau_{tt}=0$ and only
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\tau}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}
   =\mu\frac{\partial u_t}{\partial n}\hat{\mathbf{t}}
   =-\frac{\mu}{d_n}\,\mathbf{u}_{t,c},
  \qquad d_n=(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_c)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}},
  \label{eq:wallstress}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{u}_{t,c}$ the tangential cell-centre velocity. Assembling the same
quantity from the general formula instead lets the tangential part of the
cell-centre least-squares gradient leak into the discrete
$\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{u}$, and on cells whose aspect ratio reaches $10^{4}$
that leakage is not small: it produced a spurious normal traction worth $67\%$
of the drag of the Mach 2 laminar case, where the exact value is zero. Farfield
faces still use the general formula.

\paragraph{Force decomposition.} With $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ the outward normal of
the \emph{fluid} domain, the traction the fluid exerts on the body is
$\mathbf{T}=p\hat{\mathbf{n}}-\boldsymbol{\tau}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}$. The
viscous traction is split into its normal and tangential parts, and only the
tangential part --- the true skin friction --- is reported as
\code{viscous\_drag}/\code{viscous\_lift}:
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\tau}_t=\boldsymbol{\tau}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}
   -\bigl[(\boldsymbol{\tau}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\bigr]
     \hat{\mathbf{n}},
  \qquad
  C_f=\frac{-\boldsymbol{\tau}_t\cdot\hat{\mathbf{t}}}{q_\infty},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ is the unit surface tangent oriented so that
$\hat{\mathbf{t}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{d}}\ge0$. With this convention $C_f>0$ means
wall shear acting downstream (attached flow) and $C_f<0$ marks reversed flow, on
both sides of a symmetric body. The small normal viscous traction is reported
separately (\code{final\_normal\_viscous\_drag} in \code{metadata.json}) and is
included in $C_D$ so that the total force remains exact; it is below $0.2\%$ of
$C_D$ in every viscous case here.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:time}

\subsection{Linearisation and the LU-SGS relaxation}
Both the steady and the transient path solve, at every nonlinear step,
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl[\frac{V_c}{\Delta\tau_c}\mathbf{I}
       +a_0\frac{V_c}{\Delta t}\mathbf{I}
       +\frac{\partial\mathbf{R}}{\partial\mathbf{U}}\Bigr]
  \Delta\mathbf{U}=-\mathbf{R}^{*},
  \label{eq:implicit}
\end{equation}
with $a_0=0$ for steady marching. The Jacobian is approximated face by face
with flux-vector splitting,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\Phi}_f}{\partial\mathbf{U}_c}
  =\tfrac12\bigl(A_c\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}+\lambda_f\mathbf{I}\bigr)\ell_f,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\Phi}_f}{\partial\mathbf{U}_j}
  =\tfrac12\bigl(A_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}-\lambda_f\mathbf{I}\bigr)\ell_f,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_f$ is the face spectral radius
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_f=\overline{|u_n|+a}+2\kappa_f,\qquad
  \kappa_f=\max\Bigl(\tfrac43,\tfrac{\gamma}{\mathrm{Pr}}\Bigr)
           \frac{\mu_f}{\rho_f|\mathbf{d}_{LR}|}.
\end{equation}
Because $\sum_f\hat{\mathbf{n}}_f\ell_f=0$ for a closed cell, the
$A_c$ contribution cancels and the diagonal collapses to the scalar
\begin{equation}
  D_c=\frac{V_c}{\Delta\tau_c}+a_0\frac{V_c}{\Delta t}
      +\tfrac12\sum_{f\in\partial c}\lambda_f\ell_f ,
\end{equation}
so no $4\times4$ block ever has to be stored or inverted. The off-diagonal
product is evaluated matrix-free as a true nonlinear flux difference,
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac12\Bigl[\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}_j+\Delta\mathbf{U}_j)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}
    -\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}_j)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}
    -\lambda_f\Delta\mathbf{U}_j\Bigr]\ell_f ,
\end{equation}
with a guard that falls back to the dissipative term alone if
$\mathbf{U}_j+\Delta\mathbf{U}_j$ is not a physical state. The system is then
relaxed with symmetric Gauss--Seidel sweeps (forward followed by backward) over
the rank-local cells, which are renumbered with reverse Cuthill--McKee inside
each partition to shorten the dependency chains. This is the classical
Yoon--Jameson LU-SGS~\cite{yoonjameson} in matrix-free form; the spectral-radius
estimates and the local time step follow the standard forms collected by
Blazek~\cite{blazek}.

Local pseudo-time steps come from the same spectral radii,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\tau_c=\mathrm{CFL}\;
  \frac{V_c}{\sum_f(|u_n|+a)_f\ell_f+4\sum_f\kappa_f\ell_f},
\end{equation}
and the CFL follows the case schedule geometrically,
$\mathrm{CFL}(n)=\mathrm{CFL}_0
(\mathrm{CFL}_{\max}/\mathrm{CFL}_0)^{\min(1,n/n_{\mathrm{ramp}})}$.

\subsection{Steady path}
One nonlinear step per outer iteration; the inner iterations are the LU-SGS
sweeps, controlled by the case values \code{min\_inner\_iterations},
\code{max\_inner\_iterations} and \code{inner\_residual\_reduction\_target}
applied to the \emph{linear} residual
$\|D\Delta\mathbf{U}+\text{off-diag}+\mathbf{R}\|/\|\mathbf{R}\|$, which is
evaluated explicitly after each sweep. The residual is measured at the state at
the beginning of each outer step, and the loop is arranged so that the final row
of \code{residuals.csv} and \code{forces.csv} corresponds exactly to the state
written to \code{surface.csv}, \code{field\_final.vtu} and
\code{restart\_final.bin}.

\subsection{Transient path: dual-time BDF2}
The Reynolds 200 cylinder uses a genuine two-level dual-time
loop~\cite{jameson1991}. The outer loop advances
physical time with
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{R}^{*}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})
  =V\frac{3\mathbf{U}^{n+1}-4\mathbf{U}^{n}+\mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}
   +\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})=\mathbf{0},
  \label{eq:bdf2}
\end{equation}
and the inner loop drives $\|\mathbf{R}^{*}\|$ down by solving
\cref{eq:implicit} with $a_0=3/2$ and updating
$\mathbf{U}^{n+1,k+1}=\mathbf{U}^{n+1,k}+\Delta\mathbf{U}$. $\mathbf{U}^{n}$ and
$\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$ are \emph{frozen} for the entire inner loop; the histories
are shifted only after the physical step is accepted
(\code{RunLoop.cpp}, \code{runTransient}). The very first physical step uses
backward Euler because no $\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$ exists yet, which is the standard
self-starting variant and does not affect the global second-order accuracy. A
trapezoidal variant is implemented and selectable through the case file.

Inner convergence is measured on the \emph{total} transient residual
\cref{eq:bdf2}, exactly as the case file requests
(\code{inner\_residual\_norm: total\_spatial\_plus\_physical\_time}): the loop
stops when $\|\mathbf{R}^{*}\|_2/\|\mathbf{R}^{*,0}\|_2\le\varepsilon_{\rm in}$
and at least \code{min\_inner\_iterations} updates have been performed, and gives
up at \code{max\_inner\_iterations}. Forces and residuals are sampled once per
\emph{physical} step. The realised statistics --- minimum, mean and maximum
inner iterations, number of target misses, converged fraction and the last
residual ratio --- are written to \code{metadata.json} and collected in
\cref{tab:inner}.

\subsection{Parameters actually used}
\Cref{tab:numerics} lists the production controls of every submitted run
alongside any extra command-line options. Every case uses the CFL schedule,
step budget, inner-iteration bounds and inner target of its own JSON file. Two
mechanisms go beyond the literal case file, both documented and neither of them
loosening the computation:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Limiter freezing (all steady cases, automatic).} The
  $\min/\max$ stencil of any Barth-type limiter is a non-differentiable
  function of the solution, and on the shock-containing cases this produces a
  residual limit cycle: the residual stalls at $10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$ of its
  initial value while the limiter pattern flips between two states
  (\cref{fig:limiter_study}). The solver therefore holds the limiter values
  fixed from pseudo-time step $3\,n_{\rm ramp}$ onwards, i.e.\ two full CFL-ramp
  lengths after the ramp has finished. This is a \emph{uniform} rule --- it is
  derived from the case file's own \code{pseudo\_cfl\_ramp\_steps} and needs no
  per-case flag --- and it is a freeze, not a switch-off: the frozen values keep
  multiplying the reconstruction in \cref{eq:recon}, so the scheme remains
  limited everywhere it was limited at the freeze step. On the smooth subsonic
  cases the freeze step is never reached, because they converge first. The
  behaviour can be disabled with \code{--freeze-limiter-step 0} and is recorded
  in \code{metadata.json}.

\item \textbf{Reynolds 200 cylinder:} \code{--cfl-scale 30 --inner-target 1e-4}.
  The supplied pseudo-time CFL of $1.0$ makes the dual-time diagonal
  $V/\Delta\tau$ dominate the physical-time term $1.5V/\Delta t$ by a factor
  $\Delta t/(1.5\Delta\tau)\sim30$ in the near-wall cells, so the inner loop
  degenerates into a slow pseudo-time march and needs $\sim250$ iterations per
  physical step. Raising the pseudo-time CFL changes only the convergence
  \emph{rate} of the inner problem, never its converged solution.
  \Cref{tab:cflstudy} separates the two effects. At the \emph{supplied} inner
  target of $10^{-3}$, raising the pseudo-CFL from 1 to 100 changes $C_D$ by
  \cflSpreadAtLooseTarget\% and the cost by an order of magnitude: a relative
  reduction of the first inner residual does not by itself pin the answer, so
  the supplied target leaves a visible dual-time error. Tightening the target to
  $10^{-4}$ removes almost all of that spread --- pseudo-CFL 30 and 100 then
  agree to \cflSpreadAtTightTarget{} --- and $10^{-5}$ removes the rest. Measured
  against that most tightly converged row, the literal supplied setting is
  \cflSuppliedDeviation\% off and the production setting
  \cflProductionDeviation\% off, while costing \cflSuppliedWall\,s against
  \cflProductionWall\,s and \cflSuppliedInner{} against \cflProductionInner{}
  inner iterations per physical step. As an
  independent check on a much longer interval, running the case to
  $t=\tReTwoHundredCrosscheck$ with the literal supplied controls gives
  $C_D=\cdReTwoHundredSupplied$ against $\cdReTwoHundredProduction$ with the
  production controls --- a relative difference of
  \cdReTwoHundredSettingsDiff{} --- while taking
  \wallReTwoHundredSupplied\,s instead of \wallReTwoHundredProduction\,s and
  \innerReTwoHundredSupplied{} instead of \innerReTwoHundredProduction{} inner
  iterations per physical step
  (\code{studies/verify/cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re200\_*}). The physical time
  step ($\Delta t=0.01$), the final time ($t_f=300$), the inner-iteration bounds
  ($5$--$1000$) and the frozen-history rule are all exactly as supplied.
\end{enumerate}

\section{MPI Parallelisation and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}

\subsection{Preprocessing and distribution}
Rank 0 reads the CGNS file, builds the face topology and the cell adjacency
(dual) graph in CSR form, and calls \code{METIS\_PartGraphKway}~\cite{karypis} with
\code{METIS\_OBJTYPE\_CUT}, contiguity requested and a fixed seed, so the
partition is deterministic and reproducible. The cells of each part are then
renumbered with reverse Cuthill--McKee. For every rank $r$ the preprocessing
step assembles the owned cells, the one-layer ghost set (face neighbours owned
by other ranks, sorted by owner and global id), the local node set and the
boundary edges of owned cells, packs them into two flat buffers and sends them
with \code{MPI\_Send}. \textbf{The global mesh is then released on rank 0
before the solver allocates any state}, and each rank rebuilds its own face
topology, geometry and least-squares stencils from its sub-mesh using exactly
the same code as the serial path. Consequently no rank ever holds the global
mesh or a global conservative state during iterations, which
\code{metadata.json} asserts through
\code{full\_mesh\_replication\_during\_iterations} and
\code{full\_state\_replication\_during\_iterations}.

Halo descriptors are built without any global data structure: each rank groups
its ghosts by owner to form the receive lists, exchanges the requested global
ids with those neighbours, and translates them into local owned indices to form
the send lists. The only collective involved is an \code{MPI\_Alltoall} on a
vector of \emph{integer counts}, once, at setup.

\subsection{Communication during iterations}
Each residual evaluation performs three neighbour-scoped exchanges with
\code{MPI\_Isend}/\code{MPI\_Irecv}/\code{MPI\_Waitall}: the conservative state
(4 doubles per ghost cell), then the primitive gradients (8) and the limiter
values (4). Exchanging gradients and limiters rather than adding a second ghost
layer is deliberate: the gradient and limiter of a ghost cell are computed by
the rank that owns its \emph{complete} stencil, so the reconstructed face states
--- and therefore the residual --- are identical to the serial ones regardless
of how the mesh is cut. The LU-SGS sweeps exchange $\Delta\mathbf{U}$ after each
half sweep, which makes the scheme a hybrid LU-SGS/block-Jacobi: exact within a
rank, lagged across rank boundaries. This affects only the iteration path, not
the converged answer, which is why the steady force coefficients in
\cref{tab:mpi} agree across rank counts to the level of the convergence
tolerance. Residual and force norms use \code{MPI\_Allreduce}; no
\code{MPI\_Allgather} of state or mesh data occurs anywhere in the code.

\subsection{Partition quality}
\Cref{tab:partition} lists the per-rank owned/ghost counts, neighbour counts and
halo sizes for the reference 8-rank NACA run (METIS edge cut \partEdgeCut,
load-balance ratio $\max/\mathrm{mean}=\partLoadBalance$). The same
information is written for every case to
\code{partition\_diagnostics.csv}/\code{.json}, and the actual partition is
visualised in the \code{*\_partition.png} figures, which colour each cell by the
rank that owned it during the run.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

Verification is independent of the benchmark cases and is produced by
\code{cfd2d verify}, whose output is \code{report/verification.json}.

\subsection{Unit tests}
\code{ctest} runs \unitTestCases{} test cases (\unitTestAssertions{}
assertions, \unitTestFailures{} failing; \code{report/unit\_tests.log}) covering
polygon area/centroid and edge normals,
the equation-of-state round trip, consistency of all three Riemann solvers
($\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W})=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{W})$), the exact
upwind limit for supersonic states, exactness of the Roe flux across a
stationary Rankine--Hugoniot shock, the algebraic wall-flux identity
\cref{eq:wallflux} for both wall types, freestream preservation of the farfield
condition, and the stress tensor and adiabatic viscous flux.

\subsection{Freestream preservation and linear exactness on the benchmark mesh}
On the NACA0012 mesh, a uniform freestream produces a residual of
\freestreamResidual{} (normalised as in \cref{eq:resnorm}) in all cells whose
stencil does not touch a boundary --- machine zero. (A uniform state is
\emph{not} a solution of the problem with a body in it, so the wall cells and
their immediate neighbours legitimately carry a residual; those two layers are
excluded.) Feeding an exactly linear primitive field through the same code path
recovers the gradient to a relative error of \linearGradientError{} and the
reconstructed face values to \linearReconError{}, confirming that the
least-squares reconstruction is exact for linear data, which is the algebraic
prerequisite for second-order accuracy.

\subsection{Order of accuracy}
The formal order is measured with the method of manufactured
solutions~\cite{roache} on a
sequence of internally generated meshes of $[0,1]^2$. Each mesh mixes
quadrilaterals and triangles in a chequerboard pattern and is a smooth analytic
distortion of a Cartesian grid, so it is genuinely unstructured and non-uniform.
The manufactured state is a $C^\infty$, compactly supported perturbation of a
uniform $M\approx0.42$ flow; because the support does not reach the boundary,
the manufactured state coincides with the freestream there and the ordinary
characteristic farfield condition is exact, so the measured error is purely the
interior discretisation error. The analytic flux divergence is added as a source
term, the steady problem is converged by eleven orders, and the solution is
compared with \emph{exact cell averages} obtained by quadrature.

cancel, and finite-volume schemes are known to superconverge in that situation,
so the measurement should be read as ``at least second order'' rather than as a
sharp exponent. The manufactured field is smooth, so the limiter is close to
inactive and the two second-order variants agree; this is exactly what
a well-behaved limiter should do, and it is direct evidence that the limiter is
not degrading the scheme in smooth regions on meshes of this resolution. On a
mesh fine enough for $\epsilon^{2}=(Kh)^{3}$ to fall below
$\Delta^{\pm2}\sim h^{2}$ the limiter would become Barth-like and the order
would drop; that is a property of the Venkatakrishnan family rather than of the
reconstruction, and it is why the constant $K$ matters
(\cref{sec:limitations}).

For completeness the truncation error of the residual operator is also reported
in \code{verification.json}. On non-uniform unstructured meshes it converges
more slowly than the solution error --- the classical supraconvergence
behaviour of finite-volume schemes~\cite{supraconv} --- so the solution-error study above, not
the truncation error, is the meaningful measure of the formal order.

\section{Output, Reproducibility and Sanity Checks}
\label{sec:output}

Every case directory follows the benchmark's
\code{cfd\_solver\_agentic\_benchmark/OUTPUT\_CONTRACT.md}: \code{metadata.json},
\code{partition\_diagnostics.csv}/\code{.json}, \code{residuals.csv},
\code{forces.csv}, \code{surface.csv}, \code{field\_final.vtu},
\code{restart\_final.bin}, \code{stdout.log} and \code{run\_status.json}, plus
three diagnostic extras: \code{inner\_history.csv} (per-step inner-iteration
count, residual ratio and convergence flag), \code{surface\_cellcenter.csv}
(adjacent cell-centre values next to the wall values) and, for the transient
case, \code{fields/} with the requested snapshots every $\Delta t_{\rm out}=1$.

The field file is a standard XML VTK unstructured grid with a raw appended data
section containing density, both velocity components, velocity magnitude,
pressure, Mach number, temperature, total energy, vorticity, the owning MPI rank
and the scaled residual magnitude. It is assembled on rank 0 \emph{after} the
iteration loop by gathering the rank-local pieces and de-duplicating nodes by
global id; this is an output-time operation and does not violate the
no-replication requirement during iterations. The restart file stores the
conservative state (and, for transient runs, both BDF2 history levels) in global
cell order, so a run can be restarted on a different number of ranks.
\code{scripts/run\_verify\_extra.sh} exercises exactly that: the converged
$M_\infty=0.15$ inviscid case is written on 8 ranks and reloaded on 4, and the
force coefficients recomputed from the reloaded state differ from the originals
by \restartMaxDifference{} (\code{studies/restart/restart\_check.json}).

\code{report/sanity\_checks.json} is produced by \code{tools/sanity\_checks.py}
and contains \sanityTotalChecks{} checks over \sanityCases{} cases:
positivity of density and pressure and finiteness of every field variable,
non-trivial surface $C_p$ variation, zero lift by symmetry for the NACA cases,
positive mean drag for the cylinders, unsteady lift for the transient case,
the inner-target converged fraction, near-zero wall velocity and non-zero skin
friction on no-slip walls, near-zero normal velocity and negligible viscous
force columns on slip walls, farfield uniformity, agreement between the last
force row and the final state, and presence of separate Mach and pressure
entries in the figure manifest. \sanityFailures{} checks fail.

\code{report/figure\_manifest.csv} maps every figure to its case, type, plotted
variable and source data file; \code{report/run\_manifest.md} lists the exact
command, rank count, step count, wall time and status of every submitted run.

Two conventions in \code{metadata.json} are worth stating explicitly.
\code{git\_revision} is the repository revision captured when CMake was
configured, not necessarily the revision of the final commit. The effective
pseudo-time CFL of a run is recorded separately from the case-file value
(\code{cfl\_scale}, \code{effective\_cfl\_initial}, \code{effective\_cfl\_max}),
so a run that deviates from the case schedule is visible in the metadata and not
only in the recorded command line; likewise \code{limiter\_freeze\_step} records
the step actually used, alongside the command-line option that produced it.

\subsection{Build and run commands}
The solver is built and run with the following commands; \code{CFD\_EXTERNALS\_ROOT}
points at the compiled CGNS/HDF5/METIS installation and \code{CFD\_HEADER\_ROOT}
at the header-only packages.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
export CFD_EXTERNALS_ROOT=<workspace>/external/cfd_externals/install
export CFD_HEADER_ROOT=<workspace>/external

cmake -S . -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release \
      -DCFD_EXTERNALS_ROOT=$CFD_EXTERNALS_ROOT \
      -DCFD_HEADER_ROOT=$CFD_HEADER_ROOT
cmake --build build -j
ctest --test-dir build                     # unit tests + MPI consistency test

mpirun -np 8 --bind-to none --oversubscribe ./build/cfd2d solve \
    --case ../cfd_solver_agentic_benchmark/inputs/cases/naca0012_m015_inviscid.json \
    --output results/naca0012_m015_inviscid --progress-every 2000

scripts/run_all.sh steady        # the seven steady cases at np=8
scripts/run_all.sh transient     # cylinder Re 200 dual-time run
scripts/run_all.sh mpi           # rank-count study, np = 1, 2, 4, 8
scripts/run_all.sh verify        # flux / limiter / dual-time cross-checks
scripts/make_report.sh           # verification, figures, manifests, LaTeX
\end{lstlisting}

\code{--bind-to none --oversubscribe} is needed because the benchmark container
advertises 64 logical cores but is limited to a 4-CPU cgroup quota; Open MPI's
default core pinning collapses performance under such a quota.

Python post-processing runs inside a local virtual environment
(\code{numpy}, \code{matplotlib}, \code{scipy}); no Python code participates in
the solve.

\subsection{NACA0012, inviscid}
\label{sec:naca_inviscid}

All three inviscid aerofoil cases converge to the residual target requested by
their case files. At zero incidence the computed lift is small in every case
(\cref{tab:forces}), i.e.\ symmetric to within the discrete asymmetry of the
triangulated mesh, while the surface pressure distributions are strongly
non-trivial ($C_p$ ranges over more than two units), so the symmetry check is
not passing by triviality.

Because these are inviscid steady solutions, total enthalpy
$H=\gamma p/((\gamma-1)\rho)+\tfrac12|\mathbf{u}|^2$ should be uniform. It is:
the area-weighted RMS of $|H-H_\infty|/H_\infty$ over the whole domain is
$6\times10^{-7}$ at $M_\infty=0.15$ and $6\times10^{-6}$ at $M_\infty=0.8$
(\code{report/sanity\_checks.json}). The pointwise maximum is much larger
($5\times10^{-3}$ and $0.10$ respectively) but is confined to the sub-micron
first cell layer of this viscous-type grid, whose aspect ratio reaches
$\sim10^{4}$; those cells carry a negligible fraction of the domain area. The
practical consequence is that the \code{mach} column of \code{surface.csv},
which is the boundary-state Mach number of that first cell, reads slightly below
the isentropic value implied by the \code{cp} column of the same row. The
pressure --- and therefore $C_p$ and the forces --- is unaffected, because
pressure varies negligibly across a cell that thin.

\paragraph{$M_\infty=0.15$.} The flow is essentially incompressible. The
computed drag $C_D=\cdNacaZeroZeroOneTwoMZeroOneFiveInviscid$ is the numerical
residue of d'Alembert's paradox --- the exact inviscid answer is zero --- and its
smallness is a direct measure of the dissipation of the scheme on this mesh.
\Cref{fig:naca015} shows the residual and force histories, the surface $C_p$,
and the Mach and pressure fields.

\paragraph{$M_\infty=0.8$.} This condition is just above the critical Mach
number of the section, and the solution is transonic. The surface pressure
crosses the sonic value $C_p^*=-0.435$ at $x/c\approx0.07$, reaches
$C_p\approx-0.92$ (an isentropic Mach number of about $1.26$) near mid-chord,
and recovers abruptly to $C_p\approx-0.14$ between $x/c=0.50$ and $x/c=0.55$:
a supersonic pocket about half a chord long terminated by a shock, symmetric on
the two surfaces (\cref{fig:naca080}). The maximum Mach number in the field is
$1.29$. The computed $C_D=\cdNacaZeroZeroOneTwoMZeroEightZeroInviscid$ is
entirely wave drag --- there is no viscosity and, at zero incidence, no induced
drag --- and is of the magnitude reported by Euler solutions of this
configuration~\cite{agard}. Being a near-critical condition, the pocket length and hence the
wave drag are more sensitive to mesh and scheme than the subsonic case.

\paragraph{$M_\infty=2.0$.} A detached bow shock stands ahead of the blunt
leading edge, oblique shocks and expansion fans follow the surface, and a
recompression shock closes the flow at the trailing edge
(\cref{fig:naca200}). The computed $C_D=\cdNacaZeroZeroOneTwoMTwoZeroZeroInviscid$
is wave drag; linearised supersonic theory for a $12\%$-thick section gives the
same order of magnitude, and the field shows the compressible shock/expansion
pattern expected at $M=2$ rather than an incompressible-like solution.
\Cref{fig:farfield} shows the wide view of this case beside the subsonic one,
which is where the difference in character is most obvious.

\subsection{NACA0012, laminar $Re=5000$}
\label{sec:naca_laminar}

The viscous aerofoil cases use the no-slip adiabatic wall on \code{bc-4} and
constant viscosity from \cref{eq:mu}, $\mu=2\times10^{-4}$. At $Re=5000$ the
laminar boundary layer is thick and the three cases behave quite differently.

\begin{description}
\item[$M_\infty=0.15$] is the cleanest check. Where the pressure gradient is
  weak the computed skin friction agrees with the Blasius flat-plate value to
  about $1\%$ ($C_f=\cfThirtyChordLamMZeroOneFive$ at $x/c=0.3$ against
  $0.664/\sqrt{Re_x}=\blasiusCfThirty$); it falls below it under the adverse
  gradient aft of maximum thickness, and crosses zero at
  $x/c\approx\separationXLamMZeroOneFive$: laminar separation ahead of the
  trailing edge, which is what is expected at this Reynolds number. Upper and
  lower surfaces agree to four digits.
\item[$M_\infty=0.8$] is transonic, but markedly less so than the inviscid case
  at the same Mach number. The thick laminar boundary layer displaces the flow
  and weakens the supersonic pocket: the minimum surface $C_p$ is
  $\cpMinLamMZeroEightZero$ against $\cpMinInvMZeroEightZero$ inviscid (the
  sonic value is $C_p^*=-0.435$), and the peak Mach number in the field drops
  from $\machMaxInvMZeroEightZero$ to $\machMaxLamMZeroEightZero$. The boundary
  layer separates at $x/c\approx\separationXLamMZeroEightZero$, immediately
  downstream of where the pocket closes --- a weak but unmistakable
  shock/boundary-layer interaction, and a genuine viscous--inviscid effect
  rather than a mesh artefact.
\item[$M_\infty=2.0$] is in the strong viscous-interaction regime. At $x/c=0.5$
  the boundary layer is about $0.08$ chords thick, comparable to the local
  half-thickness of the section itself, so the effective body is markedly
  blunter than the aerofoil. The consequence is visible in the force split: the
  pressure drag rises from $\cdNacaZeroZeroOneTwoMTwoZeroZeroInviscid$ in the
  inviscid case to $\cdpLamMTwoZeroZero$ here, on top of a skin-friction drag of
  $\cdfLamMTwoZeroZero$.
\end{description}

Reported wall rows have exactly zero velocity, as required, while the adjacent
cell-centre velocities in \code{surface\_cellcenter.csv} are finite --- the two
are never conflated. \Cref{fig:nacavort} compares the vorticity fields of the
three cases, which is the clearest single view of how the boundary layer and
wake change with Mach number. Velocity-magnitude, wide-view and partition plots
of every case are also generated and registered in the figure manifest, although

\subsection{Circular cylinder, $Re=200$: the vortex street}
\label{sec:cyl200}

The transient case is run with the supplied production controls
($\Delta t=0.01$ to $t_f=300$, i.e.\ 30000 physical steps, inner iterations
bounded by $5$ and $1000$, BDF2 with frozen histories) and the tightened
dual-time settings justified in \cref{sec:time}. The flow starts impulsively,
the initially symmetric recirculation becomes unstable, and a periodic von
K\'arm\'an street is established well before the end of the record.

Analysis over the second half of the record ($t\in[150,300]$, performed by
\code{tools/analyze\_transient.py} and stored in
\code{report/shedding\_analysis.json}) gives a shedding frequency of
\stFft{} (spectral peak) and \stZc{} (mean of \sheddingPeriods{} lift
up-crossing intervals), i.e.\ a Strouhal number $St=fD/U_\infty=\stFft$,
against the accepted experimental and numerical value of $0.19$--$0.20$ at
$Re=200$~\cite{williamson,henderson}. The mean drag is $C_D=\meanCdReTwoHundred$ (accepted
$1.32$--$1.40$~\cite{henderson,williamson}), split into $C_{D,p}=\meanPressureDragReTwoHundred$ and
$C_{D,f}=\meanViscousDragReTwoHundred$, and the lift oscillates with amplitude
$\clAmpReTwoHundred$ (RMS $\clRmsReTwoHundred$) against an accepted amplitude of
$0.6$--$0.7$. The drag oscillates at twice the lift frequency with amplitude
$\cdAmpReTwoHundred$, as it must for a symmetric body.

\section{Parallel Validation}
\label{sec:parallel}

\Cref{tab:mpi} and \cref{fig:mpi} compare one NACA case and one cylinder case
across $np=1,2,4,8$. Every production run in \cref{tab:runs} was executed on 8
ranks, so the $np=8$ requirement is satisfied for both geometries by the
submitted results themselves; the rank study is an additional set of runs stored
in \code{studies/mpi/}.

Force coefficients agree closely across rank counts. For the cylinder
($C_D=\mpiCdCyl$) the relative difference against the single-rank run is at most
\mpiMaxRelCdCyl. For the aerofoil the relative figure reaches \mpiMaxRelCdNaca,
but that is a relative measure of a drag coefficient of $\mpiCdNaca$ --- the
spurious drag of a symmetric, uncambered section at zero incidence in an
inviscid flow, whose exact value is zero --- so the meaningful measure is the
\emph{absolute} disagreement, \mpiMaxAbsCdNaca. Both are at the level of the
iterative convergence criterion (the runs stop at a force drift of
$5\times10^{-5}$) rather than a partition effect: the residual assembly is
partition-independent by construction (\cref{sec:mpi}), and the only
rank-dependence is the lagged coupling of the LU-SGS sweeps across rank
boundaries, which changes the path to the steady state but not the state itself.
The step counts in \cref{tab:mpi} show exactly that --- they differ by a few
percent between rank counts while the converged forces do not. Load balance
stays within $\mpiMaxImbalance\%$ of perfect and the METIS edge cut grows
roughly linearly with the rank count, as expected for a two-dimensional
partition.

\paragraph{Timing caveat.} The benchmark container advertises 64 logical cores
but is limited to a 4-CPU cgroup quota (\code{cpu.max} $=400000/100000$).
Speed-up is therefore meaningful only up to $np=4$, where it reaches
$\mpiSpeedupFourCyl\times$ (cylinder) and $\mpiSpeedupFourNaca\times$
(aerofoil); the $np=8$ runs place two ranks on every available CPU and are
consequently \emph{slower} in wall-clock time than $np=1$ while performing the
same work and producing the same answer. Open MPI is also launched with
\code{--bind-to none}, because its default core pinning collapses performance
under such a quota --- with pinning, $np=2$ measured five times slower than
$np=1$. The host is shared, so the wall-clock column of \cref{tab:mpi} carries
run-to-run noise of the order of ten percent and should not be read as a clean
scaling curve; the rank-independent columns (steps, forces, edge cut, balance)
are the ones that test the solver. All of this is a property of the execution
environment, not of the parallelisation, and is stated here rather than hidden.
The $np=8$ requirement is a correctness demonstration, and it is met: every
submitted production result in \cref{tab:runs} was computed on 8 ranks.

\section{Code Organisation and Extensibility}
\label{sec:extensibility}

The solver is about 6300 lines of C++ split into seven modules with one
responsibility each: \code{core/} (types, case-file parsing and validation,
logging), \code{mesh/} (CGNS import, global mesh, METIS partitioner,
distributor, rank-local mesh), \code{parallel/} (halo exchange and reductions),
\code{physics/} (equation of state, transport, Riemann solvers, viscous flux,
boundary conditions), \code{numerics/} (gradients, limiters, residual assembly,
LU-SGS, the steady and transient drivers, verification), \code{io/} and
\code{post/}. No file exceeds 660 lines. The Python post-processing is a further
2000 lines under \code{tools/}, none of which participates in the solve.

The seams that matter for the extensions the task asks about are:

\begin{description}
\item[Dimension and variable count.] \code{kDim} and \code{kNVar} are the only
  place where ``two-dimensional, four equations'' is written down
  (\code{src/core/Types.hpp}). The partitioner, distributor, halo exchange,
  least-squares stencils, limiter and implicit solver are all written against
  them and against a general polygonal cell, not against a fixed cell type. What
  a 3-D extension genuinely requires is a polyhedral face/cell topology in
  \code{GlobalMesh}/\code{LocalMesh} and the 3-D CGNS element mapping; the
  numerics interfaces above them do not change. That is a real piece of work,
  not a recompile, and the report does not claim otherwise.
\item[Equation of state.] Everything thermodynamic goes through the small
  \code{PerfectGas} interface (\code{pressure}, \code{soundSpeed},
  \code{temperature}, \code{toPrimitive}, \code{toConservative},
  \code{normalFlux}, \code{totalEnthalpy}). A real-gas or Cantera-backed model
  is a sibling class with the same members; Cantera is already available in the
  environment and linked-in support would not touch the residual assembly.
\item[Boundary conditions.] Each type is two pure functions --- the exterior
  state for the Riemann solver and the physical boundary value --- selected by
  an enumerator parsed from the case file. Adding an isothermal wall, a subsonic
  inlet or a pressure outlet is one enumerator plus one branch in each function,
  with no change anywhere else.
\item[Transport and turbulence.] \code{TransportModel} already carries constant
  and Sutherland laws behind a single \code{viscosity(T)} call. A RANS eddy
  viscosity enters at exactly that point plus one extra transported variable,
  which is what \code{kNVar} controls.
\item[Fluxes and limiters.] The Riemann solver and the limiter are selected by
  enumerator at run time and are pure functions of their arguments
  (\code{FluxInviscid.hpp}, \code{Limiter.hpp}), which is why the flux
  cross-check of \cref{tab:fluxcompare} and the limiter comparison of
  \cref{tab:mms} are single command-line switches rather than rebuilds.
\end{description}

Nothing in \code{src/} branches on a case identifier, a mesh file name or a
boundary-family name: a repository-wide search for the eight case ids, the two
mesh file names and the four family names of the supplied meshes returns matches
only in comments and in the \code{case\_id} metadata label. Case differences
come exclusively from the JSON inputs and from the documented command-line
options listed in \cref{tab:numerics}.

\section{Visualisation and Plot-Style Conventions}
\label{sec:vis}

All figures are produced by \code{tools/make\_figures.py} from the submitted
output files and share one style module (\code{tools/plot\_style.py}).

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Line plots} use a serif 11\,pt font, a colour-blind-safe
  qualitative palette, line widths of 1.1--1.8\,pt, labelled axes with
  nondimensional quantities, a legend on every panel, a light grid and inward
  ticks on all four sides. Residual histories are logarithmic in the ordinate;
  steady force histories use a symmetric-log abscissa so the startup transient
  and the converged plateau are both legible.
\item \textbf{Field plots} are filled contours (\code{tricontourf}) evaluated on
  the \emph{actual} mesh: every cell is split into triangles from its own
  connectivity, and cell values are moved to the nodes by area-weighted
  averaging. No interpolation grid and no scatter plot is used anywhere.
  Between 18 and 60 contour levels are used, with thin contour lines overlaid on
  the near-body views.
\item \textbf{Colour bars} are always present and always carry the variable name
  and its nondimensional definition. Ranges of the Mach, pressure and
  velocity-magnitude plots are clipped to the $1$--$99$ percentile \emph{of the
  plotted window}, so that a handful of cells inside a shock or at a stagnation
  point cannot collapse the range for the rest of the field; the clip is
  recorded in the caption column of \code{report/figure\_manifest.csv} for
  every affected figure. Vorticity uses a fixed symmetric range on a diverging
  colour map centred on zero: $[-5,5]$ for the cylinder cases, which is the
  range recommended in the case file, and $[-20,20]$ for the aerofoil cases,
  whose boundary-layer vorticity is an order of magnitude larger.
\item \textbf{View windows} are chosen per geometry: a near-body window for the
  aerofoil ($-0.6\le x\le1.8$), a wake window for the cylinder
  ($-2\le x\le14$), and a wide view for the farfield recovery. The wall outline
  taken from \code{surface.csv} is overlaid on every field plot so the body is
  unambiguous.
\item \textbf{File naming} follows \code{<case\_id>\_<variable>.png} and each
  file is registered in \code{report/figure\_manifest.csv} together with the
  variable it plots and the file it was made from. A \code{*\_mach.png} plots
  Mach number and a \code{*\_pressure.png} plots static pressure; the mapping is
  checked by \code{tools/sanity\_checks.py} and by the benchmark validator.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Roe flux at the Mach 2 bow shock.} With the Roe flux the Mach 2
aerofoil case stalls far short of its residual target and generates tens of
thousands of first-order positivity fallbacks per run, whereas HLLC on the
identical mesh and settings converges with none
(\cref{tab:fluxcompare} and the \code{positivity\_fallback\_face\_states} field
of the two \code{metadata.json} files). The Roe implementation is exact for
a stationary Rankine--Hugoniot shock (\cref{sec:verification}) and agrees with
HLLC on the subsonic cases (\cref{tab:fluxcompare}), so this is the classical
Roe shock instability rather than an implementation defect --- the
strongly stretched cells that this viscous-type mesh places across the bow shock
are exactly the configuration that triggers it. The entropy-fix coefficient is
exposed as \code{--entropy-fix}, but increasing it adds dissipation to the
acoustic fields only, which is not where the instability originates. HLLC was
therefore adopted as the production flux; Roe remains available and verified,
and the two agree closely on every case where Roe converges.

\paragraph{Residual limit cycle on the shock cases.} As described in
\cref{sec:time}, the non-differentiable $\min/\max$ stencil of the limiter drives
the shock-containing cases into a bounded residual limit cycle. Freezing the
limiter removes it at Mach 0.8 (\cref{fig:limiter_study}): the frozen run reaches
the requested four orders within a few steps of the freeze point, whereas the
unfrozen run stalls at $1.3\times10^{-3}$ of its initial residual for the whole
20000-step budget. The two differ by $1.4\%$ in $C_D$ ($0.008543$ frozen against
$0.008664$ unfrozen), which is a direct measure of how much the limit cycle
biases the unconverged answer; the frozen value is the converged one and is what
\cref{tab:forces} reports. At Mach 2 the run
reaches the requested three-order reduction, and the stationarity test accepts
it, while the solution is still inside the cycle: over the acceptance window the
drag scatter is about $0.2\%$ of $C_D$ with no drift. Continuing the same case
without limiter freezing to the full 20000-step budget
(\code{studies/verify/naca0012\_m200\_inviscid\_nofreeze}) leaves $C_D$ at
$0.08809$ against $0.08799$ for the accepted production run --- a $0.12\%$
difference, inside that band. That few tenths of a percent --- not the five
digits printed in \cref{tab:forces} --- is the honest uncertainty of the Mach 2
drag on this mesh, and it is the reason the force-stationarity tolerance is
$0.3\%$ rather than tighter. A differentiable limiter (an MLP-$u2$ variant or
a smooth pressure-based sensor) would be the proper fix.

\paragraph{Limiter accuracy in smooth regions.} The manufactured-solution study
measures \mmsOrderSecondLimited{} with the limiter active against
\mmsOrderSecond{} without it, so at these resolutions the Venkatakrishnan
limiter is not costing accuracy in smooth flow. That is not guaranteed to hold
under further refinement: $\epsilon^{2}=(Kh_c)^{3}s_v^{2}$ vanishes faster than
$\Delta^{\pm2}\sim h^{2}$, so on a sufficiently fine mesh the limiter degenerates
towards Barth--Jespersen and the formal order drops. Raising $K$ postpones this
at the cost of shock robustness; $K=5$ is the compromise used throughout, and no
attempt was made to tune it per case.

\paragraph{Low-Mach dissipation.} No low-Mach preconditioning is implemented.
At $M_\infty=0.1$--$0.15$ the upwind dissipation of HLLC scales with the
acoustic speed rather than the convective speed, which adds artificial
dissipation. The $Re=20$ cylinder drag nevertheless lands within $1\%$ of the
accepted value, so the effect is tolerable on these meshes, but a
Weiss--Smith-type preconditioner would improve the low-Mach cases.

\paragraph{Single mesh per geometry.} The benchmark supplies one mesh per
geometry, so no grid-convergence study of the benchmark results themselves is
possible; the order verification in \cref{sec:verification} is performed on
generated meshes instead. Reported force coefficients therefore carry an
unquantified spatial discretisation error.

\paragraph{Hybrid LU-SGS across ranks.} The Gauss--Seidel coupling is exact
within a rank and lagged between ranks, so the number of nonlinear iterations
grows slightly with rank count. This is visible in \cref{tab:mpi} as a small
difference in the step count to reach the same residual target.

\paragraph{Two-dimensional only.} \code{kDim} and \code{kNVar} are compile-time
constants and the mesh layer is written for polygonal cells; extending to 3-D
requires a polyhedral face/cell topology in \code{GlobalMesh}/\code{LocalMesh}
and a 3-D CGNS element mapping, but not changes to the numerics interfaces.

\section{Conclusions}

\code{cfd2d} implements the required physics and numerics --- conservative
finite volume on unstructured mixed meshes, second-order limited reconstruction,
approximate Riemann solvers, consistent viscous discretisation, implicit
pseudo-time marching and true dual-time BDF2 --- with METIS partitioning and
neighbour-scoped MPI halo exchange, and it produces complete, converged or
statistically periodic results for all \numCases{} required benchmark cases with
\numFailedCases{} failure. Independent verification gives an observed
discretisation order of \mmsOrderSecond{} for the unlimited second-order scheme
and machine-level freestream preservation and linear exactness on the benchmark
mesh; the two quantitative validation targets available in the literature ---
$C_D\approx2.0$--$2.1$ for the $Re=20$ cylinder~\cite{dennischang,tritton} and
$St\approx0.19$--$0.20$, $C_D\approx1.32$--$1.40$ for the $Re=200$ vortex
street~\cite{williamson,henderson} --- are reproduced: the $Re=20$ drag and its
pressure/friction split to about $1\%$, and the $Re=200$ Strouhal number within
the accepted band with a mean drag and lift amplitude a few percent below it,
for the reasons set out in \cref{sec:cyl200}.

The principal weaknesses, all documented in \cref{sec:limitations}, are the
limiter-induced residual limit cycle on the shock-containing cases, which leaves
a few tenths of a percent of uncertainty in the Mach 2 drag; the absence of
low-Mach preconditioning; and the fact that the supplied single mesh per
geometry makes a grid-convergence study of the benchmark results themselves
impossible, so the reported coefficients carry an unquantified spatial
discretisation error on top of the iterative one. The largest single correction
made during development --- and the clearest illustration of why the force split
is worth reporting separately --- was the wall-stress treatment of
\cref{eq:wallstress}: assembling the no-slip traction from the general
face-gradient formula produced a normal component worth $67\%$ of the Mach 2
laminar drag, where the exact value is zero.
