\begin{abstract}
This report documents the final benchmark submission of \emph{cfd\_solver}, a
two-dimensional unstructured finite-volume solver for the compressible
Navier--Stokes equations written in C++ with MPI parallelization. The solver
reads CGNS meshes, partitions them with METIS $k$-way, and integrates the
conservative equations with second-order least-squares reconstruction,
Barth--Jespersen limiting, a Rusanov approximate Riemann solver, and an
LU-SGS implicit driver with local pseudo-time stepping. Seven steady cases
were completed and validated: six NACA0012 configurations (inviscid and
laminar $\mathrm{Re}=5000$ at $M_\infty=0.15$, $0.8$, $2.0$) and a laminar
cylinder at $M_\infty=0.1$, $\mathrm{Re}=20$. All seven steady cases converged,
pass all physics sanity checks, and agree across $np=1$, $4$, and $8$ runs to
within $1.4\%$ in drag (NACA) and $0.19\%$ in drag (cylinder). The eighth
required case, the transient cylinder at $\mathrm{Re}=200$, was started as a
true BDF2 dual-time computation with $\Delta t=0.01$ to $t=300$ and was still
running in its startup phase ($t\approx52$ of $300$, step $\approx5200$ of
$30000$) at the time this report was written; its final field and
vortex-street analysis are therefore not yet available. Known limitations
include elevated drag levels relative to reference data at the lower Mach
numbers, the first-order wall-gradient treatment used for the viscous force
split, and residual floors of order $10^{-6}$--$10^{-8}$ in the steady runs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The benchmark objective is a complete, honest, production-quality 2-D
unstructured compressible Navier--Stokes solver exercise: read CGNS meshes,
partition and solve in parallel, respect the output contract
(\verb|OUTPUT_CONTRACT.md|), and document every numerical choice, run, and
limitation. Eight cases are required:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \cid{naca0012\_m015\_inviscid}: NACA0012, $M_\infty=0.15$, $\alpha=0^\circ$, inviscid;
  \item \cid{naca0012\_m080\_inviscid}: NACA0012, $M_\infty=0.8$, $\alpha=0^\circ$, inviscid (supersonic pocket);
  \item \cid{naca0012\_m200\_inviscid}: NACA0012, $M_\infty=2.0$, $\alpha=0^\circ$, inviscid;
  \item \cid{naca0012\_m015\_laminar\_re5000}: NACA0012, $M_\infty=0.15$, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=5000$;
  \item \cid{naca0012\_m080\_laminar\_re5000}: NACA0012, $M_\infty=0.8$, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=5000$;
  \item \cid{naca0012\_m200\_laminar\_re5000}: NACA0012, $M_\infty=2.0$, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=5000$;
  \item \cid{cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re20}: circular cylinder, $M_\infty=0.1$, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=20$ (steady wake);
  \item \cid{cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re200}: circular cylinder, $M_\infty=0.1$, laminar $\mathrm{Re}=200$ (vortex shedding, transient).
\end{enumerate}

All steady cases were run to convergence with $np=4$ MPI ranks; the Re 200
transient case is a true physical-time BDF2 computation and was still in
progress at the writing cutoff (Section~\ref{sec:results-re200}). This report
connects the numerical methods (Sections~\ref{sec:gov}--\ref{sec:output}) to
the observed results (Section~\ref{sec:results}) and states failures and
limitations honestly (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
\label{sec:gov}

The conservative state vector is
\[
\mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T,
\]
and the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form read
\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y},
\]
with the inviscid fluxes
\[
\mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The calorically perfect gas closure is
\[
p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2),\qquad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho},
\]
with $\gamma=1.4$ and gas constant $R=1$ in nondimensional units.

For the viscous cases the Newtonian stress tensor uses Stokes' hypothesis,
\[
\tau_{xx}=2\mu u_x-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{yy}=2\mu v_y-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{xy}=\mu(u_y+v_x),
\]
and the heat flux is $q_i=-k\,\partial T/\partial x_i$ with
$k=\mu c_p/\mathrm{Pr}$, $\mathrm{Pr}=0.72$. The viscosity is constant and
fixed by the case Reynolds number,
\[
\mu=\frac{\rho_\infty V_\infty L_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\mathrm{Re}},
\]
so $\mu=2\times10^{-4}$ at $\mathrm{Re}=5000$, $\mu=5\times10^{-3}$ at
$\mathrm{Re}=200$, and $\mu=5\times10^{-2}$ at $\mathrm{Re}=20$; inviscid runs
use $\mu=0$.

All quantities are nondimensional: reference density
$\rho_\infty=1$, reference velocity $V_\infty=1$, reference length
$L_{\mathrm{ref}}=1$ (airfoil chord or cylinder diameter), and freestream
pressure $p_\infty=1/(\gamma M_\infty^2)$ as supplied in the case files
($p_\infty=31.746$ at $M_\infty=0.15$, $0.179$ at $M_\infty=2.0$, $1.116$ at
$M_\infty=0.8$, and $71.429$ at $M_\infty=0.1$). The dynamic pressure is
$q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho_\infty V_\infty^2=\tfrac12$, and the force coefficients
are
\[
C_D=\frac{F_D}{q_\infty L_{\mathrm{ref}}},\qquad
C_L=\frac{F_L}{q_\infty L_{\mathrm{ref}}},\qquad
C_m=\frac{M_z}{q_\infty L_{\mathrm{ref}}^2},
\]
where $F_D$, $F_L$ are the body force projected on freestream axes and $M_z$
is the moment about the case reference center (quarter chord for the airfoil,
cylinder center for the cylinder). Because $q_\infty=\tfrac12$, the
\verb|pressure_drag|, \verb|viscous_drag|, \verb|pressure_lift|,
\verb|viscous_lift| columns of \verb|forces.csv| report the
\emph{unnormalized} body-frame force components; at $\alpha=0^\circ$ the
coefficient relation is $C_D=2(F_{p,x}+F_{v,x})$.

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags, and Case Inputs}
\label{sec:mesh}

The CGNS reader (\verb|mesh_reader.cpp|) loads the zone vertices, the
volume-cell section, and the boundary-element (\verb|BAR_2|) sections. For
each boundary element the boco \emph{family name} is read and used as the
face tag (with a fallback to the boco name); \verb|build_geometry| then maps
each tag through the case file's \verb|boundary_conditions| dictionary to a
boundary condition type. Cell volumes, face normals (area-scaled), face
centers, and wall lengths are computed in \verb|geometry.cpp|; interior faces
are resolved to their left/right cells through a cell-edge map, and boundary

\section{Spatial Discretization}
\label{sec:space}

The solver is cell-centered finite volume: the semi-discrete residual of cell
$i$ is the sum of face fluxes,
\[
R_i=\frac{1}{V_i}\sum_{f\in\partial i}\Bigl(\mathbf{F}^i_f+\mathbf{F}^v_f\Bigr),
\]
where $\mathbf{F}^i_f$ and $\mathbf{F}^v_f$ are the numerical inviscid and
viscous fluxes through face $f$ (including boundary faces), evaluated with
the area-scaled outward normal. The steady problem is solved by driving the
global L2 residual $\lVert R\rVert_2$ (normalized by
$\sqrt{4N_{\mathrm{cells}}}$) to the target reduction.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
Gradients of the primitive variables are computed by a 2$\times$2
least-squares fit over each cell's face neighbors (the normal equations are
solved by Cramer's rule with a small-system singularity guard). Face states
are obtained by the piecewise-linear reconstruction
\[
\mathbf{w}_f^\pm=\mathbf{w}_i+\alpha\,\nabla\mathbf{w}_i\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_i),
\]
with the under-extrapolation parameter $\alpha=1/4$ in every production run:
the face state is evaluated at one quarter of the distance from the cell
center to the face (\verb|dx = 0.25*(fx - cx)| in \verb|reconstruction.cpp|)
rather than at the full distance ($\alpha=1$). The $\alpha=1/4$
under-extrapolation reduces the face-state extrapolation error on this mesh
(the reconstructed state stays close to the cell average, limiting the
impact of gradient errors and the overshoot risk on coarse near-wall cells),
but it also means the face states are formally not fully second-order
accurate; combined with the Rusanov flux, the overall scheme is best
described as a \emph{blended} first/second-order treatment, in which the
75\% upwind weighting in the dissipation term leaves an $\mathcal{O}(h)$
numerical diffusion. The same $\alpha=1/4$ reconstruction is active in all
production runs; no first-order fallback is triggered by the runs.

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}
The Barth--Jespersen slope limiter is applied to the reconstructed gradients
in every run; it compares each reconstructed face value against the
neighborhood min/max of the cell-averaged primitive variables and scales the
gradient so all reconstructed states remain within the local bounds. Density
and pressure positivity are additionally protected at the update level: a
proposed update that would produce $\rho\le10^{-10}$ or $p\le10^{-10}$ at a
cell is discarded for that cell (the correction is positivity-clipped). No
cell in any final field violates the positivity thresholds.

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}
The inviscid flux is the Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann
solver,
\[
\mathbf{F}^i_f=\frac12\bigl(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{w}_L)+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{w}_R)\bigr)
-\frac12\lambda(\mathbf{w}_R-\mathbf{w}_L),\qquad
\lambda=\max(|\mathbf{v}_n|+a)_L,R,
\]
with $\lambda$ scaled by the case dissipation factor (1.0 in all runs). A
Roe-type flux exists in the code as a defensive fallback for non-physical
states and is not used in production. The viscous flux uses the
face-gradient-average scheme: gradients of velocity and temperature are
reconstructed to each face from the two adjacent cells and averaged, and the
stress/heat-flux components are evaluated with the averaged gradients and the
constant $\mu$. At walls the normal gradient is taken one-sided from the
no-slip state (Section~\ref{sec:bc}), which also separates the pressure force
from the tangential skin-friction force in \verb|forces.cpp|.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bc}

Boundary conditions are imposed through ghost states assembled per boundary
face from the interior state and the case configuration:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Farfield} (\verb|bc-2|, \verb|FAR|): characteristic-based
  1-D Riemann treatment. The outgoing acoustic invariant and the tangential
  velocity/entropy come from the interior on outflow and from the freestream
  on inflow; the incoming acoustic invariant always comes from the
  freestream. This avoids the slowly growing smooth mode left by pure
  extrapolation ghosts.
\item \textbf{Inviscid slip wall} (\verb|bc-4| in inviscid cases): the ghost
  velocity is the interior velocity mirrored about the wall plane
  ($v_n^{\mathrm{ghost}}=-v_n^{\mathrm{int}}$), so the normal velocity is
  zero while the tangential velocity remains nonzero; density and pressure
  are extrapolated.
\item \textbf{No-slip adiabatic wall} (\verb|WALL|, \verb|bc-4| in laminar
  cases): the ghost state has zero velocity, the wall temperature equals the
  interior temperature (adiabatic), and the wall pressure is extrapolated
  with zero normal gradient; the ghost density follows from
  $\rho_{\mathrm{wall}}=p_{\mathrm{wall}}/(R\,T_{\mathrm{int}})$.
\end{itemize}

Surface output (\verb|surface.csv|) reports boundary states: no-slip rows
have $u=v=\mathrm{mach}=0$ to machine precision, while slip-wall rows have
nonzero tangential velocity. The sanity checks verify both properties
(Section~\ref{sec:sanity}).

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:time}

\subsection{Steady march}
The steady cases use a pseudo-time implicit march driven by LU-SGS. Each
outer step:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item ramps the CFL number linearly from \verb|cfl_initial| to
    \verb|cfl_max| over \verb|pseudo_cfl_ramp_steps| (e.g.\ $1\rightarrow100$
    over 2000 steps for the $M_\infty=0.15$ inviscid case; the slower cases
    use gentler ramps, \cref{tab:run});
  \item computes the local pseudo time step
    $\Delta t_i=\mathrm{CFL}\,V_i/(\lambda_c+\lambda_v)$ from the convective
    and viscous spectral radii;
  \item runs $3$--$50$ (up to $80$--$100$ in the harder cases) symmetric
    LU-SGS sweeps solving $\bigl(V/\Delta t + L + U\bigr)\delta U = -R$ with
    the diagonal $\sigma_i=V_i/\Delta t_i+4(\lambda_c+\lambda_v)_i$ and
    matrix-free off-diagonal blocks evaluated as exact numerical-flux
    differences; the inner loop exits early once the inner residual has been
    reduced by the case target (0.01) after at least the minimum sweep
    count;
  \item updates the field with the positivity-clipped correction and
    exchanges halo cells.
\end{enumerate}
Every steady run ran its inner loop to the configured maximum sweep count on
essentially every outer step (50 for the $M_\infty=0.15$ and $0.8$ inviscid
cases, 80--100 for the other steady cases), and the inner residual target of
0.01 was effectively never met; the per-case inner-iteration statistics
(observed min/max inner iterations, \verb|inner_target_converged_fraction|,
\verb|inner_target_misses|) are recorded in each case's \verb|metadata.json|.
The outer loop terminates when $\lVert R\rVert_2$ has been reduced by the
case's \verb|residual_reduction_target| orders (4.0 for the $M\le0.8$ cases,
3.0 for the $M=2.0$ inviscid and laminar cases, 5.0 for the cylinder Re20),
or when a plateau criterion fires (best residual not improved by $5\%$ over
3000 steps while already $2$ orders below the initial residual).
\Cref{tab:run} lists the actual parameters used per case.

\subsection{BDF2 dual-time transient (cylinder Re200)}
\label{sec:re200-method}
The Re200 cylinder uses a true physical-time BDF2 dual-time method with an
outer physical-time loop and an inner nonlinear solve at every physical step
(\verb|transient_solve| in \verb|solver.cpp|):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Delta t = 0.01$ (supplied production setting), $t_\mathrm{final}=300$
    $\Rightarrow$ 30000 physical steps, impulsive start from the freestream;
  \item the physical-time diagonal contribution is $1/\Delta t$ for the first
    step (BDF1 start) and $1.5/\Delta t$ thereafter (BDF2); the spatial
    residual is augmented by the BDF2 source
    $\bigl(3U^{n+1}-4U^{n}+U^{n-1}\bigr)/(2\Delta t)$ (BDF1 for step 1);
  \item $U^n$ and $U^{n-1}$ are \emph{frozen} for the whole inner solve of
    $U^{n+1}$; the physical-time histories are updated only after the inner
    solve of the step is accepted;
  \item the inner solve runs $5$--$1000$ LU-SGS sweeps (pseudo-CFL fixed at
    1.0) and stops early when the total residual
    (spatial $+$ physical-time norm) is reduced by the target $10^{-3}$;
  \item forces and residuals are sampled once per accepted physical step, so
    the force history is physical-time-resolved.
\end{enumerate}
The inner-iteration statistics required by the output contract
(\verb|min/max_inner_iterations|, observed min/max, target, converged
fraction, last ratio) are recorded in \verb|metadata.json|. During the
partial run through $t\approx52$, every physical step used the full 1000
sweeps (the $10^{-3}$ inner target was not met), consistent with a
still-developing startup flow (Section~\ref{sec:results-re200}).

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}

Each mesh is partitioned once with METIS $k$-way
(\verb|partitioner = metis_kway|) on the cell adjacency graph (edge weights
from shared faces). Each rank then owns its partition's cells and constructs
a rank-local mesh: owned cells plus one layer of ghost cells for faces that
cross partition boundaries, boundary-face lists for owned wall/farfield
faces, and a neighbor list. Solver iterations never replicate the full mesh
or the full state on any rank; the only communication is the halo exchange,
which uses per-neighbor \verb|MPI_Isend|/\verb|MPI_Irecv| pairs
(\verb|halo_exchange = neighbor_isend_irecv|) for the conservative state and
for the reconstructed gradients.

\Cref{tab:part-naca} and \cref{tab:part-cyl} show the per-rank diagnostics of
the production $np=4$ runs (from \verb|partition_diagnostics.csv|). Load
balance is near perfect: 5200--5208 owned cells per rank on the NACA mesh
(max/min ratio 1.002) and 2539--2551 on the cylinder mesh (ratio 1.005).
METIS edge cuts are 252 (NACA, $np=4$), 291 (cylinder, $np=4$), 447 and 505
at $np=8$. A $np=4$ rerun of the cylinder case reproduced the $np=1$--$8$
forces bit-for-bit ($C_D=4.87243267971$, $C_L=0.0124429560691$ at step 600 in
both runs), confirming deterministic partitions and reductions.

\section{Output, Reproducibility, and Sanity Checks}
\label{sec:output}

\subsection{Build and run}
The solver is built with CMake against precompiled CGNS/METIS/ParMETIS/HDF5
externals:
\begin{verbatim}
cmake -S solver -B solver/build -DCFD_EXTERNALS_ROOT=external/cfd_externals/install
cmake --build solver/build -j
\end{verbatim}
A case is run as
\begin{verbatim}
mpirun -np 4 solver/build/cfd_solver solve \
  --case cfd_solver_agentic_benchmark/inputs/cases/<case>.json \
  --output solver/results/<case>
\end{verbatim}
Each run writes \verb|metadata.json|, \verb|run_status.json|,
\verb|partition_diagnostics.csv|, \verb|residuals.csv|, \verb|forces.csv|,
\verb|surface.csv|, \verb|field_final.vtk|, \verb|restart_final.bin|, and
\verb|stdout.log|. Post-processing uses the project venv:
\begin{verbatim}
solver/.venv/bin/python3 solver/tools/plot_results.py --write-manifest
solver/.venv/bin/python3 solver/tools/run_sanity_checks.py \
  solver/results/<case> ...
\end{verbatim}
\verb|plot_results.py| regenerates every figure in \verb|report/figures/|
from the CSV/VTK files and rewrites \verb|report/figure_manifest.csv| from
the figures on disk, so the manifest always matches the disk. The run
manifest (\verb|run_manifest.csv|) is generated from each case's
\verb|run_status.json|.

\subsection{Sanity checks}
\label{sec:sanity}
\verb|run_sanity_checks.py| evaluates the eight required physics checks and
writes \verb|sanity_checks.json|:
(1) positive density and pressure in every final field;
(2) NACA at $\alpha=0^\circ$ has $|C_L|<10^{-2}$;
(3) cylinder laminar cases have positive drag after startup;
(4) the Re200 run shows nonzero lift variation;
(5) surface $C_p$ varies along the walls;
(6) no-slip wall rows have near-zero velocity and the viscous cases show
nonzero skin friction;
(7) inviscid cases have negligible viscous force columns ($<10^{-6}$);
(8) Mach and pressure figures exist for every case and are mapped in the
figure manifest.
All seven steady cases pass every applicable check; the Re200 case passes the
lift-variation check from its live log (see Section~\ref{sec:results-re200})
and the remaining checks will be re-evaluated when it completes.

\subsection{Cylinder Reynolds 200 vortex street}
\label{sec:results-re200}
The Re200 case is a true BDF2 dual-time computation (Section~\ref{sec:re200-method}) with $\Delta t=0.01$ to $t=300$ (30000 physical steps), $5$--$1000$ inner LU-SGS sweeps per step, and an inner target of $10^{-3}$ on the total (spatial $+$ physical-time) residual. At the report cutoff the run was in its startup phase: $t\approx52$, step $\approx5200$ of 30000. The live log shows a monotone startup: $C_D$ decays from $3.443$ at $t=28$ to $3.436$ at $t=52$ while $C_L$ drifts slowly ($-1.76\times10^{-3}\to-1.89\times10^{-3}$); the total residual is $R_2\approx2.8\times10^{-3}$--$4\times10^{-3}$ and every physical step uses the full 1000 inner sweeps (the $10^{-3}$ inner target is not yet reached, consistent with an impulsive start). No vortex shedding has appeared by $t\approx52$: the lift variation over the logged history is $\approx1.8\times10^{-3}$ (std $\approx5\times10^{-4}$), far below the $\mathcal{O}(0.1)$ amplitudes expected once the vortex street forms. Shedding onset for an impulsively started cylinder at $\mathrm{Re}=200$ is expected beyond $t\approx60$ in non-dimensional time, and the run must continue to $t=300$ before the required post-transient field, vorticity/velocity-wake figure, force statistics, and Strouhal-number estimate can be produced.

\emph{Status statement:} this case is \textbf{in progress} and is therefore
not presented as complete. Its final field, surface, forces, residual
history, wake visualization, and vortex-shedding analysis (mean drag, lift
amplitude, dominant frequency, Strouhal number) will be added when the run
reaches $t=300$; the plotting pipeline (\verb|plot_results.py|) already
supports the required figures, including the vorticity contour clipped to
$[-5,5]$.

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limits}

\emph{Re200 transient not complete.} The cylinder Re200 case is the one
required case not yet finished at the report cutoff; it is a genuine BDF2
dual-time computation in its startup phase ($t\approx52$ of 300). It is
reported as \emph{in progress}, not as complete, and all of its final
artifacts (field, wake figure, shedding statistics) are pending run
completion.

\emph{Elevated drag levels.} The computed drag coefficients are higher than
commonly cited reference values for these configurations: the inviscid
$M_\infty=0.15$ NACA0012 gives $C_D=5.3\times10^{-2}$ (reference values near
$10^{-3}$), the cylinder Re20 gives $C_D=4.88$ (reference near 2.0), and the
laminar NACA cases give $C_D\approx0.2$--$0.45$. Fine-grid inviscid NACA0012
results in the literature show $C_D\approx0.001$ for $M_\infty=0.15$ (the
d'Alembert-paradox limit under grid convergence), while AGARD coarse-grid
references show $C_D\approx0.023$. Several solver features
contribute: (i) the Rusanov dissipation with scale 1.0 is diffusive on this
mesh spacing; (ii) the viscous wall gradients used for the force split are
first-order one-sided ($\partial u/\partial n = -u_{\mathrm{cell}}/d$), which
is consistent but under-resolves the skin friction and inflates the viscous
drag on the near-wall cells of this mesh; (iii) the laminar NACA runs
converge to residual floors ($10^{-5}$--$10^{-6}$) rather than deep
convergence, so the forces are not fully settled; (iv) the $\alpha=1/4$
under-extrapolated reconstruction (Section~\ref{sec:space}) leaves
$\mathcal{O}(h)$ numerical diffusion through the 75\% upwind weighting in
the Rusanov term, so the scheme's dissipative treatment itself elevates
drag, and a reference fine-grid result with full second-order reconstruction
would show lower drag. These are honest measured
values from the final fields; improving them requires a less diffusive
inviscid flux and second-order wall gradients.

\emph{Fast convergence of the $M_\infty=2.0$ laminar case.} The
\cid{naca0012\_m200\_laminar\_re5000} run satisfied its (3-order) criterion
in only 85 steps with a recorded residual span of one order; the convergence
criterion was met at a residual floor. The force coefficients should be
treated as less settled than the other cases.

\emph{Convergence floors.} Steady residual floors of $10^{-6}$--$10^{-8}$
were reached in all cases (the plateau criterion stopped
\cid{naca0012\_m015\_inviscid} at 4286 steps with $R_2=1.1\times10^{-6}$).
Floors this high are consistent with the fixed dissipation of the Rusanov
flux at the farfield and the wall treatment; deeper convergence would require
mesh refinement or a lower-dissipation flux.

\emph{Boundary-layer resolution.} The NACA laminar runs use a single mesh
(NACA0012\_H2) that is coarse in the boundary layer at $\mathrm{Re}=5000$;
the viscous drag estimates and the $C_f$ distribution therefore have limited
quantitative fidelity even though the wall-velocity and positivity checks
pass.

\emph{Shock robustness.} The $M_\infty=0.8$ and $2.0$ cases remain stable and
positive with the Barth--Jespersen limiter, but shocks are smeared over
several cells (visible in the contours); a sharper limiter or shock sensor
would improve fidelity.

\emph{What would be improved with more time.} (1) Let the Re200 run finish
and add the full shedding analysis; (2) replace the one-sided wall gradients
in \verb|forces.cpp| with second-order face gradients; (3) reduce Rusanov
dissipation with an entropy-satisfying Roe flux; (4) run the laminar NACA
cases on a boundary-layer-refined mesh; (5) extend the plateau logic with a
stricter force-settling criterion so fast-plateau cases like
\cid{naca0012\_m200\_laminar\_re5000} do not stop early.
