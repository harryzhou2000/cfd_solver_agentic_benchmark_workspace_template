\begin{abstract}
This report presents a 2-D unstructured finite-volume solver for the compressible
Navier--Stokes equations of a calorically perfect gas. The solver implements
second-order spatial reconstruction with Barth--Jespersen limiting, Roe's
approximate Riemann solver with Harten--Yee entropy fix, laminar viscous terms,
and implicit LU-SGS time integration with BDF2 dual-time stepping for transient
cases. MPI domain decomposition using METIS partitioning is employed for
parallel execution. Results are presented for short-duration test cases.
\end{abstract}

\section{Governing Equations}

The compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form are:

\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}^{i}(\mathbf{U}) = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}^{v}(\mathbf{U}, \nabla\mathbf{U}),
\]

where $\mathbf{U} = [\rho, \rho u, \rho v, \rho E]^T$ is the conservative state vector.
The inviscid flux $\mathbf{F}^{i}$ is given by the Euler equations, and the viscous flux
$\mathbf{F}^{v}$ uses the Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier heat flux.
The gas is calorically perfect with $p = (\gamma-1)\rho e$, $\gamma = 1.4$, $Pr = 0.72$.

\section{Numerical Methods}

\subsection{Spatial Discretization}
A cell-centered finite-volume method is used on mixed-element unstructured meshes.
Second-order accuracy is achieved through piecewise-linear reconstruction using
weighted least-squares gradients (inverse-distance-squared weighting).
The Barth--Jespersen limiter provides oscillation control, with first-order
fallback for negative density or pressure.

\subsection{Inviscid Flux}
Roe's approximate Riemann solver with Harten--Yee entropy fix ($\varepsilon = 0.1$)
is used for the inviscid numerical flux. The Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) flux
is available as a fallback.

\subsection{Viscous Flux}
Viscous fluxes are computed using cell-averaged gradients with directional
correction (edge-based gradient improvement). The Newtonian stress tensor and
Fourier heat flux are evaluated at cell faces using averaged primitive gradients.

\subsection{Boundary Conditions}
Farfield boundaries use characteristic-based Riemann invariants.
Inviscid walls use a slip-wall reflection condition.
Viscous no-slip adiabatic walls enforce zero velocity and zero normal
temperature gradient.

\subsection{Time Integration}
Steady cases use implicit pseudo-time continuation with local time stepping
based on convective and viscous spectral radii. The inner linear system is
solved with LU-SGS (symmetric Gauss--Seidel) sweeps using a simplified
matrix-free Jacobian. CFL ramping is used from the supplied initial to
maximum values.

Transient cases use BDF2 dual-time stepping with a physical-time outer loop
and inner pseudo-time iterations. The physical-time states are frozen during
inner iterations, and the BDF2 source term is included in the inner residual.

\subsection{Parallel Implementation}
METIS k-way partitioning divides the cell graph across MPI ranks.
Each rank stores only its owned cells plus one layer of ghost cells.
Halo exchange uses non-blocking point-to-point communication
(MPI\_Isend/MPI\_Irecv) with neighbor-scoped communication.
Global reductions (MPI\_Allreduce) are used for residuals and force coefficients.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Convergence Behavior}
The solver produces valid residuals for short runs at low CFL values
(CFL $\leq$ 1.04). At higher CFL values, the LU-SGS implicit solver
becomes unstable, producing NaN residuals. The instability threshold
is approximately CFL = 3, which is reached at step 500 of the ramp
with the supplied CFL parameters.

\subsection{Force Coefficients}
For the NACA0012 M0.15 inviscid case, the drag coefficient is approximately
0.11 and the lift coefficient is near zero, consistent with the symmetric
airfoil at zero angle of attack.

\subsection{Limitations}
The solver has several known limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{High-CFL instability}: The LU-SGS implicit solver diverges
    at CFL values above approximately 3 for low-Mach flows on the NACA0012\_H2
    mesh. This prevents convergence within the specified step budgets.
  \item \textbf{Stagnation-point anomaly}: At low Mach numbers ($M \leq 0.2$),
    the solver produces elevated stagnation pressure
    ($c_p \approx 10$--14 vs. expected $\approx 1.02$).
  \item \textbf{Cylinder case instability}: The solver diverges on the
    CylinderB1 mesh for both Re=20 and Re=200 cases.
  \item \textbf{Transient solver performance}: The BDF2 dual-time solver
    requires many inner iterations per physical time step, making the
    Re=200 transient case computationally expensive.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
A 2-D unstructured finite-volume compressible Navier--Stokes solver has been
implemented with the required numerical methods: second-order reconstruction,
Roe flux with entropy fix, Barth--Jespersen limiter, LU-SGS implicit time
integration, BDF2 dual-time stepping, and MPI parallelism. The solver
produces valid results for short runs at low CFL values. Full convergence
within the specified step budgets is limited by the high-CFL stability
constraint, which would require a more robust implicit solver or
low-Mach preconditioning.
