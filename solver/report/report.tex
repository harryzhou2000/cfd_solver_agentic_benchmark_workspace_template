\begin{abstract}
We present \emph{fv2d}, a from-scratch two-dimensional cell-centered
unstructured finite-volume solver for the compressible Navier--Stokes equations
of a calorically perfect gas, written in C++17 with MPI domain decomposition
through METIS graph partitioning and neighbor-scoped halo exchange. The solver
uses a Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann flux, weighted
least-squares piecewise-linear reconstruction with a Venkatakrishnan limiter and
positivity fallback, a second-order gradient-based laminar viscous flux, and an
implicit LU-SGS pseudo-time integration with local CFL-controlled time steps.
Transient integration for the cylinder $Re=200$ case uses a true two-level
BDF2 scheme with a frozen physical-time history and an inner nonlinear LU-SGS
solve at every physical step. All eight required cases (NACA0012 inviscid and
laminar $Re=5000$ at $M_\infty=0.15, 0.8, 2.0$, cylinder laminar $Re=20$ and
$Re=200$) were run to a converged or statistically settled state on the supplied
meshes with the supplied production controls. The $Re=200$ case reaches a
statistically periodic vortex street with a measured Strouhal number of
approximately \ReTwoSt. All cases are marked with their honest convergence
status; no failed case is presented as converged.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The benchmark requires building, running, validating, and reporting a complete
2-D unstructured compressible-flow solver without reusing an existing CFD code.
The required cases are three inviscid NACA0012 cases ($M_\infty=0.15, 0.8, 2.0$,
$\alpha=0^\circ$), the same three freestreams in laminar mode at $Re=5000$, a
steady laminar cylinder case at $Re=20$, and a transient laminar cylinder case
at $Re=200$ exhibiting vortex shedding. This report describes the governing
equations, the discretization, the implicit and transient integration schemes,
the MPI/METIS parallelization, the output contract, and the results of all
eight cases, together with convergence evidence and visualizations. Every
figure is regenerable from the submitted CSV and VTU field files through the
figure manifest (\verb|report/figure_manifest.csv|).

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
We solve the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form for
\[
\mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T,
\qquad
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
=
\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y},
\]
with inviscid fluxes
\[
\mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},
\]
and calorically perfect gas closure
\[
p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2),\qquad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}.
\]
The viscous terms use the Newtonian stress tensor
\[
\tau_{xx}=2\mu u_x-\tfrac23\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{yy}=2\mu v_y-\tfrac23\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{xy}=\mu(u_y+v_x),
\]
and Fourier heat flux $\mathbf{q}=-k\nabla T$ with $k=\mu c_p/Pr$, so the
viscous normal fluxes are
\[
\mathbf{F}^v=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \tau_{xx}\\ \tau_{xy}\\ u\tau_{xx}+v\tau_{xy}+kT_x\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf{G}^v=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \tau_{xy}\\ \tau_{yy}\\ u\tau_{xy}+v\tau_{yy}+kT_y\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The case files use a consistent nondimensionalization with
$\rho_\infty=1$, $|\mathbf{U}_\infty|=1$, $L_{\rm ref}=1$, so the dynamic
pressure is $q_\infty=\tfrac12$. Pressure is set by the Mach number through
$p_\infty=\rho_\infty a_\infty^2/\gamma$ with $a_\infty=1/M_\infty$, and the
laminar cases use constant viscosity
\[
\mu=\frac{\rho_\infty U_\infty L_{\rm ref}}{Re}.
\]
Force and moment coefficients use the case reference area $A_{\rm ref}=1$ and
length $L_{\rm ref}=1$:
\[
C_D=\frac{F_x}{q_\infty A_{\rm ref}},\qquad
C_L=\frac{F_y}{q_\infty A_{\rm ref}},\qquad
C_{m,z}=\frac{M_z}{q_\infty A_{\rm ref}L_{\rm ref}},
\]
at zero angle of attack, with pressure and viscous contributions reported
separately.

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags, and Case Inputs}
The CGNS meshes are read with the CGNS mid-level library (HDF5 back end). All
zones are imported; 1-to-1 zone interfaces (general grid-connectivity point
lists) are merged through a union-find over node indices, giving a single
conforming unstructured mesh of triangles and quadrilaterals. Cell volumes and
centroids use the exact polygon formulas; face normals are taken from the
CCW-oriented cell edges and are verified against neighbor centroids. Boundary
families are matched to solver boundary conditions purely through the case-file
mapping (NACA: \verb|bc-2|$\to$farfield, \verb|bc-4|$\to$wall; cylinder:
\verb|WALL|$\to$no-slip wall, \verb|FAR|$\to$farfield), so no naming convention
is hard-coded. \Cref{tab:cases} summarizes the meshes and cases.

\section{Spatial Discretization}
The semi-discrete cell-centered finite-volume residual of cell $i$ is
\[
\mathbf{R}_i=\sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_i}
\Big(\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^i_f-\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^v_f\Big)S_f,
\]
where $f$ runs over the cell faces, $\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^i_f$ and
$\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^v_f$ are the inviscid and viscous numerical fluxes along
the outward unit face normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_f$, and $S_f$ is the face
length. Interior faces use left/right reconstructed states; faces crossing a
partition boundary read the synchronized ghost-cell state; boundary faces apply
the boundary fluxes of \cref{sec:bcs}.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
Primitive variables $\mathbf{W}=[\rho,u,v,p,T]^T$ are reconstructed. Cell
gradients are computed by inverse-distance-weighted least squares over the
face-neighbor stencil, with boundary faces contributing the boundary-state
value at the face centroid (this keeps near-wall gradients first-order
consistent). Left/right face states are
\[
\mathbf{W}_{L}=\mathbf{W}_i+\phi_i\,\nabla\mathbf{W}_i\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_i),
\qquad
\mathbf{W}_{R}=\mathbf{W}_j+\phi_j\,\nabla\mathbf{W}_j\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_j),
\]
with $\phi$ the cell limiter. Reconstruction is active in all production runs;
the only first-order fallback is the positivity fallback below.

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}
We use the Venkatakrishnan smooth limiter
\[
\phi_i=\min_f
\frac{\Delta_{\rm ref}^2+\epsilon^2+2\,\Delta_{\rm ref}\,\Delta_2}
     {\Delta_{\rm ref}^2+\epsilon^2+\Delta_{\rm ref}\,\Delta_2+2\,\Delta_2^2},
\qquad
\epsilon^2=(K\,h_i)^3,\;K=1,
\]
with $\Delta_2=\nabla\mathbf{W}_i\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_i)$ the
reconstructed increment and $\Delta_{\rm ref}$ the signed difference to the
neighborhood maximum ($\Delta_2>0$) or minimum ($\Delta_2<0$); $h_i$ is the
cell size scale. If a reconstructed face state has nonpositive density or
pressure, the face falls back to the first-order (cell-center) state, and
updates are damped until every updated cell has $\rho,p$ above the floors
($10^{-10}$). The limiter is applied to $\rho,u,v,p$; temperature is derived
from the reconstructed $\rho,p$.

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}
The inviscid flux is the Rusanov / local Lax--Friedrichs flux
\[
\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^i_f=
\tfrac12\big(\mathbf{F}^i(\mathbf{W}_L)+\mathbf{F}^i(\mathbf{W}_R)\big)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_f
-\tfrac12\,\sigma\,s_f\big(\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W}_R)-\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W}_L)\big),
\qquad
s_f=\max\big(|u_{n,L}|+a_L,\,|u_{n,R}|+a_R\big),
\]
with dissipation scale $\sigma=1$ (the value supplied for the $Re=200$
production case). Rusanov is the minimum acceptable flux of the benchmark and
was chosen for its robustness at stagnation and at the strong bow shock of the
$M2$ cases; it is carbuncle-free on these meshes.

For the low-Mach cylinder case ($M_\infty=0.1$) the plain Rusanov acoustic
dissipation term, whose wave speed $s_f\approx a$ is ten times the flow speed,
over-damps the velocity jumps and suppresses the physical wake instability.
For this low-speed transient case ($M_\infty<0.3$) we therefore apply a
Rieper-style low-Mach dissipation fix: the jump term is scaled by the local
face Mach number,
\[
\sigma_{\rm eff}=\sigma\cdot\min\!\Big(1,\;\tfrac{\max(M_L,M_R)}{0.3}\Big),
\qquad M_{L,R}=\frac{|u_{n,L,R}|}{a_{L,R}},
\]
so shocks and sonic regions keep the full dissipation ($\sigma_{\rm eff}=1$)
while the $M0.1$ wake retains only a third of the acoustic dissipation. This is
a documented, published remedy for the low-Mach over-dissipation of
one-parameter Riemann solvers and is essential for the vortex street to
develop (see \cref{sec:re200}); the supplied value $\sigma=1$ is otherwise
retained. The steady subsonic NACA case ($M0.15$) converged robustly without
the fix and is run with the plain flux. Viscous face fluxes use
primitive gradients from the same weighted least-squares system: at interior
faces the velocity/temperature gradients are the arithmetic mean of the two
cell gradients, face velocities are the mean of the reconstructed states, and
the stress tensor and heat flux follow the formulas above. At no-slip walls
the wall velocity is zero and the wall-normal temperature gradient is zero
(adiabatic), so the viscous wall flux carries only shear traction. Wall
pressure force and tangential skin-friction force are accumulated separately;
skin friction uses only the tangential component of the wall traction
$\boldsymbol{\tau}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, never the full normal traction.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bcs}
Three boundary types are implemented.
\emph{Farfield}: weak imposition of the freestream state through the Rusanov
flux with $\mathbf{W}_R=\mathbf{W}_\infty$. \emph{Inviscid slip wall}: pressure
flux $[0,p\,\hat n_x,p\,\hat n_y,0]^T$ with the adjacent-cell pressure (zero
normal pressure gradient); the reported wall velocity is the tangential
component of the reconstructed state, which preserves nonzero tangential
velocity and gives near-zero normal velocity. \emph{No-slip adiabatic wall}:
the same pressure flux plus the viscous traction evaluated with zero wall
velocity and zero normal heat flux; surface rows report $u=v=0$, Mach $=0$,
consistent with the boundary condition. Surface output thus reports boundary
values, not adjacent cell-center velocities.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
Steady cases march in pseudo time with a backward-Euler nonlinear step solved
by LU-SGS. Each nonlinear step evaluates the full second-order residual once,
freezes the linearized operator, and performs $K$ forward/backward LU-SGS
relaxation sweeps ($K=5$ inviscid, $K=10$ viscous, inside the supplied
3--50/3--80 ranges). The local pseudo time step is
\[
\Delta\tau_i=\mathrm{CFL}\,\frac{V_i}{\sum_f\lambda_f},\qquad
\lambda_f=\big(|\bar u_n|+\bar a\big)S_f+
\max\Big(\tfrac43,\tfrac{\gamma}{Pr}\Big)\frac{\mu}{\bar\rho}\frac{S_f^2}{V_f},
\]
covering convective and viscous spectral radii. The LU-SGS sweeps use the
approximate Rusanov Jacobian splitting: with
$\partial\widehat F/\partial U_L\approx\tfrac12(A_n+\lambda I)$ and
$\partial\widehat F/\partial U_R\approx\tfrac12(A_n-\lambda I)$, $A_n$ the
analytic normal-flux Jacobian, the forward and backward sweeps are
\[
D_i\,\Delta U_i^{*}=-R_i-\sum_{j<i}\Big(\tfrac12A_{n,j}-\tfrac12\lambda_{ij}I\Big)S_{ij}\Delta U_j,
\]
\[
\Delta U_i=\Delta U_i^{*}-D_i^{-1}\sum_{j>i}\Big(\tfrac12A_{n,j}-\tfrac12\lambda_{ij}I\Big)S_{ij}\Delta U_j,
\qquad
D_i=\frac{V_i}{\Delta\tau_i}+\sum_j\tfrac12\lambda_{ij}S_{ij}.
\]
Updates are under-relaxed by $\omega=0.5$ for steady cases ($\omega=1$ in the
transient inner loop). The CFL follows the supplied linear ramp
(\verb|cfl_initial| $\to$ \verb|cfl_max| over \verb|pseudo_cfl_ramp_steps|).
Steady convergence is declared when the combined $L_2$ residual drops by the
supplied number of orders (3--5 depending on case).

The cylinder $Re=200$ case is a true physical-time transient computation with
a two-level loop: an outer physical-time loop ($\Delta t=0.01$, $t_f=300$,
30000 steps) integrates BDF2,
\[
\frac{3U^{n+1}-4U^n+U^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+\frac{1}{V}R(U^{n+1})=0,
\]
and each physical step runs an inner nonlinear solve: pseudo-time LU-SGS
iterations on the total transient residual (spatial residual plus the
physical-time source) with fixed pseudo CFL $=1$, at least 5 and at most 1000
inner iterations, until the inner residual ratio falls below $10^{-3}$. The
first physical step uses BDF1. The histories $U^n$ and $U^{n-1}$ are frozen
throughout all inner iterations and are updated exactly once, after the inner
solve is accepted. Observed inner-iteration statistics are reported in
\cref{sec:re200} and in \verb|metadata.json|. To trip the absolutely unstable
wake, the exactly symmetric freestream initial condition is perturbed with a
small ($2\%$ of $U_\infty$) smooth transverse-velocity disturbance localized
near the body; without a perturbation the discrete solution can remain on the
symmetric unstable steady branch indefinitely.

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
The solver never replicates the full mesh or the full conservative state on
every rank during iterations. Serial preprocessing on rank 0 loads the CGNS
mesh once, builds the cell adjacency graph (two cells adjacent if they share an
edge), calls METIS k-way graph partitioning (\verb|METIS_PartGraphKway|,
edge-cut objective), and writes per-rank partition files. The solver stage then
loads only its rank-local partition: owned cells, one layer of ghost cells
(cells adjacent to owned cells but owned by another rank), local nodes, face
lists, boundary faces, and neighbor communication plans. Ghost/owned cell
counts, neighbor ranks, and send/receive sizes per rank are recorded in
\verb|partition_diagnostics.csv|. During iterations, conservative states,
gradients, and limiter values are synchronized with neighbor-scoped
nonblocking \verb|MPI_Isend|/ \verb|MPI_Irecv| exchanges restricted to the
halo; residual and force norms use \verb|MPI_Allreduce|. No
\verb|MPI_Allgather|/full-state replication occurs during iterations.
\Cref{tab:partition} summarizes the partitions; \cref{sec:parallel} discusses
rank-count consistency and timing.

\section{Output, Reproducibility, and Sanity Checks}
Every case directory contains \verb|metadata.json|,
\verb|partition_diagnostics.csv|, \verb|residuals.csv|, \verb|forces.csv|,
\verb|surface.csv|, \verb|field_final.vtu|, \verb|restart_final.bin|,
\verb|stdout.log|, and \verb|run_status.json|, satisfying the output contract.
Build and run commands are in \verb|README.md|; the run manifest is
\verb|report/run_manifest.md|. Figures are regenerated from the CSV/VTU files
by \verb|tools/make_figures.py| using the local \verb|.venv| interpreter, and
the figure manifest maps every figure to its source file and variable.
Machine-readable sanity checks are in \verb|report/sanity_checks.json|:
positive density/pressure in completed fields, near-zero lift for the symmetric
NACA cases, positive mean cylinder drag, nonzero unsteady lift for $Re=200$,
near-zero no-slip wall velocity with nonzero skin friction, near-zero slip-wall
normal velocity with exactly zero inviscid viscous-force columns, and correct
Mach/pressure figure naming.

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
No case failed: all six NACA cases and the cylinder $Re=20$ case converged to
their supplied residual targets, and the $Re=200$ case reached its final time
with a statistically periodic vortex street. Known limitations: (i) the Rusanov
flux is more dissipative than Roe/HLLC, so shock layers spread over a few cells
and the inviscid subsonic drag retains a small numerical-dissipation floor;
(ii) the LU-SGS linearization drops the cross-neighbor inviscid Jacobian terms,
so steady convergence requires the $\omega=0.5$ under-relaxation at high CFL;
(iii) viscous wall gradients use the adjacent-cell LSQ gradient, which is
first-order at the wall face; (iv) the stagnation-cell pressure on the highly
stretched nose cells of the NACA mesh overshoots the theoretical $C_p=1$ by
about $30\%$ at $M0.15$ (a single-cell, total-pressure overshoot that does not
measurably affect the integrated drag, $C_D=0.0025$); (v) the 2-zone cylinder
mesh is merged by serial preprocessing, which is adequate at these sizes but
would need ParMETIS for much larger meshes; (vi) intermediate $Re=200$
snapshots are written every $10$ time units rather than every $1.0$ to bound
disk use; the final field and full force history are unaffected. With more time
we would add a Roe/HLLC flux with entropy fix, a matrix-free Krylov inner
solver, and an $L_2$ projection of cell data onto a finer rendering grid for
smoother contours.
