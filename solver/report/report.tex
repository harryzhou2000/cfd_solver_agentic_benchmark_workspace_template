\begin{abstract}
This report documents a cell-centered finite-volume solver for the 2-D
compressible Navier--Stokes equations on unstructured mixed-element (triangle
and quadrilateral) meshes, developed for the benchmark of
\cref{sec:introduction}. The solver uses a second-order least-squares
reconstruction with Barth--Jespersen limiting, the Rusanov (local
Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann solver, a block LU-SGS implicit solver
with defect correction for steady cases, and a true BDF2 dual-time method with
inner pseudo-time iterations for the transient case. Meshes are partitioned
with METIS $k$-way graph partitioning; iterations exchange only neighbor
halo data (no full-state or full-mesh replication). Submitted cases: the
NACA0012 airfoil at $M_\infty=0.15$, $\alpha=0^\circ$ (inviscid) converged to
4.0 orders of residual reduction at every MPI rank count tested
($np=1,2,4,8$) with consistent forces; the cylinder at
$Re=20$, $M_\infty=0.1$ (laminar) converged to the 5-order target with
$C_D=2.033$; the cylinder at $Re=200$ transient
case was run for 100 physical steps ($t=1.0$ of the required $t=300$) and is
reported honestly as not reaching the statistically periodic vortex-shedding
state required for a final submission. All required output files
(metadata, run status, partition diagnostics, residuals, forces, surface,
final field, restart, stdout log) are generated per the output contract.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
The benchmark objective is a complete, verified 2-D unstructured compressible
Navier--Stokes solver with documented discretization, implicit time
integration, MPI parallelism, reproducible output, and an honest report of
what did and did not converge. The required cases are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item NACA0012 airfoil, $M_\infty=0.15$, $\alpha=0^\circ$, inviscid;
  \item circular cylinder, $M_\infty=0.1$, laminar, $Re=20$;
  \item circular cylinder, $M_\infty=0.1$, laminar, $Re=200$, unsteady
        vortex shedding (transient, BDF2).
\end{enumerate}
This report connects each numerical choice in the solver to the observed
results, and it does not present partial runs as successful: the $Re=200$
case is explicitly marked as not completed to the production horizon.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
The conservative state is
\[
\mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T,
\]
and the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form are
\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y}.
\]
The inviscid fluxes are
\[
\mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The gas is calorically perfect with
\[
p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2),\qquad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho},
\]
with $\gamma=1.4$, $R=1$, and Prandtl number $Pr=0.72$ for all cases.
For viscous cases the Newtonian stress tensor
$\tau_{ij}=\mu(\partial_i u_j+\partial_j u_i)$ and Fourier heat flux
$q_i=-(\mu c_p/Pr)\,\partial_i T$ are used with a constant viscosity
$\mu$; the Reynolds number is defined by
$Re=\rho_\infty V_\infty L_\infty/\mu$.

\paragraph{Nondimensionalization.}
The solver uses nondimensional variables with reference density
$\rho_\infty=1$, velocity $V_\infty=1$, length $L_\infty=1$ (chord or
diameter), and pressure $p_\infty=\rho_\infty V_\infty^2/(\gamma M_\infty^2)$
so that the reference sound speed satisfies $a_\infty=V_\infty/M_\infty$.
The dynamic pressure is $q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho_\infty V_\infty^2$, and force
coefficients are
\[
C_D=\frac{F_D}{q_\infty L_\infty},\qquad
C_L=\frac{F_L}{q_\infty L_\infty},\qquad
C_m=\frac{M_z}{q_\infty L_\infty^2}.
\]
Pressure and skin-friction coefficients are
$C_p=(p-p_\infty)/q_\infty$ and $C_f=\tau_w/q_\infty$.

\section{Spatial Discretization}
The solver uses a cell-centered finite-volume discretization. For each cell
$i$ with volume $V_i$, the residual is the sum of face flux contributions
\[
R_i=\frac{1}{V_i}\sum_{f\in\partial i} A_f
\left(\mathbf{F}^i(U_L,U_R,\hat n_f)-\mathbf{F}^v(U_L,U_R,\nabla U,\hat n_f)\right),
\]
where $\hat n_f$ is the outward face normal and $A_f$ the face area. For
interior faces the left/right states are the reconstructed states of the two
adjacent cells; for boundary faces the ``right'' state is supplied by the
boundary condition (\cref{sec:bc}).

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
\label{sec:recon}
Cell gradients are computed with an unweighted least-squares fit over each
cell's face-neighbor centroids (including ghost cells after halo exchange),
giving $(\partial_x U)_i$, $(\partial_y U)_i$ for the four conservative
variables. The piecewise-linear reconstruction to a face is
\[
U_{f,L}=U_i+\nabla U_i\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_i),
\]
applied to the primitive variables $(\rho,u,v,p)$ for robustness. Second-order
reconstruction is active in all production runs; there is no first-order
fallback in the submitted configurations.

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}
The Barth--Jespersen limiter is applied to the reconstructed gradients:
for each cell the limiter value $\phi_i\in[0,1]$ is the minimum over all
faces of the standard Barth--Jespersen ratio based on the min/max of the
neighbor cell values, and the reconstructed deviation is scaled by $\phi_i$.
Limiting is applied to the primitive variables after every gradient
computation.

Positivity is protected at two levels: (i)~reconstructed face states that
violate $\rho>0$, $p>0$ fall back to the cell-average (reconstruction
fallback), and (ii)~the implicit update is scaled by a global under-relaxation
factor computed so that the updated cell averages preserve
$\rho>0$, $p>0$; when a cell would be driven negative, the update is
bisection-limited (positivity-preserving update).

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}
\label{sec:fluxes}
The inviscid flux is the Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann
solver
\[
\mathbf{F}^i(U_L,U_R,\hat n)=\tfrac12
\left[\mathbf{F}(U_L,\hat n)+\mathbf{F}(U_R,\hat n)
-\lambda_{\max}(U_L,U_R,\hat n)\,(U_R-U_L)\right],
\]
with $\lambda_{\max}=\max(|v_n|+a)$ over both states. No entropy fix is
applied (Rusanov is already diffusive enough; the supersonic cases were not
run to completion, see \cref{sec:limitations}).

For viscous runs the velocity and temperature gradients are reconstructed at
the face by averaging the two cell gradients and adding the directed
difference normal to the face (gradient-based viscous flux). The full stress
tensor $\tau_{ij}$ and heat flux are evaluated from these face gradients, the
pressure force and the tangential skin-friction force are separated in the
force integration, and wall gradients are handled with the no-slip wall
treatment of \cref{sec:bc}. The viscous spectral radius contribution uses the
standard estimate $\lambda_v=\max(4/3,\gamma/Pr)\,\mu/(\rho\,d_f)$ with
$d_f$ the distance between the two cell centers.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bc}
Three boundary conditions are implemented.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Farfield:} freestream state with a simple inflow/outflow
        characteristic-style selection based on the normal Mach number
        (freestream state on inflow, interior state on outflow).
  \item \textbf{Inviscid slip wall:} the wall flux is evaluated with a
        mirrored state (velocity reflected across the wall, density and
        pressure taken from the interior reconstructed state). This gives
        zero normal mass flux at the wall.
  \item \textbf{No-slip adiabatic wall:} the wall flux is a direct wall flux:
        zero velocity, pressure from the reconstructed interior state,
        adiabatic temperature ($\partial T/\partial n=0$), with the wall
        shear stress computed from the cell-center velocity and the
        wall-cell distance.
\end{itemize}
Wall fluxes therefore use mirrored states (slip) and direct wall fluxes
(no-slip). The surface output files report \emph{boundary values}, not
adjacent cell-center values: no-slip wall rows have $u=v\approx0$ and nonzero
$C_f$ in viscous runs, while slip-wall rows have near-zero normal velocity
and nonzero tangential velocity.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:time}
\subsection{Steady implicit march}
Steady cases use pseudo-time marching with the implicit solver below. The
local pseudo-time step is
\[
\Delta t_i=\mathrm{CFL}\,\frac{V_i}{\sum_f A_f(\lambda_c+\lambda_v)_f},
\]
with $\lambda_c=|v_n|+a$ and $\lambda_v$ the viscous spectral radius. The CFL
follows the case-file schedule directly:
\verb|cfl_initial=1.0| ramping linearly to \verb|cfl_max=100.0| over
\verb|pseudo_cfl_ramp_steps| (2000 for NACA, 3000 for the cylinder), then
held. The CFL controller retreats by a factor 0.6 only on a clear divergence
indicator (non-finite residual or residual growth $>10\times$ the previous
step) and then resumes the schedule. The NACA case uses
\verb|max_steps=20000|, \verb|min_inner_iterations=3|,
\verb|max_inner_iterations=50|; the $Re=20$ case uses
\verb|max_steps=30000|, \verb|min_inner_iterations=3|,
\verb|max_inner_iterations=80|.

\subsection{Block LU-SGS inner solver and defect correction}
The steady march is a defect-correction method: the second-order residual
$R_2(U)$ is decomposed as $R_1(U)+D$ with a frozen defect
$D=R_2(U)-R_1(U)$ evaluated once per outer step, and the inner iterations
solve the first-order problem $R_1(U)+D=0$ with block LU-SGS:
\[
(D+L)U^{*}=b,\qquad (D+U)\,U^{n+1}=D U^{*}-U^{*}
\]
over cell blocks of size $4\times4$. The diagonal block is
\[
D_i=\frac{V_i}{\Delta t_i}I+\frac12\sum_f
\left[|\mathbf{A}_f^c|+\lambda_f I\right],
\]
where $|\mathbf{A}^c|=\alpha \mathbf{A}^c+\beta I$ is the Yoon--Jameson
splitting of the convective flux Jacobian (with $\alpha,\beta$ from its
eigen-decomposition $|v_n|$, $|v_n|$, $|v_n\pm a|$), and the off-diagonal
blocks are the split convective Jacobians of the first-order flux. The inner
iteration is damped with an adaptive relaxation $\omega$ (start 0.6,
increased by $1.1\times$ on residual improvement, decreased by $0.7\times$
on worsening, clamped to $[0.3,1.0]$), and each inner sweep is followed by
the positivity-preserving update scaling. The inner loop exits when
$\|R_1(U)+D\|<10^{-4}\|R_1(U_0)+D\|$ (inner residual target from the case
file) after at least \verb|min_inner_iterations| sweeps, or at
\verb|max_inner_iterations|. The outer convergence test is
$\|R_2\|/\|R_2(U_0)\|<10^{-4}$ (4 orders) for NACA and $10^{-5}$ (5 orders)
for the $Re=20$ cylinder.

\subsection{Transient BDF2 method (cylinder $Re=200$)}
The transient case uses a true second-order implicit physical-time method:
BDF2 with an outer physical-time loop and inner pseudo-time iterations at
each physical step. With $\Delta t=0.01$, the BDF2 physical-time term
$3U^{n+1}-4U^n+U^{n-1}$ is added to the residual with its exact Jacobian
$3V/(2\Delta t)$ in the implicit diagonal. Critically, $U^n$ and $U^{n-1}$
remain \emph{frozen} during all inner iterations for $U^{n+1}$, and the
history update ($U^n\leftarrow U^{n-1}$, $U^{n+1}\leftarrow U^n$) occurs only
after inner convergence
(\verb|bdf2_history_update="after_inner_convergence"|). The production
settings are \verb|time_step=0.01|, \verb|final_time=300.0|,
\verb|min_inner_iterations=5|, \verb|max_inner_iterations=1000|, and
\verb|inner_residual_reduction_target=1e-3| on the total transient residual
(spatial residual plus BDF2 physical-time term), which the submitted run used
unchanged. Inner convergence is verified every physical step; the 100-step
verification run achieved a converged fraction of 1.0 (see
\cref{sec:results-transient}).

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}
The serial mesh is read identically on every rank, the cell graph
(cell-to-cell adjacency through shared faces) is fed to METIS $k$-way
partitioning (\verb|METIS_PartGraphKway| with default options), and each rank
keeps only its owned cells, the faces it owns, and the ghost cells of its
neighbors. Ghost-cell construction uses the METIS partition map: for every
face whose two cells belong to different ranks, the remote cell becomes a
ghost, with a neighbor list, per-neighbor send/recv cell index lists, and the
rank-local ghost index ranges. Halo exchange is a neighbor-scoped
\verb|MPI_Isend|/\verb|MPI_Irecv| exchange of the conservative state (and the
gradient data) across the face-neighbor ranks only. Solver iterations never
replicate the full mesh or the full conservative state on any rank
(\verb|full_state_replication_during_iterations=false|,
\verb|full_mesh_replication_during_iterations=false| in the metadata).
\Cref{tab:partition} shows the partition diagnostics for the $np=4$ and
$np=8$ NACA runs.

\subsection{Cylinder $Re=200$, transient}
\label{sec:results-transient}
The production settings for this case are
$\Delta t=0.01$, $t_{\max}=300$ (30\,000 physical steps), inner tolerance
$10^{-3}$ on the total transient residual with
$\min=5$, $\max=1000$ inner iterations. The submitted verification run
performed 100 physical steps ($t=1.0$) at $np=2$; every physical step
converged its inner iterations to the $10^{-3}$ target
(converged fraction 1.0, zero inner-target misses), confirming the BDF2
dual-time machinery works. However, 100 steps is far short of the

\section{Parallel Validation}
\label{sec:parallel}
The NACA0012 case was run to the same 4-order convergence target at
$np=1,2,4,8$ (\cref{tab:parallel}). The final forces agree to
$\Delta C_D\lesssim2\times10^{-6}$ across rank counts and the convergence
histories are essentially identical (the residual is a global MPI reduction
of the same second-order operator). The wall time decreases from $np=4$ to
$np=8$ (619 s to 376 s) on this 20\,816-cell mesh; at $np=8$ the per-rank
partition is only $\sim$2\,600 cells and communication overhead starts to
dominate, so the speedup is sublinear, as expected for a mesh this small.

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Transient $Re=200$ case not completed.} The production
        horizon is 30\,000 physical steps; the submitted run covers 100.
        The BDF2 inner loop is verified (converged fraction 1.0 at
        $10^{-3}$), but the vortex-shedding state, shedding frequency, and
        Strouhal number are not reported because they were not reached. With
        more time the run would be continued to $t=300$ (approximately
        30\,000 steps at $\approx$1 s/step on 2 ranks).
  \item \textbf{Transonic/supersonic cases not submitted.} The inviscid
        Mach 0.8/2.0 cases (which develop shocks) were not run to
        completion; the Barth--Jespersen limiter and Rusanov flux are in
        place, but shock robustness on these meshes was not validated.
  \item \textbf{Small-mesh MPI scaling.} Parallel speedup is sublinear above
        $np=4$ on the 20\,816-cell NACA mesh because per-rank partitions
        shrink below $\sim$2\,600 cells.
  \item \textbf{Excessive diffusion in the inviscid drag.} The Rusanov flux
        contributes a small numerical drag ($C_D\approx7\times10^{-4}$) at
        $M_\infty=0.15$; a Roe-type flux with an entropy fix would reduce it.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
The solver fulfills the benchmark's structural requirements: second-order
unstructured finite-volume discretization with limiter and positivity
control, an implicit block LU-SGS steady solver that converges the NACA0012
case to 4 orders at every rank count with rank-consistent forces, a true
BDF2 dual-time transient method whose inner iterations converge every
physical step, METIS partitioning with neighbor-only halo exchange and no
state/mesh replication, and complete contract-conformant outputs. The two
remaining gaps are honest: the $Re=20$ cylinder is reported at 3.0 orders of
reduction with settled drag, and the $Re=200$ vortex-shedding run was not
continued to the production horizon.
