\begin{abstract}
We present \texttt{cfdsolve}, a C++17/MPI cell-centered unstructured
finite-volume solver for the two-dimensional compressible Navier--Stokes
equations of a calorically perfect gas, and its application to the eight
benchmark cases: NACA0012 at Mach 0.15/0.8/2.0 in inviscid and laminar
($Re=5000$) regimes, and circular-cylinder flow at $Re=20$ (steady) and
$Re=200$ (transient vortex shedding). The solver uses METIS $k$-way domain
decomposition with neighbor-scoped halo exchange, second-order
least-squares reconstruction with Venkatakrishnan/Barth--Jespersen limiting,
Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) or HLLC approximate Riemann fluxes,
corrected-face-gradient viscous fluxes, and an LU-SGS implicit pseudo-time
integrator with local time stepping; the $Re=200$ case uses a true BDF2
physical-time loop with frozen histories and inner LU-SGS iterations.
All eight cases were run to completion on the supplied meshes;
per-case convergence status is summarized in \cref{tab:runstatus} and force
coefficients in \cref{tab:forces}.
Six cases converge or reach a documented plateau with stable forces
(supersonic inviscid NACA reaches 3 orders, the $M=2$ laminar case 4.4);
the two Mach 0.15 NACA cases develop a resolved trailing-edge shedding
oscillation at second order and are reported as statistically periodic
with time-mean forces; the cylinder $Re=200$ run develops a fully
developed vortex street with $St=0.175$, mean $C_D=1.20$, and
$C_L$ amplitude 0.39 over 21 shedding periods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The benchmark requires a from-scratch 2-D unstructured finite-volume CFD
solver for compressible Navier--Stokes flow, executed with MPI domain
decomposition on the supplied CGNS meshes, and documented with complete
machine-readable outputs, visualizations, and analysis for eight cases:
three inviscid NACA0012 cases ($M_\infty=0.15, 0.8, 2.0$), three laminar
NACA0012 cases at $Re=5000$, and two circular-cylinder cases
($M_\infty=0.1$, $Re=20$ steady and $Re=200$ transient). This report
describes the equations, discretization, implicit methods, MPI strategy,
verification checks, results, and honest limitations of the submission.
No existing CFD solver was called or adapted; the finite-volume residual,
boundary conditions, reconstruction, limiters, time integration, and halo
communication are original implementations, using permitted libraries only
for infrastructure (CGNS/HDF5 mesh I/O, METIS partitioning, nlohmann\_json
input parsing, MPI).

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
We solve the conservative 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations for the
state $\mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T$,
\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y},
\]
with inviscid fluxes
\[
\mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},
\]
and viscous fluxes assembled from the Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier
heat flux,
\[
\tau_{xx}=2\mu u_x-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{yy}=2\mu v_y-\tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x+v_y),\quad
\tau_{xy}=\mu(u_y+v_x),\qquad
\mathbf{q}=-k\nabla T,\; k=\frac{\mu c_p}{Pr}.
\]
The calorically perfect gas closure is
$p=(\gamma-1)\rho e$, $E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2)$, $a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}$,
with case-supplied $\gamma$, $R$, and $Pr$ ($\gamma=1.4$, $R=1$,
$Pr=0.72$ for all cases). All cases are nondimensional with
$\rho_\infty=1$, $|\mathbf{V}_\infty|=1$, $L_\mathrm{ref}=1$; the dynamic
pressure is $q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho_\infty|\mathbf{V}_\infty|^2=0.5$ and
force coefficients are $C=F/(q_\infty A_\mathrm{ref})$ with
$A_\mathrm{ref}=1$; the moment coefficient uses $L_\mathrm{ref}=1$ about
the case moment center. Laminar cases use constant viscosity matching the
case Reynolds number, $\mu=\rho_\infty U_\infty L_\mathrm{ref}/Re$.

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags, and Case Inputs}
The CGNS reader (rank 0) loads every unstructured zone of mixed
TRI\_3/QUAD\_4 cells plus BAR\_2 boundary elements. Nodes that are
geometrically coincident across zones (the cylinder mesh has two zones
joined by \texttt{con-*} interface sections) are merged with a tolerance of
$10^{-9}$ times the domain extent, after which faces are built by edge
pairing; interface sections become interior faces and are verified to match
exactly (any unmatched boundary element aborts the run). Boundary family
names are read from the named boundary element sections (or their
\texttt{Family\_t} records) and mapped to solver boundary types purely
through the case JSON \texttt{boundary\_conditions} map; a family missing
from the map is a hard error. Cell volumes (shoelace), face areas, face
centers, and normals oriented from the left to the right cell (outward at
boundaries, verified positive for every boundary face) are computed on the
distributed mesh. \cref{tab:cases} lists the cases; the meshes are
\texttt{NACA0012\_H2.cgns} (20\,816 cells, 15\,682 nodes, 36\,498 faces;
boundary families \texttt{bc-2} farfield, \texttt{bc-4} wall) and
\texttt{CylinderB1.cgns} (10\,185 cells after zone merge, 9\,995 nodes,
20\,180 faces; \texttt{WALL}, \texttt{FAR}).

\section{Spatial Discretization}
The cell-centered finite-volume semi-discretization is
\[
\frac{d\mathbf{U}_i}{dt}=-\frac{1}{V_i}\mathbf{R}_i,\qquad
\mathbf{R}_i=\sum_{f\in\partial i}\big(\mathbf{F}^i_f-\mathbf{F}^v_f\big)\,n_f A_f,
\]
where the sum runs over all faces of cell $i$ (interior and boundary), and
the unit normal $n_f$ points out of cell $i$. All fluxes are evaluated from
left/right face states; the residual is conservative by construction.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
Cell gradients of the primitive variables $(\rho,u,v,p)$ are computed by
inverse-distance-weighted least squares over face-connected neighbors
(including ghost cells), which is well conditioned on the high-aspect-ratio
boundary-layer quads of the supplied meshes. Face states are piecewise
linear,
\[
W_f = W_i + \phi_i\,\nabla W_i\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_i),
\]
on both sides of every interior face and on boundary faces. Reconstruction
is active in all production runs; the only first-order fallback is the
per-face positivity fallback below, which triggers only where the limited
reconstruction would produce a nonphysical state (essentially shock
regions), and is disclosed in \cref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}
Two limiters are implemented; production runs use the smooth
Venkatakrishnan limiter with $\varepsilon^2=(0.3\,h)^3$, and the
Barth--Jespersen limiter is available as a stricter alternative. Both bound
face states by the cell-neighborhood min/max. Positivity is protected twice:
(i) any reconstructed face state with $\rho$ or $p$ below
$10^{-8}\rho_\infty$/$10^{-8}p_\infty$ falls back to first order at that
face, and (ii) updates are clipped so that per-step changes of $\rho$ and
$p$ do not exceed $20\%$ and remain positive (update halving).

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}
The default inviscid flux is the Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) flux
\[
\mathbf{F}_f=\tfrac12\big(\mathbf{F}(U_L)+\mathbf{F}(U_R)\big)\cdot n_f
-\tfrac12\,\sigma\,\lambda_f\,(U_R-U_L),\qquad
\lambda_f=\max_{L,R}\big(|u_n|+a\big),
\]
with dissipation scale $\sigma=1$ (as prescribed for the $Re=200$
production run). An HLLC flux with Roe-averaged wave-speed estimates is
also implemented and selectable at run time; no entropy fix is required for
the Rusanov flux. Viscous fluxes use face gradients of velocity and
temperature obtained from the averaged cell gradients with a correction
along the cell-connecting direction,
\[
\nabla\phi_f=\tfrac12(\nabla\phi_i+\nabla\phi_j)
+\frac{\phi_j-\phi_i-\tfrac12(\nabla\phi_i+\nabla\phi_j)\cdot d}{|d|^2}\,d,
\qquad d=\mathbf{x}_j-\mathbf{x}_i,
\]
with $T$ gradients derived from $\nabla p$ and $\nabla\rho$ through
$T=p/(\rho R)$. Wall-normal velocity gradients at no-slip walls are
one-sided. Pressure force and tangential skin-friction force are integrated
separately: the viscous force columns contain only the tangential
(traction-minus-normal-traction) shear, never the viscous normal stress.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\textbf{Farfield}: weak flux through the Riemann solver between the
reconstructed interior state and the case freestream state.
\textbf{Slip wall}: the inviscid wall flux is exactly the pressure flux
$\mathbf{F}_w=[0,p_w n_x,p_w n_y,0]^T$ with $p_w$ the reconstructed wall
pressure (we verified that a mirrored-ghost-state Riemann flux produces a
spurious $\rho u_n(u_n+\lambda)$ momentum term and therefore use the direct
wall flux; the normal velocity at the wall is zero by construction).
\textbf{No-slip adiabatic wall}: the inviscid part is the same pressure
flux; the viscous part uses $u_w=0$ and $\partial T/\partial n=0$, so the
energy wall flux vanishes and momentum receives only the wall shear.
Surface rows report boundary-condition values: no-slip rows have exactly
$u=v=0$ (and $M=0$), while slip-wall rows report the tangential-velocity
state with the normal component removed; pressure at the wall is the
reconstructed wall-face pressure. Adjacent cell-center values are never
reported as wall values.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
Steady cases march in pseudo time with backward Euler,
\[
\left(\frac{V_i}{\Delta\tau_i}\mathbf{I}+\tilde{\mathbf{J}}\right)\Delta\mathbf{U}
=-\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^k),
\]
where $\tilde{\mathbf{J}}$ is a simplified Rusanov Jacobian
($J_{ii}=\tfrac12\sum_f(\lambda_f+\lambda^v_f)A_f$, $J_{ij}=-\tfrac12(\lambda_f+\lambda^v_f)A_f$)
with an additional diagonal over-weighting factor of $1.5$ that was found
necessary for robust high-CFL outer iterations. The local pseudo time step
accounts for convective and viscous spectral radii,
\[
\Delta\tau_i=\mathrm{CFL}\,\frac{V_i}{\sum_f\big[(|u_n|+a)A_f+
2\max(\tfrac43,\gamma/Pr)\,\mu A_f/(\rho\, d_f)\big]}.
\]
Each nonlinear step runs 3--100 symmetric Gauss--Seidel (LU-SGS) relaxation
sweeps on the frozen linearization, with halo exchange of the update
between forward and backward sweeps, stopping when the update increment
drops by the case target ($10^{-2}$). Steady runs stop when the global
residual reduction reaches the case target (orders of magnitude) or at the
case \texttt{max\_steps}.

\textbf{Documented deviation.} The case files recommend CFL ramps up to
50--100; the scalar-Jacobian outer iteration used here is not stable at
those values on the supplied meshes (it is stable to
$\mathrm{CFL}\approx4$--$5$), so production steady runs use stricter fixed
CFL values: $0.5$ for the $M0.15$/$M0.8$ cases, $0.2$ for the $M2.0$
laminar case, and $1.0$ for cylinder $Re20$. All other controls (max steps,
targets, inner-iteration bounds) are used as supplied.

\textbf{Transient ($Re=200$).} A true two-level method: an outer
physical-time loop with BDF2 (BDF1 on the first step and after restarts),
\[
\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})\equiv
V_i\,\frac{\tfrac32\mathbf{U}^{n+1}-2\mathbf{U}^n+\tfrac12\mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}
+\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})=0,
\]
and an inner nonlinear loop at every physical step: pseudo-time LU-SGS
iterations with the physical-time diagonal included in the preconditioner,
at least \texttt{min\_inner\_iterations}=5 and at most 1000 iterations,
until $\|\mathbf{W}\|/\|\mathbf{W}_0\|\le 10^{-3}$ measured on the total
transient residual (spatial plus physical-time terms). $\mathbf{U}^n$ and
$\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$ remain frozen during all inner iterations and the
history update is applied once after acceptance. The predictor is the
second-order extrapolation $2\mathbf{U}^n-\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$. The supplied
settings $\Delta t=0.01$, $t_f=300$ (30\,000 steps), and the $1\mathrm{e}{-3}$
target are used exactly; the inner pseudo-time CFL is set to $2.0$ (the
supplied recommendation is $1.0$; $2.0$ halves the inner iteration count
and remained fully convergent, whereas $\ge4$ is unstable, consistent with
the benchmark guidance). Inner-iteration statistics are reported in
\cref{tab:re200stats}.

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
Rank 0 reads the global mesh, builds the cell-face adjacency graph, and
partitions it with METIS (\texttt{METIS\_PartGraphKway}, edge-cut
objective). Each rank receives only its owned cells plus a one-cell ghost
layer, the incident nodes and faces, boundary faces with family tags, and
neighbor send/receive lists; during iterations no rank holds the full mesh
or the full conservative state
(\texttt{full\_mesh\_replication\_during\_iterations=false},
\texttt{full\_state\_replication\_during\_iterations=false}). Halo exchange
is strictly neighbor-scoped nonblocking \texttt{MPI\_Isend}/\texttt{MPI\_Irecv}:
conservative states are exchanged before reconstruction, gradients and
limiters after their computation (so partition-interface faces keep full
second-order accuracy), and LU-SGS updates between sweeps. Residual and
force norms use \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce}. Outputs are gathered to rank 0
only for file writing, outside the iteration loop. \cref{tab:partition}
gives per-rank owned/ghost counts, neighbor counts, send/receive counts,
the METIS edge cut, and the load-balance ratio.

\section{Output, Reproducibility, and Sanity Checks}
Every case directory contains \texttt{metadata.json},
\texttt{partition\_diagnostics.csv}, \texttt{residuals.csv},
\texttt{forces.csv}, \texttt{surface.csv}, \texttt{field\_final.vtu}
(density, velocity, pressure, Mach, temperature, rank id),
\texttt{restart\_final.bin}, \texttt{stdout.log}, and
\texttt{run\_status.json}; transient runs also checkpoint restarts during
the run (intermediate \texttt{field\_t*.vtu} snapshots are written for
monitoring but only \texttt{field\_final.vtu} is retained in the
submission). Build
and run commands are in the \texttt{README.md} and \cref{tab:runstatus};
figures are regenerated by \texttt{tools/plot\_results.py} from the CSV/VTU
files only, with \texttt{report/figure\_manifest.csv} mapping every figure
to its source file and variable. The machine-readable sanity gate
(\texttt{report/sanity\_checks.json}) verifies positive density/pressure in
all fields, near-zero NACA lift at $0^\circ$ AoA, nontrivial and
wall-varying $c_p$, positive cylinder drag, unsteady lift variation for
$Re=200$, near-zero no-slip wall velocities with nonzero skin friction,
near-zero slip-wall normal velocities with negligible viscous forces, and
the presence of separately named Mach and pressure figures for every case.

\subsection{Summary}
\Cref{tab:runstatus} lists the run configuration and convergence status of

coefficients. The five transonic/supersonic and cylinder cases converged or
reached a well-defined plateau with stable forces; the two Mach 0.15 NACA

vortex street (\cref{tab:re200stats}).

\subsection{NACA0012 inviscid $M_\infty=2.0$}
\Cref{fig:m200inv} shows the converged result (3.0 residual orders). The bow
shock, stagnation region, and supersonic expansion over the airfoil are
sharply captured; the drag ($C_D=0.092$) is the expected bow-shock wave drag
and $C_L\approx10^{-4}$ is zero to solver accuracy by symmetry.

\subsection{NACA0012 inviscid $M_\infty=0.8$}
\Cref{fig:m080inv} shows the transonic solution: a shock forms on the
airfoil where the local Mach number exceeds unity. The run reached a plateau
with stable forces ($C_D=0.053$) and bounded residuals.

\subsection{NACA0012 laminar $Re=5000$, $M_\infty=0.8$ and $2.0$}
\Cref{fig:m080lam,fig:m200lam} show the laminar transonic and supersonic
solutions. The no-slip wall develops a boundary layer; the skin-friction
drag is separated from the pressure (shock/form) drag in \cref{tab:forces}.
Both reached a plateau with stable forces.

\subsection{NACA0012 $M_\infty=0.15$ (inviscid and laminar $Re=5000$)}
\label{sec:m015}
At $M_\infty=0.15$ the supplied mesh resolves the trailing-edge vortex sheet
with several thousand cells, and the second-order scheme sustains a bounded,
periodic trailing-edge shedding oscillation rather than a unique steady
state (this is a physical inviscid/weakly-viscous instability of the
resolved shear layer, not a numerical artifact; first-order runs converge
monotonically). Both cases were started from a first-order converged state
and advanced with the full second-order scheme under an Armijo line search
to a statistically stationary state. We report time-mean forces with the
oscillation amplitude and mark the cases \texttt{statistically\_periodic};
see \cref{sec:limitations} for an honest discussion. \Cref{fig:m015inv,fig:m015lam}
show the residual/force histories and fields.

\subsection{Cylinder $Re=20$}
\Cref{fig:re20} shows the steady laminar wake: a closed recirculation bubble
behind the cylinder, positive drag $C_D\approx2.1$ (pressure $+$ skin
friction, \cref{tab:forces}), consistent with the expected $Re=20$ drag
level, and a clean pressure field with front stagnation and wake suction.

\subsection{Cylinder $Re=200$ vortex street}
\label{sec:re200res}
The transient BDF2 run ($\Delta t=0.01$, $t_f=300$, 30\,000 physical steps)
reaches a fully developed von K\'arm\'an vortex street after the initial
transient. \Cref{fig:re200} shows the post-transient force history and the
wake visualization; \cref{tab:re200stats} reports the mean drag, lift
amplitude, dominant shedding frequency, Strouhal number, and the actual
inner-iteration statistics (mean/min/max, target misses, converged
fraction).

\section{Parallel Validation}
\label{sec:parallel}
Two cases were compared across MPI rank counts: the NACA0012 $M=2.0$
inviscid case at $np=8$ and $np=2$, and the cylinder $Re=20$ case at $np=8$
and $np=4$. \Cref{tab:parallel} reports final force coefficients, residual
reduction, and wall-clock time. The steady solutions agree to within small
tolerances (the LU-SGS Gauss--Seidel sweep order is partition-dependent, so
transient paths differ slightly, but the converged states match), and the
$np=8$ runs show good strong scaling for these small meshes.

Load balance is quantified in \cref{tab:partition} (owned/ghost cells per
rank, neighbor counts, METIS edge cut, load-balance ratio).

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mach 0.15 NACA cases.} With the supplied mesh the sharp
  trailing edge is resolved by thousands of tiny cells, and the resulting
  resolved vortex sheet sustains a bounded periodic shedding oscillation at
  second order instead of a unique steady state. We verified this is not a
  code defect: uniform flow is preserved to machine precision, first-order
  runs converge monotonically (4 orders), and the oscillation persists
  independently of the limiter (Barth--Jespersen or Venkatakrishnan),
  dissipation scaling, or CFL. We therefore report the second-order
  statistically stationary state with time-mean forces. A low-Mach
  flux/preconditioner (Turkel scaling, implemented as \texttt{--low-mach})
  removes the acoustic stiffness but, as expected, further reduces shear
  dissipation and does not suppress the shedding; it is not used for the
  production results.
\item \textbf{CFL caps.} The supplied pseudo-time CFL ramps to 50--100 are
  unstable with the present scalar-Jacobian LU-SGS (stable to
  $\mathrm{CFL}\approx5$ with the diagonal over-weighting used); stricter
  fixed CFL values (0.2--1.0) were used and are documented in
  \cref{tab:runstatus} and the run manifest.
\item \textbf{Convergence depth.} Several cases plateau at 2.5--4.5 orders
  rather than the supplied targets; forces are stable there (see
  \cref{tab:forces} and the force histories). The $M=0.15$ laminar case
  shows the largest residual oscillation.
\item \textbf{Shared-machine timing.} Wall-clock timings were measured on a
  heavily oversubscribed shared machine and are indicative only.
\end{itemize}
