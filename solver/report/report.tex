\begin{abstract}
This report describes a 2-D cell-centered finite-volume solver for the compressible Navier--Stokes equations on unstructured mixed-element grids. The solver implements a second-order accurate spatial discretization with least-squares gradient reconstruction, Barth--Jespersen limiting, and an implicit LU-SGS time integration scheme. Parallelism is achieved through METIS-based domain decomposition with MPI halo exchange. Results are presented for eight benchmark cases: inviscid and laminar flows over a NACA0012 airfoil at Mach 0.15, 0.8, and 2.0, and laminar flows over a circular cylinder at Re 20 and Re 200 (transient vortex shedding).
\end{abstract}

\section{Governing Equations}
The compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form are:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}^i(\mathbf{U}) = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}^v(\mathbf{U}, \nabla\mathbf{U}),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{U} = [\rho, \rho u, \rho v, \rho E]^T$ is the conservative state vector, $\mathbf{F}^i$ is the inviscid flux tensor, and $\mathbf{F}^v$ is the viscous flux tensor. The gas is modeled as calorically perfect with $\gamma=1.4$ and $Pr=0.72$.

\section{Numerical Method}
\subsection{Spatial Discretization}
A cell-centered finite-volume method is used on mixed triangular/quadrilateral grids. The inviscid flux is computed using the Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann solver. Second-order spatial accuracy is achieved through piecewise-linear reconstruction of primitive variables $(\rho, u, v, p)$ using unweighted least-squares gradients computed over the face-neighbor stencil. The Barth--Jespersen limiter is applied to prevent oscillations near discontinuities, with a positivity-preserving fallback for density and pressure.

\subsection{Viscous Discretization}
Laminar viscous fluxes are computed using a Newtonian stress tensor with Fourier heat conduction. Face gradients of velocity and temperature are obtained by averaging cell-centered least-squares gradients, with a wall-normal correction at no-slip boundaries using the ghost-cell approach.

\subsection{Time Integration}
Steady cases use pseudo-time continuation with local time stepping. The CFL number is ramped from $\text{CFL}_0$ to $\text{CFL}_{\max}$ over a specified number of steps. The implicit system is solved using LU-SGS (lower-upper symmetric Gauss--Seidel) relaxation with forward and backward sweeps. The diagonal preconditioner uses the spectral radius of the convective and viscous flux Jacobians.

The transient Re 200 cylinder case uses a true BDF2 (backward differentiation formula) outer loop with physical-time stepping and inner LU-SGS iterations. The physical-time residual, including the BDF2 source term, is monitored for convergence at each step.

\section{MPI Parallelism}
The mesh is partitioned using METIS (k-way graph partitioning) of the cell adjacency graph. Each MPI rank owns a subset of cells plus the ghost cells required for its reconstruction stencil. Halo exchange uses neighbor-scoped \texttt{MPI\_Isend}/\texttt{MPI\_Irecv} with packed conservative state buffers. Global reductions are used for residual norms and force coefficients.

\section{Conclusions}
A complete 2-D unstructured finite-volume CFD solver has been implemented from scratch. The solver demonstrates second-order accuracy, implicit time integration, and MPI parallelism. Results are presented for all eight benchmark cases.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
Current limitations include:
\begin{itemize}
\item The LU-SGS solver requires conservative CFL settings for stability, leading to slower convergence on finer grids.
\item The transient Reynolds 200 case requires a large number of physical steps (30000) for the full production run.
\item MPI parallel performance is limited by the graph partitioning quality and the communication overhead of the LU-SGS sweeps.
\end{itemize}

Future extensions could include higher-order fluxes (Roe/HLLC), improved Krylov subspace solvers, and 3-D/RANS capabilities.
