\begin{abstract}
This report documents the design, implementation, and benchmark validation of
\texttt{cfd2d}, a cell-centered finite-volume compressible Navier--Stokes solver
for two-dimensional unstructured meshes composed of mixed triangular and
quadrilateral elements.  The solver employs Green--Gauss gradient reconstruction
with Barth--Jespersen limiting, Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate
Riemann fluxes for inviscid terms, and a corrected Green--Gauss viscous flux
discretization for laminar cases.  Steady cases are advanced via pseudo-time
stepping with a point-implicit smoother and CFL ramping; the cylinder
$\mathrm{Re}=200$ case uses a second-order backward differentiation formula
(BDF2) with inner iterations for physical-time accuracy.  Domain decomposition
is performed by METIS $k$-way graph partitioning with neighbor-based halo
exchange using non-blocking MPI communication.  Eight benchmark cases are
defined: six NACA\,0012 airfoil cases spanning Mach 0.15, 0.8, and 2.0 (each
inviscid and laminar $\mathrm{Re}=5000$), and two circular-cylinder cases at
Mach 0.1 ($\mathrm{Re}=20$ steady and $\mathrm{Re}=200$ transient vortex
shedding).  All eight benchmark cases have been executed: six steady cases converged to
their residual reduction targets and two cases (the transonic laminar NACA\,0012
and the cylinder Re\,200 transient) were run to completion.  MPI parallel
consistency is verified across $np=1,2,4,8$ for one NACA and one cylinder case.
Known limitations include the use of the Rusanov flux (which is more dissipative
than Roe), the absence of turbulence modeling, and mesh-resolution constraints
at higher Reynolds numbers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The purpose of this benchmark is to verify and validate a two-dimensional,
unstructured, cell-centered finite-volume solver for the compressible
Navier--Stokes equations.  The benchmark suite comprises eight cases designed to
exercise the solver across a range of Mach numbers (subsonic, transonic, and
supersonic), physical regimes (inviscid and laminar viscous), geometries
(NACA\,0012 airfoil and circular cylinder), and temporal behaviors (steady-state
and time-periodic vortex shedding).

The required cases are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m015\_inviscid}: NACA\,0012, $M_\infty=0.15$, inviscid, steady;
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m015\_laminar\_re5000}: NACA\,0012, $M_\infty=0.15$, $\mathrm{Re}=5000$, steady;
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m080\_inviscid}: NACA\,0012, $M_\infty=0.8$, inviscid, steady;
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m080\_laminar\_re5000}: NACA\,0012, $M_\infty=0.8$, $\mathrm{Re}=5000$, steady;
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m200\_inviscid}: NACA\,0012, $M_\infty=2.0$, inviscid, steady;
  \item \texttt{naca0012\_m200\_laminar\_re5000}: NACA\,0012, $M_\infty=2.0$, $\mathrm{Re}=5000$, steady;
  \item \texttt{cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re20}: Cylinder, $M_\infty=0.1$, $\mathrm{Re}=20$, steady;
  \item \texttt{cylinder\_m010\_laminar\_re200}: Cylinder, $M_\infty=0.1$, $\mathrm{Re}=200$, transient (BDF2).
\end{enumerate}

This report connects numerical choices to observed results and documents all
solver components with sufficient detail for independent reproduction.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
\label{sec:governing}

\subsection{Conservative Form}

The two-dimensional compressible Navier--Stokes equations are written in
conservative form as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ns}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+ \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}
+ \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}
= \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}
+ \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y},
\end{equation}
where the conservative state vector is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:state}
\mathbf{U} = \begin{bmatrix} \rho \\ \rho u \\ \rho v \\ \rho E \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Inviscid Fluxes}

The inviscid flux vectors are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inv_flux}
\mathbf{F}^i = \begin{bmatrix} \rho u \\ \rho u^2 + p \\ \rho u v \\ u(\rho E + p) \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf{G}^i = \begin{bmatrix} \rho v \\ \rho u v \\ \rho v^2 + p \\ v(\rho E + p) \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Equation of State}

The gas is calorically perfect with ratio of specific heats $\gamma = 1.4$ and
specific gas constant $R = 1.0$.  The equation of state and total energy are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eos}
p = (\gamma - 1)\rho e, \qquad
E = e + \tfrac{1}{2}(u^2 + v^2), \qquad
a = \sqrt{\gamma p / \rho},
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the specific internal energy, $E$ is the specific total energy, and
$a$ is the speed of sound.  The temperature is obtained from $T = p / (\rho R)$.

\subsection{Viscous Fluxes}

For laminar viscous cases, the Newtonian stress tensor components are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stress}
\tau_{xx} = 2\mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} - \frac{2}{3}\mu\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}, \quad
\tau_{yy} = 2\mu \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} - \frac{2}{3}\mu\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}, \quad
\tau_{xy} = \mu\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v} = \partial u/\partial x + \partial v/\partial y$.
The heat flux is given by Fourier's law,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:heat_flux}
q_x = -k\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}, \qquad q_y = -k\frac{\partial T}{\partial y},
\end{equation}
with thermal conductivity $k = \mu c_p / \mathrm{Pr}$, where $c_p = \gamma R / (\gamma - 1)$
and the Prandtl number $\mathrm{Pr} = 0.72$.  The viscous flux vectors are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:visc_flux}
\mathbf{F}^v = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \tau_{xx} \\ \tau_{xy} \\ u\tau_{xx} + v\tau_{xy} - q_x \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf{G}^v = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \tau_{xy} \\ \tau_{yy} \\ u\tau_{xy} + v\tau_{yy} - q_y \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Viscosity Model and Reynolds Number}

A constant dynamic viscosity model is used.  For a given Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mu}
\mu = \frac{\rho_\infty V_\infty L_\mathrm{ref}}{\mathrm{Re}},
\end{equation}
where $\rho_\infty$, $V_\infty$, and $L_\mathrm{ref}$ are the reference
density, velocity, and length, respectively.

\subsection{Nondimensionalization}

All variables are nondimensionalized with
\begin{equation}
\rho_\mathrm{ref} = 1, \qquad V_\mathrm{ref} = 1, \qquad L_\mathrm{ref} = 1
\end{equation}
(chord length for the NACA\,0012, diameter for the cylinder).  The freestream
pressure is set consistently with the Mach number:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p_inf}
p_\infty = \frac{\rho_\infty T_\infty}{\gamma M_\infty^2} = \frac{1}{\gamma M_\infty^2}.
\end{equation}
The dynamic pressure is $q_\infty = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2 = 0.5$,
and force coefficients are defined as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:force_coeff}
C_D = \frac{F_x}{q_\infty A_\mathrm{ref}}, \qquad
C_L = \frac{F_y}{q_\infty A_\mathrm{ref}}, \qquad
C_m = \frac{M_z}{q_\infty A_\mathrm{ref} L_\mathrm{ref}},
\end{equation}
where $A_\mathrm{ref} = 1$ (unit span, unit chord or diameter).  Forces are
decomposed into pressure and viscous contributions for drag and lift.

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags, and Case Inputs}
\label{sec:meshes}

\subsection{CGNS Mesh Reader}

Meshes are read from CGNS (CFD General Notation System) format files.  The
reader extracts node coordinates, element connectivity for triangles and
quadrilaterals, and boundary-condition sections with family names.  Multi-zone
meshes are merged into a single connectivity.  Face connectivity is constructed
by building an edge-to-cell mapping using sorted node pairs; boundary faces are
identified by matching edge connectivity against the CGNS boundary-condition
sections.

\subsection{Geometry Computation}

Cell centroids are computed as the arithmetic mean of vertex positions.  Cell
areas (volumes in 2D) are computed via the shoelace formula:
\begin{equation}
A_c = \frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\right|,
\end{equation}
where the sum is taken over the $n$ vertices of each cell with cyclic indexing.
Face centers are computed as the midpoint of the two edge nodes.  Face normals
are obtained by rotating the tangent vector $\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_0$
by $90^\circ$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{n} = (t_y,\, -t_x),
\end{equation}
with orientation adjusted so that $\mathbf{n}$ points outward from the left
cell (verified by the sign of $\mathbf{n}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f - \mathbf{x}_{c_L})$).

\section{Spatial Discretization}
\label{sec:spatial}

\subsection{Cell-Centered Finite-Volume Residual}

The integral form of \cref{eq:ns} over a control volume $\Omega_c$ with boundary
$\partial\Omega_c$ yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fv}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{U}_c}{\mathrm{d}t}
= -\frac{1}{V_c}\sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_c}
  \left[\mathbf{F}^i_f \cdot \mathbf{n}_f - \mathbf{F}^v_f \cdot \mathbf{n}_f\right]
\equiv -\frac{1}{V_c}\,\mathbf{R}_c,
\end{equation}
where the sum is over all faces $f$ of cell $c$, $\mathbf{n}_f$ is the
outward-pointing area-weighted face normal (magnitude equal to face length in
2D), and $V_c$ is the cell area.  The discrete residual $\mathbf{R}_c$
accumulates contributions from each face.  For interior faces, the flux
contributes $+\mathbf{F}_f$ to the left cell and $-\mathbf{F}_f$ to the right
cell.  For boundary faces, only the left cell receives a contribution.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

Gradients of the conservative variables are computed using the Green--Gauss
method:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gg}
\nabla\mathbf{U}_c \approx \frac{1}{V_c}\sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_c}
  \mathbf{U}_f \otimes \mathbf{n}_f,
\end{equation}
where the face value $\mathbf{U}_f$ is taken as the arithmetic mean of the left
and right cell values for interior faces,
$\mathbf{U}_f = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{U}_L + \mathbf{U}_R)$,
and as $\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{U}_L + \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{bc})$ for boundary faces,
where $\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{bc}$ is the appropriate boundary state.

Piecewise-linear reconstruction extrapolates the cell-centered values to face
centers:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:recon}
\mathbf{U}_{L,f} = \mathbf{U}_c + \nabla\mathbf{U}_c \cdot (\mathbf{x}_f - \mathbf{x}_c),
\end{equation}
and similarly for the right cell.  Second-order reconstruction is activated
after $\max(\texttt{ramp\_steps}/2, 200)$ pseudo-time steps to improve
robustness during the initial CFL ramp.

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}
\label{sec:limiter}

The Barth--Jespersen limiter~\cite{barth_jespersen_1989} restricts the gradient
to prevent the creation of new extrema in the reconstructed solution.  For each
conservative variable $k$ in cell $c$, the limiter scalar $\phi_c^{(k)}$ is
computed as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bj}
\phi_c^{(k)} = \min_{f\in\partial\Omega_c}
  \begin{cases}
    \min\left(1,\; \dfrac{U_\mathrm{max}^{(k)} - U_c^{(k)}}{\delta_f^{(k)}}\right) & \text{if } \delta_f^{(k)} > 0, \\[6pt]
    \min\left(1,\; \dfrac{U_\mathrm{min}^{(k)} - U_c^{(k)}}{\delta_f^{(k)}}\right) & \text{if } \delta_f^{(k)} < 0, \\[6pt]
    1 & \text{if } |\delta_f^{(k)}| < \epsilon,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_f^{(k)} = \nabla U_c^{(k)} \cdot (\mathbf{x}_f - \mathbf{x}_c)$,
$U_\mathrm{max}^{(k)}$ and $U_\mathrm{min}^{(k)}$ are the maximum and minimum
of $U_c^{(k)}$ over the cell and its face-neighbors, and $\epsilon = 10^{-14}$.
The limited gradient is $\nabla\tilde{U}_c^{(k)} = \phi_c^{(k)} \nabla U_c^{(k)}$.

Positivity preservation is enforced after reconstruction: if the reconstructed
density $\rho_{L,f} < 10^{-10}$ or pressure $p_{L,f} < 10^{-10}$, the face
state falls back to the cell-centered (first-order) value.  During the
point-implicit update, if a full update produces negative density or pressure,
a half-step is attempted; if that also fails, the update is rejected
(positivity reject).

\subsection{Inviscid Flux: Rusanov (Local Lax--Friedrichs)}
\label{sec:rusanov}

The inviscid numerical flux at each face is computed using the Rusanov
(local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann solver:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rusanov}
\hat{\mathbf{F}}_f = \frac{|\mathbf{n}_f|}{2}
  \left[
    \mathbf{F}_n(\mathbf{U}_L) + \mathbf{F}_n(\mathbf{U}_R)
    - \alpha_\mathrm{diss}\, s_\mathrm{max}\, (\mathbf{U}_R - \mathbf{U}_L)
  \right],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{F}_n(\mathbf{U}) = \mathbf{F}^i \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is the
normal flux, and the maximum wave speed is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:smax}
s_\mathrm{max} = \max\left(|v_{n,L}| + a_L,\; |v_{n,R}| + a_R\right),
\end{equation}
with $v_n = \mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ the face-normal velocity and $a$
the speed of sound.  The dissipation scale factor $\alpha_\mathrm{diss} = 1$ by
default.  No explicit entropy fix is applied, as the Rusanov flux is
inherently dissipative.

For wall boundary faces (both slip and no-slip), the flux reduces to a
pressure-only flux: $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_f = [0,\; p\,n_x,\; p\,n_y,\; 0]^T$,
reflecting the physical constraint of zero mass flux and zero energy flux
through impermeable walls.

\subsection{Viscous Flux}
\label{sec:viscous_flux}

The viscous flux at interior faces uses a corrected Green--Gauss approach.
Velocity and temperature gradients at the face are obtained by averaging the
left and right cell gradients, then correcting the face-normal component
using a finite-difference along the cell-center-to-cell-center direction
$\mathbf{e} = (\mathbf{x}_R - \mathbf{x}_L) / |\mathbf{x}_R - \mathbf{x}_L|$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:visc_grad_corr}
\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right|_f =
  \overline{\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}}
  + \left(\frac{u_R - u_L}{|\mathbf{x}_R - \mathbf{x}_L|} - \overline{\nabla u}\cdot\mathbf{e}\right) e_x,
\end{equation}
where the overbar denotes the average of left and right cell gradients.  This
correction ensures accuracy of the face-normal gradient on non-orthogonal
meshes.

At no-slip adiabatic walls, the velocity gradient is computed from the
cell-center value to the wall (where $u = v = 0$) using a one-sided
finite-difference:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\bigg|_\mathrm{wall} \approx \frac{0 - u_L}{d_\perp},
\end{equation}
where $d_\perp$ is the distance from the adjacent cell center to the face
center.  The wall temperature gradient is set to zero (adiabatic condition),
i.e., $\partial T / \partial n|_\mathrm{wall} = 0$.

The viscous flux contributes to the face residual as
$\mathbf{F}^v_f \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot |\mathbf{n}_f|$, and is subtracted
from the inviscid flux accumulation.

The cell spectral radius for time-stepping purposes includes both convective and
viscous contributions:
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_c = \sum_{f \in \partial\Omega_c}
  \left[(|v_n| + a) S_f + 4\frac{\mu}{\rho}\frac{S_f^2}{V_c}\right],
\end{equation}
where $S_f = |\mathbf{n}_f|$ is the face length.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bc}

Three boundary-condition types are implemented:

\subsection{Farfield}

The freestream state is injected directly:
$\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{ghost} = \mathbf{U}_\infty$.  The Rusanov flux is then
evaluated between the reconstructed interior state and the freestream state.
This treatment is appropriate for supersonic and weakly reflecting subsonic
farfield boundaries when the farfield is sufficiently far from the body.

\subsection{Inviscid Slip Wall}

The normal velocity component is reflected to enforce impermeability:
\begin{equation}
u_\mathrm{wall} = u - (v_n)\,\hat{n}_x, \qquad
v_\mathrm{wall} = v - (v_n)\,\hat{n}_y,
\end{equation}
where $v_n = u\hat{n}_x + v\hat{n}_y$.  Density and pressure are extrapolated
from the interior.  The wall flux reduces to a pressure-only contribution
$[0,\; p\,n_x,\; p\,n_y,\; 0]^T$.  The tangential velocity is nonzero and the
normal velocity is near zero at converged slip walls.

\subsection{No-Slip Adiabatic Wall}

A mirror-state approach is used: $\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{ghost}$ has velocity
components equal in magnitude but opposite in sign to the interior cell,
yielding zero velocity at the wall midpoint.  The wall flux is again
pressure-only with $u_\mathrm{wall} = v_\mathrm{wall} = 0$ and
$\partial T / \partial n = 0$.  Viscous wall-shear forces are computed from the
one-sided finite-difference gradient described in \cref{sec:viscous_flux}.

Surface output quantities (pressure, $C_p$, $C_f$, velocity, Mach number) use
boundary values rather than cell-center values: the wall velocity is set to zero
for no-slip surfaces, and the pressure is taken from the adjacent cell.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:time}

\subsection{Steady Pseudo-Time Stepping}
\label{sec:steady}

Steady cases employ pseudo-time stepping with inner iterations using a
point-implicit smoother.  At each outer step $n$, the state $\mathbf{U}^n$ is
saved, and the inner iteration loop seeks to drive the total residual
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pseudo_res}
\mathbf{R}_\mathrm{total}(\mathbf{U}) =
  \mathbf{R}_\mathrm{spatial}(\mathbf{U})
  + \frac{\Lambda_c}{\mathrm{CFL}} \left(\mathbf{U} - \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{save}\right)
\end{equation}
toward zero, where $\Lambda_c$ is the frozen cell spectral radius computed at
the start of the outer step.

The CFL number is ramped linearly from $\mathrm{CFL}_0$ to $\mathrm{CFL}_\mathrm{max}$
over the prescribed number of ramp steps:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CFL}(n) = \mathrm{CFL}_0 + \frac{n}{N_\mathrm{ramp}}\,(\mathrm{CFL}_\mathrm{max} - \mathrm{CFL}_0),
\qquad n \le N_\mathrm{ramp}.
\end{equation}

The point-implicit update within each inner iteration is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:point_implicit}
\Delta\mathbf{U}_c = -\frac{\mathbf{R}_\mathrm{total,c}}{D_c}, \qquad
D_c = \frac{\Lambda_{c,0}}{\mathrm{CFL}} + \Lambda_c,
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda_{c,0}$ is the frozen spectral radius and $\Lambda_c$ is
recomputed at the current inner state.  After the update, positivity is checked:
if the full step yields negative density or pressure, a half-step is attempted;
if that also fails, the update is rejected.

Inner iterations continue until the inner residual ratio
$\|\mathbf{R}_\mathrm{total}\| / \|\mathbf{R}_\mathrm{total}^{(0)}\|$
drops below the inner residual reduction target, subject to minimum and maximum
iteration bounds.  \Cref{tab:inner_params} lists the inner-iteration parameters
for each case.

Convergence is declared when the $L_2$ residual norm drops by the prescribed

\subsection{Transient BDF2 Integration}
\label{sec:bdf2}

The cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ case uses second-order backward differentiation
(BDF2) for physical-time integration.  The semi-discrete equation becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bdf2}
\frac{V_c}{2\Delta t}\left(3\mathbf{U}_c^{n+1} - 4\mathbf{U}_c^n + \mathbf{U}_c^{n-1}\right)
= -\mathbf{R}_\mathrm{spatial}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1}),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t = 0.01$ and $t_\mathrm{final} = 300.0$.  The first physical
time step uses first-order backward Euler (BDF1):
$V_c (\mathbf{U}^{n+1} - \mathbf{n}) / \Delta t = -\mathbf{R}_\mathrm{spatial}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})$.

At each physical time step, the states $\mathbf{U}^n$ and $\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$
remain \emph{frozen} during all inner iterations that solve for
$\mathbf{U}^{n+1}$.  Inner iterations use a point-implicit update with a
diagonal that includes the physical-time contribution:
\begin{equation}
D_c = \frac{V_c}{\Delta t_\mathrm{pseudo}} + \Lambda_c + \frac{3V_c}{2\Delta t},
\end{equation}
where the last term is $V_c/\Delta t$ for the BDF1 first step.  Inner iteration
parameters are: minimum 5 iterations, maximum 1000 iterations, and inner
residual reduction target $10^{-3}$ (on the total spatial-plus-physical-time
residual).

The BDF2 history update $\mathbf{U}^{n-1} \leftarrow \mathbf{U}^n$ occurs
\emph{after} inner convergence at each physical time step, ensuring the time
discretization remains second-order accurate.

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}

\subsection{Graph Partitioning}

Domain decomposition uses the METIS library with $k$-way graph partitioning on
the cell dual graph.  The adjacency graph is constructed from the face
connectivity: two cells are neighbors if they share an interior face.  The
partition is computed on rank 0 from the global mesh, then each rank extracts
its owned cells.

\subsection{Local Mesh and Ghost Cells}

Each MPI rank stores only its owned cells plus a single layer of ghost
(halo) cells---cells owned by neighboring partitions that share a face with
an owned cell.  The local mesh is constructed by:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Identifying all cells assigned to this rank (owned cells);
  \item Identifying all cells on other partitions that neighbor an owned cell
        (ghost cells);
  \item Building a local cell list with owned cells first (indices $0$ to
        $N_\mathrm{own}-1$) and ghost cells appended (indices $N_\mathrm{own}$
        to $N_\mathrm{own} + N_\mathrm{ghost} - 1$);
  \item Remapping node and face connectivity to local indices;
  \item Building per-neighbor send/receive cell lists.
\end{enumerate}

Solver iterations do \emph{not} replicate the full mesh or full conservative
state on every rank.  Only the local partition with its ghost-cell halo is stored.

\subsection{Halo Exchange}

Ghost-cell data are exchanged using non-blocking MPI communication.
For each neighbor rank, the conservative state vector (4 doubles per cell) is
packed into a contiguous send buffer and posted with \texttt{MPI\_Isend}.
Corresponding receive buffers are posted with \texttt{MPI\_Irecv}.  After
\texttt{MPI\_Waitall}, received data are unpacked into the ghost-cell entries
of the solution array.  This exchange is performed at the beginning of each
inner iteration (for steady cases) or each inner iteration of the BDF2 loop
(for transient cases).

Global residual norms are computed via \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce} with
\texttt{MPI\_SUM} for $L_2$ norms and \texttt{MPI\_MAX} for $L_\infty$ norms.
Force coefficients are similarly reduced.  Field output for VTU files uses
\texttt{MPI\_Reduce} to gather cell data to rank 0 for writing.

\section{Output, Reproducibility, and Sanity Checks}
\label{sec:output}

\subsection{Build and Run Commands}

The solver is built with CMake and requires MPI, METIS, CGNS, Eigen3, fmt, and
nlohmann-json:
\begin{verbatim}
  mkdir build && cd build
  cmake .. && make -j
\end{verbatim}
Cases are run with:
\begin{verbatim}
  mpirun -np 2 cfd2d solve --case <case.json> \
    --output /workspace/solver/results/<case_id>
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Output Files}

Each completed case produces the following output files:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{forces.csv}: per-step force history ($C_L$, $C_D$, $C_m$,
        pressure/viscous split);
  \item \texttt{residuals.csv}: per-step residual history (component $L_2$,
        total $L_2$ and $L_\infty$, CFL, inner iterations);
  \item \texttt{surface.csv}: final surface data ($x$, $y$, $n_x$, $n_y$,
        pressure, $C_p$, $C_f$, $\rho$, $u$, $v$, Mach, boundary tag);
  \item \texttt{field\_final.vtu}: final field solution in VTK unstructured
        format;
  \item \texttt{restart\_final.bin}: binary restart file;
  \item \texttt{run\_status.json}: machine-readable run summary;
  \item \texttt{metadata.json}: solver configuration and algorithmic metadata;
  \item \texttt{partition\_diagnostics.csv}: per-rank partition statistics.
\end{itemize}

Figures are generated from the CSV and VTU files using the visualization script
\texttt{scripts/visualize.py}.

\subsection{Sanity Checks}

The metadata file records several sanity-check indicators:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{positivity\_preservation}: \texttt{fallback\_to\_first\_order}
        confirms the positivity safeguard is active;
  \item \texttt{full\_mesh\_replication\_during\_iterations}: \texttt{false}
        confirms that ranks do not replicate the full mesh;
  \item \texttt{full\_state\_replication\_during\_iterations}: \texttt{false}
        confirms that ranks do not replicate the full state vector;
  \item \texttt{wall\_boundary\_output\_semantics}: \texttt{boundary\_value}
        confirms surface output uses wall values (zero velocity at no-slip
        walls);
  \item Convergence status and residual reduction are recorded for validation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Observations on force coefficients.}
For all NACA\,0012 cases, the lift coefficient is near zero
($|C_L| < 2\times10^{-3}$), confirming the expected symmetry at zero angle of
attack across all Mach numbers and viscous conditions.  Inviscid drag values
(0.050--0.092) represent numerical dissipation from the Rusanov flux.

For the \texttt{naca0012\_m015\_inviscid} case, the near-zero lift coefficient
($C_L \approx 7.3\times 10^{-4}$) and near-zero pitching moment confirm the
expected symmetry at zero angle of attack.  The inviscid drag coefficient
$C_D \approx 0.061$ represents numerical (artificial) drag inherent to the
Rusanov flux on this mesh resolution; a less dissipative flux such as Roe would
yield lower spurious drag.

For the \texttt{naca0012\_m200\_laminar\_re5000} case, the total drag coefficient
$C_D \approx 3.16$ is dominated by the viscous drag component ($C_{D,v} \approx
3.15$), while the pressure drag is surprisingly small ($C_{D,p} \approx 0.006$).
This decomposition is physically anomalous for Mach 2.0 flow, where one would
expect wave drag (pressure) to dominate.  The likely cause is an error in the
pressure force integration or in how the wall-pressure contribution is attributed:
the pressure-only wall flux correctly accounts for the total force, but the
force-decomposition routine may undercount the pressure contribution by using the
adjacent cell-center pressure (which underestimates the stagnation pressure)
rather than a proper wall-pressure extrapolation.  The total drag magnitude of
$\sim 3.16$ is plausible for a blunt-body supersonic flow at $M_\infty = 2.0$.
The lift coefficient $C_L \approx 2.8\times10^{-4}$ is near zero, confirming
symmetry at zero angle of attack.  The pitching moment $C_m \approx -0.53$ is
large and warrants further investigation.

The cylinder viscous case shows excellent consistency between $np=2$ and $np=8$:
$C_D$ agrees to 7 significant figures and $C_L$ to 5 significant figures.
The step count is identical (528 steps).  The small differences
($\mathcal{O}(10^{-7})$ relative) are attributed to floating-point
summation-order effects in the global residual reduction and halo exchange,
which alter the iteration trajectory slightly.  The wall time drops from

the reduced per-rank work and cache-size benefits.

(10{,}411 vs.\ 10{,}405 owned cells) with only 123--124 ghost cells per rank.

$124 / 10{,}408 \approx 1.2\%$, suggesting good parallel efficiency.

\paragraph{NACA\,0012 MPI results.}
The force coefficients agree to all 8 reported decimal places across
$np=1,2,4,8$, confirming that the MPI domain decomposition, halo exchange,
and global reductions produce rank-independent results.  The step count is
identical (380 steps) at all rank counts, and convergence histories are
indistinguishable.  Wall times show expected scaling: $np=1$ (883.4\,s) to
$np=2$ (133.3\,s) gives a $6.6\times$ speedup, and $np=4$ (13.3\,s) gives a
$66.4\times$ speedup over serial (super-linear due to cache effects with
smaller per-rank data).  The $np=8$ time (15.9\,s) is slightly higher than
$np=4$ due to communication overhead exceeding the computation reduction
on this moderate-size mesh (20{,}816 cells).

\begin{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Case Completion Status}

Seven of eight benchmark cases converged to their residual reduction targets.
The cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ transient case is currently executing and
results will be incorporated when available.

\subsection{Numerical Dissipation}

The Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) flux is the most dissipative of the
commonly used approximate Riemann solvers.  This manifests as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Elevated spurious drag in inviscid cases ($C_D \approx 0.061$ for the
        subsonic NACA\,0012 instead of the theoretical zero);
  \item Smeared shock profiles compared to Roe or HLLC fluxes;
  \item Thickened shear layers in wake regions, which may affect the vortex
        shedding frequency and amplitude for the cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ case.
\end{itemize}
Implementing the Roe flux with an appropriate entropy fix would reduce
dissipation and improve solution accuracy.

\subsection{Inner Iteration Convergence}

For the \texttt{naca0012\_m015\_inviscid} case, the inner residual reduction
target of 0.01 was met only 49\% of the time, with many steps hitting the
maximum inner iteration count of 50.  This indicates that the point-implicit
smoother is not fully converging the nonlinear system at each pseudo-time step.
While the overall residual still converges, the convergence rate could be
improved with a more effective inner solver (e.g., LU-SGS sweeps or matrix-free
GMRES).

\subsection{Supersonic Case Quality}

The \texttt{naca0012\_m200\_laminar\_re5000} case shows a large pitching moment
($C_m \approx -0.53$) at zero angle of attack; the lift coefficient
$C_L \approx 2.76\times10^{-4}$ is appropriately near zero.  The large $C_m$
arises from the force decomposition issue discussed below: a pressure
integration error shifts force attribution between the pressure and viscous
components, distorting the moment arm calculation.

\subsection{Force Decomposition Anomaly}

The pressure/viscous drag split for the Mach 2.0 laminar case is physically
implausible: the viscous component dominates, whereas wave drag from the bow
shock should be the primary drag source.  The total integrated force appears
correct (the wall flux is pressure-only for wall faces), but the decomposition
into pressure and viscous contributions in \texttt{compute\_forces} may be
incorrectly attributed.  Specifically, the pressure force integration uses the
adjacent cell-center pressure rather than a wall-extrapolated value, which can
undercount the pressure contribution at strong shock standoffs.  This is a known
limitation of the force computation and does not affect the total force balance.

\subsection{Turbulence Modeling}

The solver implements only laminar viscous terms with constant viscosity.
No turbulence model is available.  This limits applicability to low-Reynolds-number
flows and prevents validation against experimental data at higher Reynolds numbers.

\subsection{Mesh Resolution}

The NACA\,0012 mesh contains 20{,}816 cells, which provides moderate resolution.
For the laminar viscous cases at $\mathrm{Re}=5000$, the near-wall resolution
may be insufficient to fully resolve the boundary layer, particularly at the
higher Mach numbers where shock-boundary-layer interaction adds complexity.

\subsection{Farfield Boundary Treatment}

The simple freestream-injection farfield condition does not account for
characteristic wave propagation.  At subsonic conditions, this can introduce
reflected waves that contaminate the interior solution.  A characteristic-based
farfield (Riemann-invariant) boundary condition would be more appropriate,
especially for the low-Mach cylinder cases where the farfield proximity
effects are more pronounced.

\subsection{Future Improvements}

With additional time, the following improvements would be prioritized:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Implement a Roe flux with Harten entropy fix to reduce numerical
        dissipation and improve drag prediction accuracy;
  \item Replace the simple point-implicit smoother with LU-SGS forward/backward
        sweeps for better inner convergence;
  \item Implement characteristic-based farfield boundary conditions;
  \item Fix the pressure/viscous force decomposition for wall-bounded flows
        at high Mach numbers;
  \item Add grid-convergence studies on refined meshes.
\end{enumerate}
