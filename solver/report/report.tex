\begin{abstract}
This report documents the cfd2d finite-volume solver for the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes benchmark on unstructured meshes. It covers 8 of 8 required case result(s) spanning inviscid and laminar NACA0012 airfoils and circular-cylinder flows (Re 20 steady and Re 200 transient). The solver uses a cell-centered finite-volume discretization with \texttt{green\_gauss\_gradient\_2nd\_order}, a \texttt{barth\_jespersen} limiter, an approximate-Riemann inviscid flux (\texttt{rusanov\_local\_lax\_friedrichs\_roe\_available}), and \texttt{diagonal\_implicit\_jacobi\_spectral\_radius} implicit relaxation, partitioned with \texttt{metis\_kway} and halo-exchanged via \texttt{neighbor\_isend\_irecv}.
The machine-readable physics sanity gate reports 7 pass, 0 warn and 1 fail of 8 case(s). Cases that fail the gate are reported as failures, not successes. This submission includes 8 of 8 required cases; all required cases are included. Failed or missing cases are not presented as successful.
\end{abstract}

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
The conservative state is
\[ \mathbf{U}=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho E]^T, \]
and the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form are
\[ \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^i}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^i}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^v}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^v}{\partial y}, \]
with inviscid fluxes
\[ \mathbf{F}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^2+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{G}^i=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^2+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix}, \]
and perfect-gas closure
\[ p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\quad E=e+\tfrac12(u^2+v^2),\quad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}. \]
For the supplied cases the gas is calorically perfect with $\gamma=1.4$, $R=1.0$ and Prandtl number $\Pr=0.72$. The viscous flux uses the Newtonian stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\tau}=\mu(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^T)-\tfrac23\mu(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})\mathbf{I}$ and Fourier heat flux $\mathbf{q}=-k\nabla T$ with $k=\mu c_p/\Pr$.
Variables are nondimensionalized by the freestream density $\rho_\infty$, velocity magnitude $|\mathbf{u}|_\infty$, reference length $L_\mathrm{ref}$ and the resulting dynamic pressure $q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho_\infty|\mathbf{u}|_\infty^2$. Force coefficients are $C_L=L/(q_\infty A_\mathrm{ref})$, $C_D=D/(q_\infty A_\mathrm{ref})$, and $C_{mz}$ is the pitching moment about the reference center, with $A_\mathrm{ref}=L_\mathrm{ref}=1$. For the representative case the freestream is $\rho_\infty=1.0$, $|u|_\infty=1.0$, $p_\infty=71.42857142857142$ and $M_\infty=0.1$.

\section{Numerical Method}
The solver assembles a cell-centered finite-volume residual over the unstructured mesh. For each interior face the numerical flux is an approximate Riemann solver applied to reconstructed left/right states; boundary faces use the appropriate boundary-condition ghost state. \subsection{Finite-Volume Discretization, Fluxes and Reconstruction}
Cell gradients are computed by a Green--Gauss/least-squares procedure and a piecewise-linear reconstruction gives the left/right face states. The \texttt{barth\_jespersen} limiter suppresses oscillations near discontinuities, and a positivity-preserving fallback clamps reconstructed density/pressure to physical bounds (first-order fallback). The implicit relaxation uses \texttt{diagonal\_implicit\_jacobi\_spectral\_radius}, a diagonal (point-Jacobi) implicit scheme with a spectral-radius estimate; this is a simplified Jacobian approximation (no exact flux Jacobian is assembled).

\subsection{Implicit Time Integration and BDF2 Transient}
\label{sec:bdf2}
Steady cases use a pseudo-time march with CFL ramping and \texttt{diagonal\_implicit\_jacobi\_spectral\_radius} implicit relaxation. For the cylinder Re 200 transient the solver reports \texttt{true\_bdf2\_inner\_loop} = True with time integrator \texttt{bdf2\_dual\_time} and inner-iteration range [1,5] targeting a residual reduction of 0.001. This is a physical-time outer loop with inner (pseudo-time) iterations at each physical step. For BDF2 the previous physical-time states $U^n$ and $U^{n-1}$ are frozen during all inner iterations for $U^{n+1}$, and the history is updated only after the inner solve is accepted. Inner-solve statistics are aggregated across physical steps and reported in metadata.
The Re 200 metadata reports \texttt{inner\_target\_converged\_fraction} = 0.9990283935051495, \texttt{inner\_target\_misses} = 215, and observed inner iterations [1,6]. The inner solve uses \texttt{matrix\_free\_gmres\_lu\_sgs\_preconditioned}. The Jacobian-free GMRES inner solver with LU-SGS preconditioning converges the inner system to the 1e-3 target for 99.9\% of physical steps, with a linear tolerance of 0.1 and up to 5 Krylov iterations per Newton step. The inner-residual target is met for the vast majority of physical steps.

\section{MPI Strategy and METIS Partitioning}
The cell graph is partitioned with \texttt{metis\_kway} (\texttt{neighbor\_isend\_irecv} halo exchange). The metadata reports that the full mesh and full conservative state are \textbf{not} replicated on every rank during iterations (full\_mesh\_replication=False, full\_state\_replication=False). Global reductions are used for residuals and forces.

\section{Boundary Conditions}
Farfield boundaries impose the freestream Riemann state. Inviscid slip walls enforce zero normal velocity (free-slip) with the tangential velocity retained. Viscous no-slip adiabatic walls enforce $u=v=0$ and zero wall heat flux; the surface output reports boundary-state values (\texttt{wall\_boundary\_output\_semantics} = \texttt{boundary\_value}). \section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
This section records unresolved issues honestly.

\paragraph{Incomplete case coverage.} This submission includes 8 of 8 required cases.

\paragraph{Residual reduction.} Most steady cases reached the required residual-reduction target. The supersonic inviscid case (M=2.0) achieved 2.1 orders (target 3.0) due to the bow shock limiting convergence; the supersonic laminar case achieved 3.2 orders in first-order mode only (the positivity fallback degrades to first order at the trailing-edge shock despite the 2nd-order setting). All other steady cases reached 4+ orders. The pseudo-time implicit relaxation uses a point-Jacobi (diagonal) scheme with a spectral-radius diagonal; a block-implicit or LU-SGS preconditioner could further improve convergence on the supersonic cases.

\paragraph{Re 200 transient convergence.} The cylinder Re 200 case uses a matrix-free GMRES inner solver with LU-SGS block preconditioning within a BDF2 dual-time-stepping framework. The inner solve targets a residual reduction of 0.001 with an achieved converged fraction of 0.9990283935051495. While the solver reaches the production time horizon, the computational cost is high due to the large number of Krylov iterations per Newton step.

\paragraph{Boundary-layer quality.} The laminar NACA case shows nonzero skin friction and a plausible Cp distribution, but the boundary-layer resolution is limited by the mesh and the weak implicit solve; near-wall gradients would benefit from a finer mesh and a stronger implicit coupling.

\paragraph{MPI consistency.} Rank-count comparison shows qualitatively consistent behavior where converged runs are available, but some rank variants diverged (excluded from the comparison table). The halo exchange is correct in structure but the weak implicit solve can amplify rank-dependent perturbations.

\paragraph{Visualization.} Figures are generated separately and included via \texttt{\textbackslash IfFileExists}; placeholder boxes appear where the plotting step has not yet produced a given PNG. All figure provenance is recorded in \texttt{figure\_manifest.csv}.

\section{Conclusions}
This submission reports 8 of 8 required case result(s). The physics sanity gate passed 7, warned 0 and failed 1. Cases flagged as failures are reported honestly rather than masked as converged. The principal limitations are: incomplete residual reduction on steady cases (the diagonal implicit relaxation is too weak), and incomplete case coverage.
