\begin{abstract}
This report documents a 2-D unstructured finite-volume compressible Navier--Stokes solver built for the CFD Solver Agentic Benchmark. The solver is implemented in C++17 with MPI parallelism, using METIS graph partitioning, cell-centered finite-volume discretization, second-order reconstruction with Barth-Jespersen limiting, Rusanov and Roe approximate Riemann solvers, block-diagonal LU-SGS implicit time integration, and BDF2 transient capability. All eight required cases (six NACA0012, two circular cylinder) were executed with documented run parameters. The solver produces complete output artifacts per the benchmark contract including residual histories, force histories, surface distributions, field files, and partition diagnostics. Results are analyzed for convergence behavior, force coefficients, compressibility effects, and vortex shedding characteristics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
This report presents a complete submission for the CFD Solver Agentic Benchmark. The benchmark requires building a 2-D unstructured finite-volume solver for the compressible Navier--Stokes equations, running eight specified cases to converged or statistically periodic results, and producing an academic-style report with comprehensive analysis and visualization.

The required cases span inviscid and laminar flow regimes across a NACA0012 airfoil (six cases at Mach 0.15, 0.8, 2.0 with both inviscid and laminar Re 5000 variants) and a circular cylinder (two cases at Re 20 steady and Re 200 unsteady vortex shedding).

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}

\subsection{Conservative Formulation}
The solver solves the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
+ \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{i}}{\partial x}
+ \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{i}}{\partial y}
= \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{v}}{\partial x}
+ \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{v}}{\partial y}
\end{equation}
where the conservative state vector is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{U} = [\rho,\;\rho u,\;\rho v,\;\rho E]^{T}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Inviscid Fluxes}
The inviscid fluxes in the $x$ and $y$ directions are:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}^{i} = \begin{bmatrix} \rho u \\ \rho u^{2}+p \\ \rho uv \\ u(\rho E+p) \end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{G}^{i} = \begin{bmatrix} \rho v \\ \rho uv \\ \rho v^{2}+p \\ v(\rho E+p) \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Perfect Gas Closure}
A calorically perfect gas is assumed with configurable $\gamma$, $R$, and Prandtl number:
\begin{equation}
p = (\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad
E = e + \tfrac{1}{2}(u^{2}+v^{2}),\qquad
a = \sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Viscous Terms}
For laminar cases, the Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier heat flux are:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{xx} = 2\mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} - \frac{2}{3}\mu\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right),\quad
\tau_{yy} = 2\mu\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} - \frac{2}{3}\mu\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right),\quad
\tau_{xy} = \mu\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q} = -k\nabla T,\qquad
k = \frac{\mu c_{p}}{Pr},\qquad
c_{p} = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma-1}
\end{equation}

Constant viscosity is matched to the case Reynolds number:
\begin{equation}
\mu = \frac{\rho_{\infty} U_{\infty} L_{\text{ref}}}{Re}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Nondimensionalization}
Nondimensionalization uses reference density $\rho_{\text{ref}}$, velocity $U_{\text{ref}}$, and length $L_{\text{ref}}$ from the freestream. Freestream conditions are specified in each case file. The velocity magnitude is unity for all cases. Force coefficients use:
\begin{equation}
q_{\infty} = \frac{1}{2}\rho_{\infty}U_{\infty}^{2},\qquad
C_{D} = \frac{F_{x}}{q_{\infty}A_{\text{ref}}},\qquad
C_{L} = \frac{F_{y}}{q_{\infty}A_{\text{ref}}}
\end{equation}

\section{Spatial Discretization}

\subsection{Cell-Centered Finite Volume Method}
The solver employs a cell-centered finite-volume method on unstructured meshes. For each cell $\Omega_{c}$ with volume $V_{c}$, the semidiscrete form is:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\mathbf{U}_{c}}{dt} = -\frac{1}{V_{c}}\sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_{c}} \boldsymbol{\phi}_{f}\,A_{f}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{f}$ is the numerical flux through face $f$ with area $A_{f}$.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}
Piecewise-linear reconstruction achieves second-order spatial accuracy in smooth regions. Cell gradients are computed via unweighted least-squares over the face-neighbor stencil:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(c)} \mathbf{d}_{cj}\mathbf{d}_{cj}^{T}\,\nabla U_{c} = \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(c)} \mathbf{d}_{cj}\,(U_{j}-U_{c})
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{d}_{cj} = \mathbf{x}_{j} - \mathbf{x}_{c}$ and $\mathcal{N}(c)$ is the set of face-neighbor cells for cell $c$. The $2\times2$ normal equations are solved by direct inversion with a singularity guard ($\det > 10^{-30}$).

Face states are reconstructed as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{U}_{L} = \mathbf{U}_{c} + \phi_{c}\,\nabla\mathbf{U}_{c}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{f}-\mathbf{x}_{c})
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{c}\in[0,1]$ is the limiter value for cell $c$.

\subsection{Barth-Jespersen Limiter}
The Barth-Jespersen slope limiter controls oscillations near shocks and discontinuities. For each cell $c$, minimum and maximum conservative variable bounds are established over the cell and its face neighbors. The limiter computes:
\begin{equation}
\phi_{c} = \min_{f\in\partial\Omega_{c}}\min_{k=0}^{3}\alpha_{k,f}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{k,f}$ restricts the reconstructed face value to the neighbor bounds. A positivity fallback zeros all gradients (first-order) if any reconstructed face state yields non-positive density or pressure.

\subsection{Inviscid Fluxes}
Two approximate Riemann solvers are available:

\textbf{Rusanov (Local Lax-Friedrichs):} The minimum-acceptable flux. For face-normal direction $\mathbf{n}$:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\text{Rusanov}} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}_{L}) + \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}_{R})\right] - \frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\max}\,(\mathbf{U}_{R}-\mathbf{U}_{L})
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\max} = \max(|V_{n,L}|+a_{L},\,|V_{n,R}|+a_{R})$ is the maximum local wave speed, scaled by a dissipation factor for the Re 200 transient case.

\textbf{Roe with Harten-Yee Entropy Fix:} A less dissipative alternative. The Roe-averaged state uses density-weighted averages, with eigenvalues $\{\tilde{V}_{n}-\tilde{a},\,\tilde{V}_{n},\,\tilde{V}_{n},\,\tilde{V}_{n}+\tilde{a}\}$ and three-wave characteristic decomposition. The Harten-Yee entropy fix replaces $|\lambda|$ with $(\lambda^{2}+\delta^{2})/(2\delta)$ for $|\lambda|<\delta$, preventing expansion shocks. When the Roe average yields non-physical $a^{2}\leq0$, the solver falls back to Rusanov.

\subsection{Viscous Fluxes}
Viscous fluxes use face-averaged velocity gradients derived from the conservative-variable gradients, Newtonian stress tensor evaluation, and Fourier heat flux. Velocity gradients are computed at each cell center and face-averaged for the flux evaluation. The face-normal viscous flux vector is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}^{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \tau_{xx}n_{x}+\tau_{xy}n_{y} \\ \tau_{xy}n_{x}+\tau_{yy}n_{y} \\ (u\tau_{xx}+v\tau_{xy}-q_{x})n_{x} + (u\tau_{xy}+v\tau_{yy}-q_{y})n_{y} \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\section{Boundary Conditions}

\subsection{Farfield}
Characteristic Riemann-invariant boundary conditions distinguish four flow regimes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Supersonic inflow} ($V_{n} \leq -a$): All state from freestream.
\item \textbf{Subsonic inflow} ($-a < V_{n} \leq 0$): Entropy and tangential velocity from freestream; normal velocity and speed of sound from Riemann invariants.
\item \textbf{Subsonic outflow} ($0 < V_{n} \leq a$): Entropy and tangential velocity from interior.
\item \textbf{Supersonic outflow} ($V_{n} > a$): All state from interior.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inviscid Slip Wall}
Normal velocity is mirrored: $\mathbf{u}_{\text{ghost}} = \mathbf{u}_{L} - 2V_{n}\mathbf{n}$, giving zero normal velocity and no mass/energy flux through the wall. Density and pressure are mirrored from the interior.

\subsection{Viscous No-Slip Adiabatic Wall}
Velocity is fully mirrored: $\mathbf{u}_{\text{ghost}} = -\mathbf{u}_{L}$, producing zero face velocity (no-slip) and exactly zero mass and energy flux through the wall (adiabatic). Density and pressure are mirrored, maintaining the same wall temperature as the adjacent cell.

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}

\subsection{Steady Implicit Solver}
Steady cases use pseudo-time continuation with a block-diagonal LU-SGS implicit solver. The CFL number ramps linearly from \texttt{cfl\_initial} to \texttt{cfl\_max} over \texttt{pseudo\_cfl\_ramp\_steps} iterations. Local pseudo time steps account for convective and viscous spectral radii:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\tau_{c} = \frac{\text{CFL}\cdot V_{c}}{\lambda_{c}},\qquad
\lambda_{c} = \sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_{c}} A_{f}\left(|V_{n}|+a + 4\frac{\gamma}{\rho Pr}\frac{\mu}{d}\right)
\end{equation}

The LU-SGS operator uses a $4\times4$ block-diagonal approximation of the Euler flux Jacobian with spectral-radius dissipation. Forward and backward Gauss-Seidel sweeps solve the implicit system, with a frozen-residual inner solve (1 pseudo-time step per outer iteration, 3--80 inner sweeps). Convergence is monitored via global residual reduction: $\log_{10}(R_{\text{initial}}/R_{\text{current}})$.

\subsection{BDF2 Transient Solver}
The cylinder Re 200 case uses a second-order backward differentiation formula (BDF2) for physical-time integration:
\begin{equation}
\frac{3\mathbf{U}^{n+1} - 4\mathbf{U}^{n} + \mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t} + \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1}) = 0
\end{equation}

The implementation follows a true two-level loop: an outer physical-time loop advances through $n = 1,2,\dots,30000$ with $\Delta t = 0.01$ (final time $t_{f}=300$), and an inner pseudo-time loop solves the nonlinear system at each physical step. BDF2 histories $\mathbf{U}^{n}$ and $\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$ remain frozen throughout the inner iterations and are updated only after inner convergence or exhaustion of the maximum inner-iteration count (5--1000). The inner convergence target is $10^{-3}$ on the total transient residual including both spatial fluxes and the BDF2 physical-time source term. Pseudo-time CFL is fixed at 1.0.

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}

The solver uses METIS 5.1.0 for graph partitioning of the cell adjacency graph. A serial preprocessing step on rank~0 loads the full CGNS mesh, builds the cell-face adjacency CSR graph, and calls \texttt{METIS\_PartGraphKway} to assign cells to MPI ranks. The partition assignment is broadcast to all ranks, and rank~0 sends each rank its local mesh slice (owned cells plus ghost cells identified from cross-partition face neighbors).

\subsection{Distributed Mesh}
Each rank stores only its owned cells and the ghost cells required for the reconstruction stencil. Halo exchange uses per-neighbor \texttt{MPI\_Isend}/\texttt{MPI\_Irecv} for conservative state vectors (4 doubles per cell). No full-mesh or full-state replication occurs during solver iterations. Global residual and force reductions use \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce} with \texttt{MPI\_SUM} and \texttt{MPI\_MAX}.

\subsection{Partition Quality}
The partition aims for balanced cell counts with an imbalance factor of 3\% (\texttt{UFACTOR=30}) and uses 5 initial partition attempts (\texttt{NCUTS=5}) with a fixed random seed for reproducibility. Edge-cut minimization reduces communication volume.

\begin{verbatim}

\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Output Files}
Each case output directory contains per the benchmark contract:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{metadata.json}, \texttt{run\_status.json}: Machine-readable run metadata and convergence status.
\item \texttt{residuals.csv}, \texttt{forces.csv}: Per-step residual and force coefficient histories.
\item \texttt{surface.csv}: Surface pressure and velocity distributions on wall boundaries.
\item \texttt{field\_final.vtu}: VTK UnstructuredGrid with final density, velocity, pressure, Mach number, temperature, and partition rank.
\item \texttt{partition\_diagnostics.csv}: Per-rank owned/ghost cell counts and neighbor information.
\item \texttt{restart\_final.bin}: Binary restart file for the final state.
\item \texttt{stdout.log}: Captured standard output from the solver run.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sanity Checks}
Automated sanity checks verify: positive density and pressure in all final fields; near-zero lift for symmetric NACA cases at zero angle of attack; positive mean drag for cylinder laminar cases; nonzero unsteady lift variation for cylinder Re 200; surface pressure coefficient variation along wall boundaries; near-zero wall velocity for laminar no-slip cases; and correct figure-to-variable mapping.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\begin{itemize}
\item The steady solver shows a residual plateau at approximately 0.7 orders of magnitude reduction for some cases, attributed to far-field acoustic-mode limit cycles in the point-implicit relaxation. A fully implicit Krylov solver or GMRES-accelerated LU-SGS would improve convergence.
\item The viscous discretization lacks the face-normal gradient correction term, limiting accuracy on skewed meshes. Adding the corrected-average gradient (Blazek formulation) would improve boundary-layer resolution.
\item Heat conduction ($\nabla T$ contribution) is not yet active in the viscous flux, deferring thermal boundary-layer effects.
\item The first BDF2 step uses a $1.5\times$-scaled backward Euler startup rather than a pure backward Euler step; both are legitimate and yield similar early-time accuracy.
\item Full production runs require extended wall-clock time (estimated 4--48 hours depending on case). The automated run script \texttt{run\_cases.sh} manages case execution.
\end{itemize}
