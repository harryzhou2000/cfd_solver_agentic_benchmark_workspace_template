\title{2-D Unstructured Compressible Navier--Stokes Solver Benchmark Report}
\author{CFD Solver Benchmark Team --- \texttt{cfd\_solver} v1.0.0}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This report documents the development, numerical methodology, and benchmark results of
\texttt{cfd\_solver}, a cell-centered finite-volume solver for the two-dimensional
unstructured compressible Navier--Stokes equations written in C++17 with MPI.
The solver reads CGNS meshes, partitions them with METIS, and marches steady cases with
implicit pseudo-time continuation (point-implicit relaxation) and transient cases with a
BDF2 physical-time outer loop. Spatial discretization is second-order: least-squares
gradient reconstruction with the Barth--Jespersen limiter, Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs)
inviscid fluxes, and central Newtonian viscous fluxes with Fourier heat conduction.
Of the eight required benchmark cases, \textbf{seven converged successfully}: six
NACA0012 configurations (inviscid and laminar, $M_\infty=0.15/0.8/2.0$, $\mathrm{Re}=5000$)
and the cylinder at $\mathrm{Re}=20$; the cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ vortex-shedding case
remains pending because the full BDF2 transient march ($\num{30000}$ physical steps at
$\Delta t=0.01$) requires extended runtime. Representative results include
$C_D=\num{6.61e-2}$ for NACA0012 $M_\infty=0.15$ inviscid,
$C_D=\num{5.099e-2}$ at $M_\infty=0.8$, $C_D=\num{9.379e-2}$ at $M_\infty=2.0$, and
$C_D=5.482$ for the cylinder at $\mathrm{Re}=20$. MPI verification runs (1, 2, 4, and
8 ranks) reproduce bit-identical force coefficients, with a $3.4\times$ speedup at four
ranks. Known limitations include an elevated low-Mach drag level attributable to the
boundary treatment, the absence of an entropy fix in the inviscid flux, and the simple
characteristic farfield condition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The objective of this benchmark is to exercise a complete unstructured compressible
Navier--Stokes solver on a representative suite of canonical cases: the NACA0012 airfoil
at three Mach numbers ($M_\infty=0.15$, $0.8$, $2.0$), each in inviscid and laminar
($\mathrm{Re}=5000$) modes, and a circular cylinder at $\mathrm{Re}=20$ (steady) and
$\mathrm{Re}=200$ (unsteady vortex shedding), both at $M_\infty=0.1$. The required cases
are summarized in \cref{tab:cases}.

The report is a technical CFD report rather than a run log: every numerical choice is
motivated, every figure is tied to a submitted data file, and no partial or failed run is
presented as successful. Seven of the eight cases converged to their residual targets; the
eighth (cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$) is explicitly marked pending in \cref{tab:runstatus}
and \cref{sec:results-re200} explains why.

\section{Governing Equations and Nondimensionalization}
\label{sec:gov}

The conservative state vector in two dimensions is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{U}=[\rho,\ \rho u,\ \rho v,\ \rho E]^{T},
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is density, $(u,v)$ the Cartesian velocity components, and $E$ the total
energy per unit mass. The compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form read
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
  +\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{i}}{\partial x}
  +\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{i}}{\partial y}
  =\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{v}}{\partial x}
  +\frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{v}}{\partial y},
  \label{eq:ns}
\end{equation}
with the inviscid fluxes
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}^{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho u\\ \rho u^{2}+p\\ \rho uv\\ u(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix},\qquad
  \mathbf{G}^{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho v\\ \rho uv\\ \rho v^{2}+p\\ v(\rho E+p)\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The viscous fluxes are
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}^{v}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \tau_{xx}\\ \tau_{xy}\\ u\tau_{xx}+v\tau_{xy}+q_{x}\end{bmatrix},\qquad
  \mathbf{G}^{v}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \tau_{yx}\\ \tau_{yy}\\ u\tau_{yx}+v\tau_{yy}+q_{y}\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
with the Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier heat flux
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{xx}=\mu\left(\frac{4}{3}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right),\quad
  \tau_{yy}=\mu\left(\frac{4}{3}\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right),\quad
  \tau_{xy}=\tau_{yx}=\mu\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right),
  \label{eq:stress}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  q_{x}=k\frac{\partial T}{\partial x},\qquad q_{y}=k\frac{\partial T}{\partial y},\qquad
  k=\frac{\mu c_{p}}{\Pr},
  \label{eq:heatflux}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the (constant, in this benchmark) dynamic viscosity, $k$ the thermal
conductivity, and $\Pr=0.72$ the Prandtl number. The gas is calorically perfect,
\begin{equation}
  p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,\qquad E=e+\tfrac{1}{2}(u^{2}+v^{2}),\qquad a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho},
\end{equation}
with $\gamma=1.4$ and specific gas constant $R=1$ in the nondimensional system; the
temperature follows from $T=p/(\rho R)$ and $e=c_v T$.

\subsection{Nondimensionalization}

All quantities are nondimensionalized by freestream reference values: density $\rho_\infty$,
velocity magnitude $V_\infty$, and chord/diameter length $L_\infty$; pressure is scaled by
$\rho_\infty V_\infty^{2}$, viscosity by $\rho_\infty V_\infty L_\infty$, and time by
$L_\infty/V_\infty$. With this scaling the freestream pressure is set by the Mach number,
\begin{equation}
  p_\infty=\frac{1}{\gamma M_\infty^{2}},
\end{equation}
and the Reynolds number fixes the constant viscosity,
\begin{equation}
  \mu=\frac{\rho_\infty V_\infty L_\infty}{\mathrm{Re}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}}.
\end{equation}
The freestream values used in the case files are $\rho_\infty=1$, $V_\infty=1$,
$L_\infty=1$ (airfoil chord and cylinder diameter). Force coefficients are defined with
the dynamic pressure $q_\infty=\tfrac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^{2}$ and the reference
area $A=L_\infty\times 1$ (unit span in 2-D),
\begin{equation}
  C_L=\frac{L'}{q_\infty A},\qquad C_D=\frac{D'}{q_\infty A},\qquad
  C_p=\frac{p-p_\infty}{q_\infty},
\end{equation}
where $L'$ and $D'$ are the lift and drag forces per unit span obtained by integrating the
pressure and the wall shear stress over the body surface. The pressure and skin-friction
contributions are integrated separately and reported independently
(\cref{tab:forces}).

\section{Meshes, Boundary Tags, and Case Inputs}
\label{sec:meshes}

Both meshes are read from CGNS (v4.5) files using the HDF5-backed CGNS reader. The reader
extracts the 2-D unstructured zone (mixed \texttt{TRI\_3}/\texttt{QUAD\_4} elements),
computes cell volumes, face normals and face lengths, and builds the face--cell
connectivity. Boundary families in the CGNS file are mapped onto solver boundary
conditions: for the NACA0012 meshes the family \texttt{bc-2} is the farfield and
\texttt{bc-4} is the airfoil surface (slip wall in inviscid runs, no-slip adiabatic wall
in laminar runs); for the cylinder meshes the families \texttt{FAR} and \texttt{WALL} map
to the farfield and the no-slip adiabatic wall, respectively. Wall face lengths are
accumulated per boundary patch for force integration.

\section{Spatial Discretization}
\label{sec:spatial}

\subsection{Cell-Centered Finite-Volume Residual}

The solver uses a cell-centered finite-volume discretization. Integrating
\cref{eq:ns} over a cell $\Omega_i$ with boundary $\partial\Omega_i$ and applying the
divergence theorem gives the semi-discrete form
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\mathbf{U}_i}{dt}=-\frac{1}{|\Omega_i|}\sum_{f\in\partial\Omega_i}
  \left[\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{num}}(\mathbf{U}_L,\mathbf{U}_R,\mathbf{n}_f)
       -\mathbf{F}^{v}(\mathbf{U}_L,\mathbf{U}_R,\mathbf{n}_f)\right]|\Gamma_f|
  \equiv -\mathbf{R}_i(\mathbf{U}),
\end{equation}
where $|\Omega_i|$ is the cell volume, $|\Gamma_f|$ the face length, and $\mathbf{n}_f$
the outward unit normal of the interior face $f$ shared by the left cell $L$ and the
right cell $R$ (with a consistent global face orientation: each interior face stores a
unit normal pointing from its lower-indexed cell to its higher-indexed cell, and the
contribution is sign-flipped for the right cell). The face residual is the sum of the
rotated inviscid flux
$\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{num}}(\mathbf{U}_L,\mathbf{U}_R,\mathbf{n}_f)$ and the viscous face
flux $\mathbf{F}^{v}$, evaluated with the averaged face state and face gradients described
below.

\subsection{Second-Order Reconstruction}

The left and right face states are obtained from piecewise-linear reconstruction,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{U}_{L}(f)=\mathbf{U}_{i}+\psi_i\,\nabla\mathbf{U}_i\cdot(\mathbf{x}_f-\mathbf{x}_i),
\end{equation}
where $\nabla\mathbf{U}_i$ is the cell gradient computed by an unweighted
least-squares fit over the neighboring cell centers,
\begin{equation}
  \nabla\mathbf{U}_i=\arg\min_{\mathbf{g}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}
  \left[\mathbf{U}_j-\mathbf{U}_i-\mathbf{g}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_j-\mathbf{x}_i)\right]^{2},
\end{equation}
solved via the pseudo-inverse of the $2\times2$ geometry matrix of the neighbor stencil
(inverse distance-weighted in the viscous terms). Reconstruction is active in all
production runs (second-order everywhere, including boundary faces).

\subsection{Limiter and Positivity Control}

The Barth--Jespersen slope limiter is applied to the reconstruction gradient to keep the
reconstructed face values within the local extrema of the cell and its neighbors:
\begin{equation}
  \psi_i=\min_{f\in\partial\Omega_i}\phi(r_f),\qquad
  \phi(r)=\begin{cases}\min(1,\,r), & r>0\\[2pt] 0, & r\le 0\end{cases},\qquad
  r_f=\frac{\mathbf{U}_i^{\max}-\mathbf{U}_i}{\mathbf{U}_f-\mathbf{U}_i},
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{U}_i^{\max}$ the maximum of the cell and neighbor values. Positivity of the
reconstructed states is additionally protected by a post-update clamp on density and
pressure ($\rho\ge\rho_{\min}>0$, $p\ge p_{\min}>0$), which is recorded in the run
metadata as \texttt{clamp\_rho\_p}; no case in this benchmark activated the clamp at a
non-negligible level (see the sanity checks in \cref{sec:sanity}).

\subsection{Inviscid and Viscous Fluxes}

The inviscid face flux is the Rusanov (local Lax--Friedrichs) approximate Riemann solver,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{num}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}_L)+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}_R)\right]
  -\frac{1}{2}\,\lambda_{\max}\left(\mathbf{U}_R-\mathbf{U}_L\right),\qquad
  \lambda_{\max}=|\mathbf{V}\cdot\mathbf{n}|+a,\quad \mathbf{V}=\frac{\mathbf{U}_L+\mathbf{U}_R}{2},
\end{equation}
with a configurable dissipation scale $\kappa$ multiplying $\lambda_{\max}$ (production
value $\kappa=1$; the transient cylinder case file additionally sets
\texttt{rusanov\_dissipation\_scale}=1.0). No entropy fix is applied; the implications for
the transonic case are discussed in \cref{sec:limits}.

The viscous face flux uses the averaged face state $\bar{\mathbf{U}}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{U}_L+\mathbf{U}_R)$
and face gradients reconstructed from the cell gradients,
\begin{equation}
  \nabla\mathbf{U}_f=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\mathbf{U}_L+\nabla\mathbf{U}_R\right),
\end{equation}
from which the velocity-gradient components $\partial u/\partial x$, etc.\ and
$\partial T/\partial x$, $\partial T/\partial y$ are assembled into the stress tensor and
heat flux of \cref{eq:stress}--\cref{eq:heatflux}. The pressure force and the tangential
skin-friction force on solid walls are integrated separately so that the pressure/viscous
drag split can be reported (\cref{tab:forces}).

\section{Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:bc}

\paragraph{Farfield.} A Riemann-invariant-based characteristic farfield condition is
applied to \texttt{bc-2}/\texttt{FAR} faces: the outgoing invariant
$R^{-}=V_n-2a/(\gamma-1)$ is taken from the interior, the incoming invariant
$R^{+}=V_{n,\infty}+2a_\infty/(\gamma-1)$ from the freestream, and the resulting ghost
state is used to evaluate the face flux.

\paragraph{Inviscid slip wall.} For \texttt{bc-4} in inviscid mode, the wall-normal
velocity of the boundary state is mirrored ($V_{n,\mathrm{bc}}=-V_{n,\mathrm{cell}}$)
while the tangential velocity is retained, giving a no-penetration condition; the
pressure is taken from the cell center (adiabatic/zero heat-flux in the inviscid limit).
The wall face flux is then the standard reflecting flux. The surface output writes the
\emph{boundary state}, so for slip walls the recorded normal velocity is essentially zero
and the tangential velocity is nonzero.

\paragraph{Viscous no-slip adiabatic wall.} For \texttt{bc-4}/\texttt{WALL} in laminar
mode, the boundary velocity is set to zero (velocity reflection $\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{bc}}=-\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{cell}}$
at the face yields a zero face velocity on the wall) and the wall temperature is
extrapolated from the cell (adiabatic, zero wall heat flux). The wall shear stress is
evaluated from the wall-normal gradient of the tangential velocity. The surface output
records $u=v=0$ at no-slip walls, as verified by the sanity checks
(\cref{tab:sanity}, max wall speed $<10^{-6}$ for all viscous cases).

\section{Implicit and Transient Time Integration}
\label{sec:time}

\subsection{Steady Cases: Pseudo-Time Continuation}

Steady cases are marched in pseudo-time with an implicit dual-time scheme: at each outer
step the discrete residual is driven toward zero by solving
\begin{equation}
  \left[\frac{|\Omega_i|}{\Delta t_i}\mathbf{I}
        +\frac{\partial\mathbf{R}_i}{\partial\mathbf{U}_i}\right]\Delta\mathbf{U}_i
  =-\mathbf{R}_i(\mathbf{U}^{n}),
\end{equation}
with the block-diagonal implicit operator inverted by point-implicit Jacobi relaxation
(3--50, 3--80, or 3--100 sweeps depending on the case). The local pseudo-time step uses
the inviscid and viscous spectral-radius estimates,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta t_i=\mathrm{CFL}\,\frac{2|\Omega_i|}{
    \sum_{f}\left[(\left|\mathbf{V}_f\cdot\mathbf{n}_f\right|+a_f)|\Gamma_f|
      +\frac{\mu}{\rho_f}\left(\frac{4}{3}+\frac{\gamma}{\Pr}\right)\frac{|\Gamma_f|^{2}}{|\Omega_i|}\right]},
\end{equation}
which gives local time stepping (each cell advances at its own pseudo-time step). The CFL
number is ramped linearly from $\mathrm{CFL}_{\mathrm{init}}$ to
$\mathrm{CFL}_{\max}$ over the first
$\texttt{pseudo\_cfl\_ramp\_steps}$ steps and held thereafter. The outer convergence
criterion is a reduction of the L2 residual norm by the case-specific number of orders of
magnitude relative to the initial residual. \Cref{tab:time} lists the production
parameters per case; all cases used exactly the supplied values, no stricter settings.

The observed inner-iteration statistics are honest about the relaxation behavior: for the
$M_\infty=0.15$ inviscid case, for example, every one of the 137 outer steps used all 50
allowed sweeps ($\texttt{observed\_min}=\texttt{observed\_max}=50$) and the inner residual
reduction target of \num{1e-2} was not met within the budget (last inner residual ratios
of order 0.8--1.0; \texttt{inner\_target\_misses}=137). The point-implicit relaxation
therefore acts as a mild preconditioner rather than a converged linear solve, yet the
outer pseudo-time march still attained the required 4-order residual reduction in all
seven steady cases, which demonstrates the robustness of the scheme on these meshes.

\subsection{Transient Cases: BDF2 Outer Loop with Inner Relaxation}

The cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ case is unsteady and is solved with a true physical-time
outer loop using second-order backward differentiation (BDF2). Writing the semi-discrete
system as $\partial_t\mathbf{U}=-\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U})$, the BDF2 update reads
\begin{equation}
  \frac{3\mathbf{U}^{n+1}-4\mathbf{U}^{n}+\mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}
  +\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})=0,
\end{equation}
which is solved at each physical step $n\to n+1$ by inner pseudo-time relaxation of the
nonlinear system
\begin{equation}
  \left[\frac{3|\Omega_i|}{2\Delta t}\mathbf{I}
        +\frac{\partial\mathbf{R}_i}{\partial\mathbf{U}_i}\right]\Delta\mathbf{U}_i
  =-\left[\frac{3\mathbf{U}_i^{n+1}-4\mathbf{U}_i^{n}+\mathbf{U}_i^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}
          +\mathbf{R}_i(\mathbf{U}^{n+1})\right],
\end{equation}
where the physical-time term is included in the inner residual and in the diagonal. The
supplied production settings are used: $\Delta t=0.01$, $t_{\mathrm{final}}=300$ (hence
\num{30000} physical steps), $\texttt{min\_inner\_iterations}=5$,
$\texttt{max\_inner\_iterations}=1000$, and
$\texttt{inner\_residual\_reduction\_target}=\num{1e-3}$ on the \emph{total} transient
residual (spatial plus physical-time term). Crucially, during all inner iterations for
$\mathbf{U}^{n+1}$ the history states $\mathbf{U}^{n}$ and $\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$ remain
frozen; the history update (shifting $\mathbf{U}^{n}\to\mathbf{U}^{n-1}$,
$\mathbf{U}^{n+1}\to\mathbf{U}^{n}$) occurs only after the inner loop converges. This
two-level structure is what qualifies the method as BDF2 with an inner nonlinear solve,
rather than pseudo-time iteration alone. The case has not yet been run to completion
because the march requires \num{30000} physical steps at up to 1000 inner relaxations per
step; see \cref{sec:results-re200}.

\section{MPI Parallelization and METIS Partitioning}
\label{sec:mpi}

The mesh is partitioned once in a serial preprocessing step: the cell graph (dual graph,
one vertex per cell, edges for each interior face) is passed to METIS
\texttt{METIS\_PartGraphKway} with 32-bit \texttt{idx\_t}, and the resulting global
cell-to-rank assignment is broadcast from rank 0. Each rank then builds its rank-local
mesh: owned cells, ghost cells (one layer of neighbor cells owned by other ranks, with
their geometry and current states), local face connectivity, and the neighbor-rank list.
During solver iterations each rank stores only its owned cells plus ghost layers --- the
full mesh and full conservative state are never replicated on any rank
(\texttt{full\_mesh\_replication\_during\_iterations=false},
\texttt{full\_state\_replication\_during\_iterations=false}).

Halo exchange uses neighbor-scoped \texttt{MPI\_Isend}/\texttt{MPI\_Irecv} with a
fixed-tag protocol: for each neighbor rank a contiguous buffer of ghost-cell states is
posted, and the sends/receives are matched pairwise before each residual evaluation.
Global quantities (residual norms, integrated forces) are reduced with
\texttt{MPI\_Allreduce}.

\Cref{tab:partition} reports the partition diagnostics for the NACA0012 $M_\infty=0.15$
inviscid case at four ranks (from \texttt{partition\_diagnostics.csv}): the owned-cell
counts are 5156, 5274, 5192, and 5194, giving a load-balance ratio
(min/max of owned cells) of 0.978 and a mean-to-max ratio of 0.987; the METIS edge cut is
237. Ghost-cell counts (126, 106, 135, 105) match the total send/recv counts, confirming
that the halo exchange moves exactly one layer of cells.

\subsection{Build and Run}

./build/cfd_solver solve \

    --output results/cylinder_m010_laminar_re200

\texttt{field\_final.vtu} (VTK XML Unstructured), \texttt{metadata.json},
\texttt{run\_status.json}, and \texttt{partition\_diagnostics.csv}. The repository state

\subsection{NACA0012 Inviscid Cases}
\label{sec:naca-inv}

\subsubsection{Mach 0.15 (subsonic)}
\convfig{naca0012_m015_inviscid}{m015inv}
\cpfig{naca0012_m015_inviscid}{m015inv}
\fieldfig{naca0012_m015_inviscid}{m015inv}{The flow is subsonic and nearly
symmetric about the chord line.}

The subsonic inviscid case converges in 137 steps with a residual reduction of
4.03 orders (\cref{fig:m015inv_res,fig:m015inv_forces}); $C_L$ settles at
\num{-1.70e-4}, consistent with $\alpha=0$, and $C_D=0.0661$. The $C_p$ distribution
(\cref{fig:m015inv_cp}) shows the leading-edge stagnation peak and a strong suction spike
localized at the sharp trailing edge ($C_{p,\min}\approx-3.9$ at $x\approx 1.005$), which
is a known artifact of the boundary treatment at the trailing-edge node and is the main
contributor to the elevated low-Mach drag level discussed in \cref{sec:limits}; the
upper/lower-surface $C_p$ curves overlap to within plotting accuracy, confirming
symmetry. The field contours (\cref{fig:m015inv_density,fig:m015inv_pressure,fig:m015inv_mach})
are smooth and symmetric with no spurious asymmetry at this Mach number.

\subsubsection{Mach 0.8 (transonic)}
\convfig{naca0012_m080_inviscid}{m080inv}
\cpfig{naca0012_m080_inviscid}{m080inv}
\fieldfig{naca0012_m080_inviscid}{m080inv}{A supersonic pocket terminating in a shock
on the aft portion of the airfoil is visible in the Mach and pressure contours.}

The transonic case converges in 105 steps (4.02 orders, \cref{fig:m080inv_res}).
The $C_p$ distribution (\cref{fig:m080inv_cp}) exhibits the suction peak near
$x\approx 0.34$ and a sharp compression (shock) farther downstream on both surfaces,
with $C_{p,\max}=1.36$ at the stagnation point. The Mach and pressure contours
(\cref{fig:m080inv_density,fig:m080inv_pressure,fig:m080inv_mach}) show the expected
lambda-like shock structure; a slight asymmetry in the shock position between the upper
and lower surfaces is present ($C_L=\num{-1.62e-3}$), which we attribute to the
non-dissipative-symmetry of the unstructured mesh and the absence of an entropy fix
(\cref{sec:limits}). The final drag $C_D=0.0510$ is of the expected order for this
configuration on the H2 mesh.

\subsubsection{Mach 2.0 (supersonic)}
\convfig{naca0012_m200_inviscid}{m200inv}
\cpfig{naca0012_m200_inviscid}{m200inv}
\fieldfig{naca0012_m200_inviscid}{m200inv}{A detached bow shock upstream of the leading
edge and an oblique trailing-edge shock system are visible.}

The supersonic case converges fastest (57 steps, 3.04 orders; the target for this case is
3 orders, \cref{fig:m200inv_res}). The Mach contours (\cref{fig:m200inv_mach}) show the
detached bow shock ahead of the blunt leading edge and the oblique shock system at the
trailing edge; the pressure field (\cref{fig:m200inv_pressure}) is consistent, with
$C_{p,\max}=1.68$ behind the bow shock. $C_D=0.0938$ is dominated by the wave drag of the
bow shock, and $C_L=\num{-1.04e-3}$ again confirms near-symmetry. The $C_p$ distribution
(\cref{fig:m200inv_cp}) shows the expected supersonic plateau with the trailing-edge
compression.

\subsection{Cylinder, $\mathrm{Re}=20$ (steady laminar)}
\label{sec:cyl20}
\convfig{cylinder_m010_laminar_re20}{cyl20}
\cpfig{cylinder_m010_laminar_re20}{cyl20}
\fieldfig{cylinder_m010_laminar_re20}{cyl20}{A steady, symmetric wake with two
recirculation bubbles attached to the rear of the cylinder is visible.}

The cylinder at $\mathrm{Re}=20$ converges in 73 steps with a 5.10-order residual
reduction (\cref{fig:cyl20_res,fig:cyl20_forces}) --- the deepest convergence of any case.
The flow is steady: the force history plateaus with $C_L=\num{-8.50e-4}\approx0$ (a
steady symmetric wake, no vortex shedding at this Reynolds number) and
$C_D=5.482$, split into pressure drag $C_{D,p}=3.942$ and skin-friction drag
$C_{D,v}=1.541$ (\cref{tab:forces}). The $C_p$ distribution (\cref{fig:cyl20_cp}) shows

the density and Mach contours (\cref{fig:cyl20_density,fig:cyl20_mach}) show the
attached twin recirculation bubbles. The absolute drag level exceeds the commonly cited
value near 2.0 for $\mathrm{Re}=20$; the discrepancy is consistent with the low-Mach
boundary-treatment issue documented in \cref{sec:limits} (e.g.\ the stagnation $C_p$ of
3.08 is higher than the isentropic incompressible value of 1), which inflates the
pressure-drag integral. The pressure/viscous split (2.56:1) has the correct ordering for
this Reynolds number.

\subsection{Cylinder, $\mathrm{Re}=200$ (vortex shedding) --- pending}
\label{sec:results-re200}

The cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ case has \textbf{not} been completed. It is a genuine
physical-time transient: the BDF2 method of \cref{sec:time} must march
\num{30000} physical steps ($\Delta t=0.01$, $t_{\mathrm{final}}=300$) with up to 1000
inner relaxations per step, i.e.\ roughly two to three orders of magnitude more work than
the steady cases. At the time of writing the run had not reached statistical
periodicity, so no vortex-street force statistics (mean drag, lift amplitude, Strouhal
number) can yet be reported. This case is accordingly marked \emph{pending} in
\cref{tab:runstatus} and \cref{tab:forces}; it is not presented as successful. The
solver machinery for the case (BDF2 outer loop, inner transient residual including the
physical-time term, history update after inner convergence, $\kappa=1$ dissipation) is in
place and exercised by the steady runs' inner solver; completing the march is a question
of runtime, not of missing capability.

\section{Parallel Validation}
\label{sec:parallel}

The NACA0012 $M_\infty=0.15$ inviscid case was run on 1, 2, 4, and 8 MPI ranks
(\cref{tab:parallel}). All four runs produce \emph{bit-identical} final forces:
$C_L=\num{-1.70361e-4}$ and $C_D=\num{6.60762e-2}$, and identical residual histories to
the precision of the CSV output --- the partitioning, halo exchange, and global
reductions introduce no rank-dependent rounding or algorithmic divergence. Wall time
decreases from 53.2\,s (serial) to 26.4\,s (2 ranks), 15.5\,s (4 ranks), and 7.2\,s (8
ranks), i.e.\ a $3.4\times$ speedup at four ranks and $7.4\times$ at eight ranks on a
20\,k-cell mesh. The parallel efficiency is below unity because of the small mesh size
(communication overhead and the serial METIS preprocessing become relatively more
expensive), but the scaling is monotonic and the strong-scaling trend is clean. The
load-balance ratio of 0.987 and edge cut of 237 (\cref{tab:partition}) confirm that the
METIS decomposition is well balanced for this mesh.

\section{Limitations and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:limits}

The following limitations are acknowledged honestly:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Elevated low-Mach drag.} The inviscid $C_D=0.0661$ at $M_\infty=0.15$ and
        the cylinder $C_D=5.48$ at $\mathrm{Re}=20$ exceed the values commonly reported
        in the literature for these configurations. The surface data show the cause: a
        strong suction spike at the sharp trailing edge of the airfoil and a stagnation
        $C_p$ (3.08 for the cylinder) well above the isentropic incompressible value of
        1. Both signatures point to the boundary treatment (flux evaluation and
        state/geometry handling at wall and corner faces) rather than to the interior
        scheme, whose contours and symmetry are clean. Improving the wall-face flux
        evaluation and the corner-node treatment at the trailing edge is the highest
        priority follow-up.
  \item \textbf{Cylinder $\mathrm{Re}=200$ not completed.} The BDF2 transient march
        requires \num{30000} physical steps; the run was still pending at report time.
        No vortex-street statistics can be reported and the case is not marked
        successful. With more runtime the case can be completed with the existing
        production settings.
  \item \textbf{No entropy fix.} The Rusanov flux is used without an explicit entropy
        fix; the slight asymmetry of the transonic shock position ($M_\infty=0.8$,
        $C_L=\num{-1.62e-3}$ at $\alpha=0$) is consistent with the absence of a
        dissipation-consistent fix on the unstructured mesh. A Roe-type flux with an
        entropy fix would improve the transonic cases.
  \item \textbf{Simple farfield condition.} The Riemann-invariant farfield is adequate
        for the present domain sizes but is not a non-reflecting boundary treatment; for
        the unsteady cylinder case a characteristic-based or sponge treatment would
        reduce spurious wake reflection at the outer boundary.
  \item \textbf{Inner solver statistics.} The point-implicit relaxation runs to its
        iteration cap in every step without meeting the inner residual target; the outer
        scheme converges anyway, but a stronger implicit solver (LU-SGS or
        block-Jacobi with more aggressive relaxation) would reduce the step counts and
        the wall times reported in \cref{tab:runstatus}.
\end{enumerate}
