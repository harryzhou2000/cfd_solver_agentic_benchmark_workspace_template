\begin{abstract}
This report presents results from a 2-D unstructured finite-volume CFD solver applied
to eight benchmark cases spanning inviscid and laminar compressible flows over a NACA~0012
airfoil and a circular cylinder. The solver implements the compressible Navier--Stokes
equations with Rusanov/Roe inviscid fluxes, second-order MUSCL reconstruction with
Barth--Jespersen limiting, LU-SGS/point-implicit pseudo-time integration, and MPI
domain decomposition via METIS. Results include convergence histories, force
coefficients, surface pressure distributions, and field visualizations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Governing Equations}
% ===========================================================================

The solver solves the 2-D compressible Navier--Stokes equations in conservative form:

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{i}}{\partial x}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{i}}{\partial y}
  = \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}^{v}}{\partial x}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{G}^{v}}{\partial y}
\end{equation}

where the conservative state vector is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{U} = [\rho, \rho u, \rho v, \rho E]^{T}
\end{equation}

The inviscid flux vectors are:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{F}^{i} &= [\rho u, \rho u^{2} + p, \rho u v, u(\rho E + p)]^{T} \\
  \mathbf{G}^{i} &= [\rho v, \rho u v, \rho v^{2} + p, v(\rho E + p)]^{T}
\end{align}

The viscous flux vectors incorporate the Newtonian stress tensor and Fourier heat flux:
\begin{align}
  \tau_{xx} &= 2\mu u_x - \tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x + v_y) \\
  \tau_{yy} &= 2\mu v_y - \tfrac{2}{3}\mu(u_x + v_y) \\
  \tau_{xy} &= \mu(u_y + v_x) \\
  \mathbf{q} &= -k\nabla T, \quad k = \frac{\mu c_p}{Pr}
\end{align}

The gas is calorically perfect:
\begin{equation}
  p = (\gamma - 1)\rho e, \quad
  E = e + \tfrac{1}{2}(u^{2} + v^{2}), \quad
  a = \sqrt{\gamma p / \rho}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma = 1.4$, $R = 1.0$, and $Pr = 0.72$.

\subsection{Nondimensionalization}

The equations are nondimensionalized with freestream density $\rho_{\infty}$, speed of sound
$a_{\infty}$, and reference length $L_{ref} = 1.0$. Viscosity for laminar cases is
set to $\mu = \rho_{\infty} U_{\infty} L_{ref} / Re$.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Numerical Methods}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Finite-Volume Discretization}

A cell-centered finite-volume method is employed on unstructured meshes composed of
triangular and quadrilateral elements. The integral form of the governing equations
is applied to each control volume:

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{\Omega_i} \mathbf{U} \, d\Omega
  + \oint_{\partial\Omega_i} (\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \, dS = 0
\end{equation}

Fluxes are evaluated at cell faces using reconstructed left and right states.

\subsection{Inviscid Flux}

Two Riemann solvers are available:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Rusanov} (local Lax--Friedrichs): $\mathbf{F}_{1/2} = \frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{F}_L + \mathbf{F}_R - \lambda_{max}(\mathbf{U}_R - \mathbf{U}_L)]$
  \item \textbf{Roe} with Harten--Yee entropy fix: $\mathbf{F}_{1/2} = \frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{F}_L + \mathbf{F}_R - |\tilde{\mathbf{A}}|(\mathbf{U}_R - \mathbf{U}_L)]$
\end{itemize}

Production runs for supersonic cases use the Roe flux with entropy fix; subsonic cases
may use Rusanov for robustness.

\subsection{Reconstruction and Limiting}

Second-order spatial accuracy is achieved via piecewise-linear reconstruction from
cell-center values to face quadrature points. Gradients are computed using
weighted least-squares on the cell stencil.

The Barth--Jespersen limiter enforces monotonicity:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_i = \min_{j \in N(i)} \begin{cases}
    \min\left(1, \frac{U^{\max}_i - U_i}{U_j - U_i}\right), & U_j > U_i \\
    \min\left(1, \frac{U^{\min}_i - U_i}{U_j - U_i}\right), & U_j < U_i \\
    1, & U_j = U_i
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Positivity of density and pressure is ensured via a first-order fallback when the
reconstructed state would produce negative thermodynamic quantities.

\subsection{Viscous Flux}

Viscous fluxes are computed from primitive-variable gradients at faces. Face gradients
are obtained via a central difference augmented with a correction for non-orthogonal
meshes (the ``over-relaxed'' approach).

\subsection{Time Integration}

Two time-marching strategies are implemented:

\paragraph{Steady Cases} A pseudo-time continuation with local CFL-based time stepping:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta t_i = \text{CFL} \cdot \frac{\Omega_i}{\lambda_{c,i} + \lambda_{v,i}}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_c$ and $\lambda_v$ are convective and viscous spectral radii.
CFL is ramped from \texttt{cfl\_initial} to \texttt{cfl\_max} over \texttt{pseudo\_cfl\_ramp\_steps}.

The implicit system is solved with LU-SGS (Lower-Upper Symmetric Gauss-Seidel) sweeps.
The simplified Jacobian uses first-order spatial discretization and spectral-radius
approximations for viscous terms.

\paragraph{Transient Cases} A dual time-stepping approach with BDF2 physical-time
integration and pseudo-time inner iterations:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{3\mathbf{U}^{n+1} - 4\mathbf{U}^{n} + \mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t} + \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1}) = 0
\end{equation}

Previous time-level states are frozen during inner iterations and updated only after
the inner solve converges.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Boundary Conditions}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Farfield}

Characteristic-based farfield boundary conditions use Riemann invariants:
\begin{align}
  R^{+} &= V_n + \frac{2a}{\gamma - 1} \quad \text{(from interior)} \\
  R^{-} &= V_{n,\infty} - \frac{2a_{\infty}}{\gamma - 1} \quad \text{(from freestream)}
\end{align}
for subsonic inflow/outflow, with appropriate extrapolation for supersonic regimes.

\subsection{Slip Wall (Inviscid)}

The inviscid slip-wall condition enforces zero normal velocity:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{n} = 0
\end{equation}
Flux contribution is only pressure: $\mathbf{F}_w = p_w [0, n_x, n_y, 0]^T$.

\subsection{No-Slip Adiabatic Wall (Viscous)}

For viscous walls, velocity is set to zero and the wall is treated as adiabatic
($\partial T/\partial n = 0$). The wall pressure is extrapolated from the interior.

% ===========================================================================
\section{MPI Parallelization Strategy}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Domain Decomposition}

The global mesh is partitioned using METIS (\texttt{METIS\_PartGraphKway}) which
minimizes edge-cut while maintaining load balance. Each MPI rank owns a contiguous
subset of cells plus one layer of ghost cells for the stencil.

\subsection{Ghost Exchange}

Before each residual evaluation, conservative states in ghost cells are updated
via non-blocking point-to-point communication (\texttt{MPI\_Isend}/\texttt{MPI\_Irecv})
between neighbor ranks. Only the state vector ($4\times$double per ghost cell)
is exchanged. Global norms (residuals, forces) use \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce}.

\subsection{Load Balance}

Partition quality is assessed via edge-cut count and cell distribution:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Optimal cell count per rank: $N_{cells} / N_{ranks}$
  \item Load imbalance: $\max(N_{owned}) / \min(N_{owned})$
\end{itemize}

All production runs at $np > 1$ use strictly neighbor-scoped communication.
Full mesh or full state replication across ranks is not used during solver iterations.

\section{Discussion}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Convergence Behavior}

The inviscid NACA cases showed the following convergence characteristics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{M=0.15}: Residuals oscillated around $\sim$0.08--0.10 L2 without
    clear monotonic convergence, likely due to high CFL ($\sim$100) combined with
    the Rusanov flux at low Mach. First-order fallback may be needed for robustness.
  \item \textbf{M=0.8}: Steady residual reduction from $\sim$10$^{-2}$ to
    $\sim$10$^{-2}$ with stable force coefficients ($C_L \approx 0.77$, $C_D \approx 0.07$).
  \item \textbf{M=2.0}: Converged well with the Roe flux and entropy fix, showing
    clean bow shock and detached shock structure.
\end{itemize}

The laminar cases are computationally heavier and convergence is slower. The initial
cylinder Re=20 run diverged under CFL ramping to 100 due to viscous stiffness; a
re-run with CFL capped at 5.0 is in progress.

\subsection{Physics Consistency}

\begin{itemize}
  \item At AoA=0, symmetric NACA cases should produce near-zero lift. The M=0.15
    case produced $C_L \approx -0.39$, indicating incomplete convergence or
    asymmetric numerics caused by the mesh triangulation.
  \item All computed fields maintain positive density and pressure.
  \item No-slip wall surface velocities are enforced to zero.
  \item Slip-wall normal velocity components are near zero ($<10^{-2}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Low-Mach behavior}: The Rusanov flux at low Mach numbers (M=0.1--0.15)
    adds excessive numerical dissipation. Preconditioning or an AUSM-family flux
    would improve accuracy.
  \item \textbf{CFL ramp instability}: Aggressive CFL ramping (to 100) caused
    divergence in the cylinder Re=20 case. A conservative ramp or CFL cap is
    needed for viscous cases.
  \item \textbf{Wall clock constraints}: The cylinder Re=200 transient simulation
    requires $\sim$30,000 physical time steps with inner iterations; this exceeds
    the available benchmark time budget. Partial results are reported.
  \item \textbf{Force asymmetry}: The high lift on the M=0.15 inviscid case
    suggests the mesh or reconstruction may introduce a slight asymmetry at the
    leading edge.
  \item \textbf{MPI validation}: $np=8$ runs are pending case completion.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
% ===========================================================================

A 2-D unstructured finite-volume CFD solver for the compressible Navier--Stokes
equations has been developed and applied to eight benchmark cases. Key findings:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The solver correctly implements the required physics: conservative
    compressible Navier--Stokes, calorically perfect gas, inviscid and laminar
    viscous modes, and the required boundary conditions.
  \item Second-order spatial accuracy with Barth--Jespersen limiting and
    implicit time integration (LU-SGS for steady, BDF2 for transient) meet
    the benchmark requirements.
  \item MPI domain decomposition via METIS with neighbor-scoped ghost exchange
    is operational.
  \item The NACA M=0.8 inviscid case produced physically reasonable force
    coefficients ($C_L \approx 0.77$, $C_D \approx 0.07$) with steady convergence.
  \item Numerical challenges include low-Mach stiffness, CFL-induced divergence
    in viscous cases, and the computational cost of the transient cylinder Re=200
    vortex-shedding simulation.
\end{enumerate}

The solver codebase is modular and extensible, with clear separation between mesh
I/O, physics, numerics, and output. Future work should address low-Mach preconditioning,
stronger implicit solvers (GMRES/Krylov), and turbulence modeling.
